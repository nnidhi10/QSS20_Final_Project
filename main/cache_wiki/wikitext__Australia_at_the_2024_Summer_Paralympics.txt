[[Australia]] competed at the [[2024 Summer Paralympics]] in [[Paris]], France, from 28 August to 8 September 2024.

==Administration==
In June 2022, [[Paralympics Australia]] announced [[Kate McLoughlin]] as the Chef de Mission, her third Summer Paralympics in this role.<ref>{{Cite web |date=20 June 2022 |title=McLoughlin Ready For Two-Year Lead-In As Paris 2024 Chef De Mission |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2022/06/mcloughlin-ready-for-two-year-lead-in-as-paris-2024-chef-de-mission/ |access-date=20 June 2022 |website=Paralympics Australia}}</ref> Assistant  Chef de Missions: [[Bridie Kean]], Tim Mannion and Ben Troy.<ref>{{Cite web |title='Lived Experience' Key To Prominent Games Appointment {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2023/10/lived-experience-key-to-prominent-games-appointment/ |access-date=2023-10-22 |website=www.paralympic.org.au|date=18 October 2023 }}</ref> [[Curtis McGrath]] and [[Angie Ballard]] were appointed team captains.<ref>{{Cite web |date=28 August 2023 |title=Superb Athletes And Fierce Advocates: Australia's Paris 2024 Co-Captains Named |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2023/08/superb-athletes-and-fierce-advocates-australias-paris-2024-co-captains-named/ |access-date=28 August 2023 |website=Paralympics Australia}}</ref>

On 2 July 2024, Prime Minister [[Anthony Albanese]] and Opposition Leader [[Peter Dutton]] showed bipartisan support for the 2024 Australian Paralympic Team with the official team launch in the Great Hall of [[Parliament House, Canberra|Parliament House]] in Canberra.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-07-02 |title=Federal Parliament Officially Launches Australian Paralympic Team {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/07/federal-parliament-officially-launches-australian-paralympic-team/ |access-date=2024-07-02 |website=www.paralympic.org.au |language=en-AU}}</ref>

On 12 July 2024, wheelchair racer [[Madison de Rozario]] and swimmer [[Brenden Hall]] were announced as Opening Ceremony flag bearers at a ceremony at [[Admiralty House, Sydney|Admiralty House]] in Kirribilli, Sydney. Both will be five-time Paralympians.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-07-12 |title=Flag bearers named as superstar Paralympians get nod |url=https://www.nine.com.au/sport/paralympics/paris-2024-australia-flag-bearers-madison-de-rozario-brenden-hall-announcement-20240712-p5jt91.html |access-date=2024-07-12 |website=Nine |language=en}}</ref>
[[File:2024 Summer Paralympics opening ceremony - Australia 29.jpg|thumb|left|Madison de Rozario and Brenden Hall, Opening Ceremony flag bearers]]

The team's uniforms are designed and supplied by [[R. M. Williams (company)|R.M. Williams]], Belgravia,  [[Birkenstock]], [[Mizuno]] and [[Speedo]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-08 |title=Paralympic Team uniform launched at Australian fashion week |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/05/aussie-paralympic-team-uniform-launched-at-australian-fashion-week/ |access-date=2024-08-12 |website=Paralympics Australia |language=en}}</ref>

The [[Australian government]] announced that gold medallists would be awarded $20,000, silver medallists  $15,000 and bronze medallists $10,000. It brings Australian Paralympic medallists in line with Australian Olympic medallists who receive similar medal reward payments by the [[Australian Olympic Committee]].<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-26 |title=Federal Government Backs Australian Paralympians With Rewards For Medallists {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/08/federal-government-backs-australian-paralympians-with-rewards-for-medallists/ |access-date=2024-08-27 |website=www.paralympic.org.au |language=en-AU}}</ref>

The 159 strong team was finalised on 8 August 2024.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-08-08 |title=Record-Breakers, Change-Makers: Australian Paralympic Team Finalised For Paris 2024 |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/08/record-breakers-change-makers-australian-paralympic-team-finalised-for-paris-2024/ |access-date=2024-08-12 |website=Paralympics Australia |language=en}}</ref> Dual individual gold medallists - [[Lauren Parker]] (paratriathlon and cycling) and [[James Turner (parathlete)|James Turner]] (athletics) carried the flag at the Closing Ceremony.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-09-08 |title=The Best Of Australian Sport, Spirit And Character: Aussie Paralympians Bid Fond Farewell To Paris {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/09/the-best-of-australian-sport-spirit-and-character-aussie-paralympians-bid-fond-farewell-to-paris/ |access-date=2024-09-08 |website=www.paralympic.org.au |language=en-AU}}</ref>

==Archery==
{{main|Archery at the 2024 Summer Paralympics|Archery at the 2024 Summer Paralympics – Qualification}}
Australia secured seven quota places in all of the individual event, and two quotas in mixed team event, by virtue of their result at the 2023 World Para Archery Championships in [[Plzeň]], [[Czech Republic]]; and at the 2024 Africa–Oceania Qualification Tournament in [[Dubai]], United Arab Emirates.<ref>{{cite news|title=Olszewska serves two thrillers to grab Paris 2024 quota and reach final|url=https://www.worldarchery.sport/news/201292/olszewska-serves-two-thrillers-grab-paris-2024-quota-and-reach-final|publisher=[[World Archery]]|date=21 July 2023}}</ref> Six athletes were selected  by Paralympics Australia on 21 June 2024: Men - [[Jonathon Milne]], [[Taymon Kenton-Smith]], [[Patrick French (archer)|Patrick French]]; Women - [[AJ Jennings|Amanda Jennings]], [[Ameera Lee]], [[Melissa Tanner]]<ref>{{Cite web |date=2024-06-21 |title=Governor-General Hosts Paralympic Archery Team Announcement {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/06/governor-general-hosts-paralympic-archery-team-announcement/ |access-date=2024-06-24 |website=www.paralympic.org.au |language=en-AU}}</ref>  Reigning W1 men's World Champion [[Christopher Davis (archery)|Christopher Davis]] was forced to withdraw from the team due to injury.

== Facts ==
* Paralympics Games representation: Eight - [[Daniela Di Toro|Daniela di Toro]] (tennis / table tennis); Seven – [[Angie Ballard]] (athletics); Six - [[Lei Li Na]] (table tennis), [[Tristan Knowles]] (wheelchair basketball), [[Shaun Norris]] (wheelchair basketball), [[Ryley Batt]] (wheelchair rugby) [[Ben Weekes]] (wheelchair tennis){{sfn|Paralympics Australia|2024|p=40}}
* Sixty one athletes made their Paralympic Games debut.
* Indigenous athletes – [[Amanda Reid]] (cycling), [[Ruby Storm]] (swimming), [[Telaya Blacksmith]] (athletics) and [[Samantha Schmidt]] (athletics)<ref name="Finalised" /> 
* Youngest – [[Holly Warn]] (swimming) and oldest [[Jimmy Huo]] (table tennis player born in 1955)<ref name="Finalised" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=HUO Jimmy |url=https://www.paralympic.org/en/paris-2024-paralympics/athlete/jimmy-huo_2285194 |access-date=2024-08-29 |work=[[Paris 2024 Paralympics]] }} ([https://olympics.com/en/paris-2024-paralympics/athlete/jimmy-huo_2285194 alternate link], [https://paris2024.rtve.es/es/paris-2024-paralimpicos/atleta/jimmy-huo_2285194 alternate link 2])</ref>
* Dual Paralympian and Olympian – [[Melissa Tapper]] (table tennis)<ref name="Finalised" />
* Multiple Paralympic sports representation – [[Daniela Di Toro|Daniela di Toro]] (tennis / table tennis), [[Amanda Reid]] (swimming / cycling), [[AJ Jennings|A J Jennings]] (canoeing / archery), [[Jessica Gallagher]] (athletics / skiing / cycling), [[Ella Sabljak]] (wheelchair basketball / wheeelchair rugby). At the 2024 Games, [[Lauren Parker]] will be competing in triathlon and road cycling.<ref name="Finalised" /> 
*Families - brothers [[Chad Perris]] (athletics) and [[Kane Perris]] (track cycling); [[Taylor Gosens]] (judo) joins father [[Gerrard Gosens|Gerrad Gosens]] (goalball / athletics); [[Tom Goodman]]'s (triathlon) sister [[Molly Goodman]] competed at the Olympics.<ref name="Finalised" />
*Represented other Paralympic nations – [[Ma Lin (table tennis)|Ma Lin]], [[Lei Li Na]], [[Yang Qian (table tennis)|Yang Qian]] (All represented China in table tennis), [[Vanessa Low]] (represented Germany in athletics)<ref name="Finalised">{{Cite web |date=2024-08-08 |title=Record-Breakers, Change-Makers: Australian Paralympic Team Finalised For Paris 2024 {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/08/record-breakers-change-makers-australian-paralympic-team-finalised-for-paris-2024/ |access-date=2024-08-12 |website=www.paralympic.org.au }}</ref>
*Seventeen Australian team athletes won multiple medals.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |date=2024-09-08 |title=The Best Of Australian Sport, Spirit And Character: Aussie Paralympians Bid Fond Farewell To Paris {{!}} Paralympics Australia |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2024/09/the-best-of-australian-sport-spirit-and-character-aussie-paralympians-bid-fond-farewell-to-paris/ |access-date=2024-09-30 |website=www.paralympic.org.au |language=en-AU}}</ref>
*[[Lauren Parker]] won a gold medal in two distinct sports - triathlon and cycling.
*Six dual gold medallists: [[Alexa Leary]] (swimming), [[Callum Simpson]] (swimming), [[Lauren Parker]] (triathlon/cycling) and [[Timothy Hodge|Tim Hodge]] (swimming) each won two gold medals and a silver medal; [[Yang Qian (table tennis)|Qian Yang]] (table tennis), [[James Turner (parathlete)|James Turner]] (athletics) each won two gold medals.<ref name=":0" />  
*Several athletes repeated their event performance from Tokyo Paralympics[[James Turner (parathlete)|: James Turner]] (athletics 400 m T36),  [[Vanessa Low]] (athletics long jump T63),   [[Curtis McGrath]] (canoeing Kl2), [[Amanda Reid]] (track cycling time trial C1–3  ), [[Emily Petricola]] (track cycling  C4 3000m individual pursuit), [[Benjamin Hance]] (swimming backstroke S14)  and  [[Yang Qian (table tennis)|Qian Yang]] (table tennis singles 10). James Turner, Vanessa Low and Curtis McGrath went on to win gold medals at three successive Paralympic Games.
*Australia won medals in nine sports: boccia, athletics, canoe, cycling, rowing, swimming, table tennis, triathlon and wheelchair rugby.<ref name=":0" />
