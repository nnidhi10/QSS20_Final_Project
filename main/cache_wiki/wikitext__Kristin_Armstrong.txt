'''Kristin Armstrong Savola''' ({{nee|'''Armstrong'''}}; born August 11, 1973) is a former professional [[road bicycle racer]]<ref name=vn>{{cite web |url=http://www.velonews.com/2017/12/news/qa-kristin-armstrong-helps-team-usa-toward-gold-tokyo-2020_453605 |title=Q&A: Kristin Armstrong helps Team USA toward gold in Tokyo 2020 |last=Case |first=Chris |date=December 19, 2017 |website=[[VeloNews]] |access-date=March 3, 2018}}</ref> and three-time [[Summer Olympic Games|Olympic]] gold medalist, the winner of the women's [[individual time trial]] in [[Cycling at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|2008]], [[Cycling at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|2012]], and [[Cycling at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|2016]]. Before temporarily retiring to start a family in 2009, she rode for {{UCI team code|CWT|2009}} in women's elite professional events on the National Racing Calendar (NRC) and [[World Cup of Road Cycling (women)|UCI Women's World Cup]]. She announced a return to competitive cycling beginning in the 2011 season, competing for [[Peanut Butter & Co.]] TWENTY12 at the Redlands Classic.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.theroaddiaries.com/2011/04/07/a-day-in-images-with-kristin-armstrong-champion-mentor-mom/ |publisher=The Road Diaries |last=Zellmann |first=Michael |title=A day in images with Kristin Armstrong – champion, mentor, mom |date=April 27, 2011 |access-date=August 15, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120723152120/http://www.theroaddiaries.com/2011/04/07/a-day-in-images-with-kristin-armstrong-champion-mentor-mom/ |archive-date=July 23, 2012 |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Background==
Prior to her professional cycling career, Armstrong had been a junior Olympian in swimming, a distance runner in college, and then a [[triathlon|triathlete]].<ref name="family" /><ref name=envis09>{{cite web |url=http://www.uidaho.edu/~/media/Files/orgs/ED/News%20and%20Events/Envision-Winter_09.ashx |publisher=University of Idaho College of Education, Envision |title=After the gold: Kristin Armstrong's life before |date=Winter 2009 |access-date=August 15, 2012 |page=3 |archive-date=November 6, 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191106164423/https://www.uidaho.edu/~/media/Files/orgs/ED/News%20and%20Events/Envision-Winter_09.ashx |url-status=dead }}</ref> She spent many hours perfecting her strokes in the pool at the Boise Family [[YMCA]], where she also served as Director of Aquatics, managing more than 50 lifeguards, swim instructors, and others.  She was diagnosed with [[osteoarthritis]] in both hips in 2001 at age 27, and told that she could no longer run at an elite level, thus ending her triathlon career and leading her to focus exclusively on cycling.<ref name="family">{{cite news |url=http://www.elpasotimes.com/ci_17937090 |title=Tour of the Gila: Armstrong's comeback includes family time |last=Bloomquist |first=Bret |date=April 4, 2011 |newspaper=[[El Paso Times]] |access-date=May 19, 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://archive.today/20130122012140/http://www.elpasotimes.com/ci_17937090 |archive-date=January 22, 2013 }}</ref><ref name=usat09>{{cite news |url=https://www.usatoday.com/sports/cycling/2009-10-30-kristin-armstrong_N.htm |newspaper=USA Today |agency=Associated Press |title=Retired Kristin Armstrong ponders future on and off the bike |date=October 30, 2009 |access-date=August 15, 2012}}</ref>

The three-time national champion finished 8th (top U.S. finisher) in the [[Cycling at the 2004 Summer Olympics – Women's road race|women's road race]] at the [[United States at the 2004 Summer Olympics|2004 Summer Olympics]] in Athens.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://legacy.usacycling.org/kristin-armstrong-savola.htm|title=Kristin Armstrong Savola - USA Cycling|website=USAcycling.org|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200122031258/https://legacy.usacycling.org/kristin-armstrong-savola.htm|archive-date=January 22, 2020|access-date=January 21, 2020}}</ref>

At the [[2008 Summer Olympics]] in Beijing, Armstrong completed the biggest achievement of her career by winning the gold medal in the [[Cycling at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|women's road time trial]] competition on August 13.  Finishing in under 35 minutes, Armstrong was 25 seconds ahead of silver medalist [[Emma Pooley]] from [[Great Britain]], with [[Karin Thürig]] from [[Switzerland]] taking the bronze.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.olympic.org/beijing-2008/cycling-road/individual-time-trial-women|title=Beijing 2008 individual time trial women - Olympic Beijing 2008 Cycling Road|website=olympic.org|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200122031820/https://www.olympic.org/beijing-2008/cycling-road/individual-time-trial-women|archive-date=January 22, 2020|access-date=January 21, 2020}}</ref>

Of Armstrong, Velonews wrote: "Kristin Armstrong is famous for both her success in bicycle racing, and the 'type-A' attention to detail that keeps her climbing the podium at critical races every season."<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.velonews.com/article/90511/born-for-beijing-the-k-edge-chain-catcher-goes-into |publisher=Velonews |last=Vestal |first=Zack |title=Born for Beijing, the K-Edge chain catcher goes into production |date=April 14, 2009 |access-date=August 15, 2012 |archive-date=June 4, 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090604153503/http://www.velonews.com/article/90511/born-for-beijing-the-k-edge-chain-catcher-goes-into |url-status=dead }}</ref>

Armstrong announced at the end of 2010 that she would return to competitive cycling, with the goal of competing at the [[London]] [[2012 Summer Olympics]]. In a written statement, she stated that her retirement was merely temporary in order to start a family:

<blockquote>I love cycling and I love competing.  I stopped racing after the 2009 World Championships not because I was burned out, but because my husband and I wanted to start a family...I told myself from the beginning if everything went smoothly with the birth of our son, Lucas William, I would consider racing again.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.ktvb.com/home/Olympic-gold-medalist-Kristin-Armstrong-will-race-again-105563968.html |publisher=KTVB |title=Olympic gold medalist Kristin Armstrong will race again |date=October 22, 2010 |access-date=August 15, 2012 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120315224325/http://www.ktvb.com/home/Olympic-gold-medalist-Kristin-Armstrong-will-race-again-105563968.html |archive-date=March 15, 2012 }}</ref></blockquote>

In 2011, Armstrong returned to compete with the Peanut Butter & Co. Team TWENTY12, of which she was a part owner. She won three of the four stages (criterium, road race, and time trial) and the overall title during the [[Sea Otter Classic]] in April.<ref name="family" />  However, during the first stage of the [[Tour of the Gila]], which she had won twice previously, Armstrong came down with food poisoning.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=http://velonews.competitor.com/2011/04/news/abbott-mancebo-take-mountain-top-wins-at-sram-tour-of-the-gila_170211 |title=Abbott, Mancebo, take mountain-top wins at SRAM Tour of the Gila |author=Steve Frothingham |magazine=VeloNews |date=April 27, 2011 |access-date=May 19, 2011 |archive-date=April 22, 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160422042627/http://velonews.competitor.com/2011/04/news/abbott-mancebo-take-mountain-top-wins-at-sram-tour-of-the-gila_170211 |url-status=dead }}</ref> She ended up withdrawing from the race and returning home to recover.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.dailypeloton.com/displayarticle.asp?pk=18603 |title=Taylor Wiles takes third place at Giro |publisher=DailyPeloton.com |date=April 29, 2011 |access-date=May 19, 2011 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120322062931/http://www.dailypeloton.com/displayarticle.asp?pk=18603 |archive-date=March 22, 2012 }}</ref>

Armstrong successfully defended her Olympic title in the [[Cycling at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|individual time trial]] at the 2012 Olympics in London and became the oldest rider to win an Olympic time trial, and finished 35th in the [[Cycling at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's road race|Women's road race]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.nbcolympics.com/news-blogs/cycling/armstrong-wins-the-time-trial.html |title=Armstrong becomes oldest rider to win time trial |publisher=NBC Olympics |date=August 1, 2012 |access-date=August 2, 2012 |archive-date=February 21, 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180221040548/http://www.nbcolympics.com/news-blogs/cycling/armstrong-wins-the-time-trial.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>  She announced her retirement after the 2012 Olympics.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cyclingnews.com/news/armstrong-goes-out-on-top-with-olympic-gold |title=Armstrong goes out on top with Olympic gold |publisher=CyclingNews |date=August 1, 2012}}</ref>

In September 2012, Armstrong's Olympic gold medal-winning time trial bike was stolen while in transit between Germany and the USA.<ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.nzherald.co.nz/sport/news/article.cfm?c_id=4&objectid=10833771 |title=Cycling: Gold medalist's bikes stolen |newspaper=New Zealand Herald |date=September 13, 2012 |access-date=September 13, 2012}}</ref>

In April 2015, Armstrong announced that she would be coming out of retirement to race at the 2015 [[Pan American Road and Track Championships|Pan American Road Championships]] in [[Leon, Mexico]], having been selected by USA Cycling for the individual time trial.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cyclingnews.com/news/womens-news-shorts-armstrong-comes-out-of-retirement-for-pan-am-championships |title=Women's news shorts: Armstrong comes out of retirement for Pan Am Championships |last1=Frattini |first1=Kirsten |date=April 17, 2015 |website=[[cyclingnews.com]] | access-date=April 17, 2015}}</ref> Two days after announcing Armstrong's selection, however, the USA Cycling Selection Committee reconvened, deciding that the new selection process under which Armstrong had been chosen had not been published in a timely manner, and that as a result the federation's older Principles of Athlete Selection, which had been issued in 2008, should be used instead for selection to the Pan American Championships. The upshot was that Armstrong's place was taken by [[Tayler Wiles]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cyclingnews.com/news/usa-cycling-replaces-armstrong-with-wiles-for-2015-pan-am-championships |title=USA Cycling replaces Armstrong with Wiles for 2015 Pan Am Championships |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.--> |date=April 18, 2015 |website=[[cyclingnews.com]] | access-date=April 18, 2015}}</ref> The following month, Armstrong won the [[United States National Time Trial Championships]] in [[Chattanooga, Tennessee]], beating [[Carmen Small]] by 13 seconds.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.cyclingnews.com/races/usa-cycling-professional-road-championships-2016/womens-time-trial/results |title=Kristin Armsthong wins women's US pro time trial |author=<!--Staff writer(s); no by-line.--> |date=May 23, 2015 |website=[[cyclingnews.com]] | access-date=May 23, 2015}}</ref>

In August 2016, Armstrong made history at the [[2016 Summer Olympics]] in [[Rio de Janeiro]] after coming out of retirement to win the gold medal in the [[Cycling at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's road time trial|women's individual time trial]] and becoming the first rider ever to win three gold medals in the same discipline. She also became the oldest female cyclist to win an Olympic medal.<ref>{{Cite news |last=McMahon  |first=Daniel  |date=August 10, 2016 |title=Out of retirement and into gold, 42-year-old American Kristin Armstrong just made Olympic history |language=en |work=Business Insider |url=http://www.businessinsider.com/kristin-armstrong-wins-third-straight-olympic-gold-medal-2016-8 |access-date=May 31, 2017}}</ref>

In September 2017 Armstrong joined [[USA Cycling]] as Endurance Performance Director.<ref name=vn />

In December 2019 Armstrong created and launched her bike computer mount line of products called [https://kx3sports.com KX3 Sports].
