{{Canoeing at the 2020 Summer Olympics}}

The women's C-1 200 metres sprint canoeing event at the [[2020 Summer Olympics]] took place on 4 and 5 August 2021 at the [[Sea Forest Waterway]].<ref name="Dates">{{cite web |url=https://olympics.com/tokyo-2020/olympic-games/en/results/canoe-sprint/event-schedule-women-s-canoe-single-200m.htm |title=Canoe Sprint - Women's Canoe Single 200m Schedule |publisher=Tokyo 2020 |access-date=3 August 2021 |archive-date=3 August 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210803203929/https://olympics.com/tokyo-2020/olympic-games/en/results/canoe-sprint/event-schedule-women-s-canoe-single-200m.htm |url-status=dead }}</ref> At least 12 canoeists from at least 12 nations competed.<ref name=Qualifying>{{cite news |url=https://www.canoeicf.com/sites/default/files/fin2021_covid_final_-_tokyo_2020_-_revised_qualification_system_-_canoe_sprint_-_eng.pdf |title=Qualification System – Games of the XXXI Olympiad – ''Canoe Sprint''|publisher=[[International Canoe Federation]]|access-date=25 June 2021}}</ref>
{{TOC limit|3}}

==Background==
This was the debut appearance of the event, replacing the men's C-1 200 metres as the Olympics moved towards gender equality.<ref name=ProgrammeChange>{{cite news |url=https://www.canoeicf.com/news/ioc-announcement-confirms-changes-canoeing-program-tokyo |title=The IOC's final approval of the event programme for the 2020 Olympics confirms changes to the canoe sprint and slalom rundown for Tokyo. |publisher=International Canoe Federation |date=9 June 2017 |access-date=24 June 2021}}</ref> The women's C-1 200 metres has been an event at the [[ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships]] in 2010.<ref name=Harrison>{{cite news |url=https://www.teamusa.com/news/2021/march/20/sprint-canoer-nevin-harrison-qualifies-for-tokyo-at-the-aca-sprint-olympic-national-team-trials |publisher=USOPC |author=Lisa Costantini |title=Sprint Canoer Nevin Harrison Qualifies for Tokyo at the ACA Sprint Olympics & National Team Trials |date=20 March 2021 |access-date=25 June 2021}}</ref>

[[2019 ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships|2019 World Champion]] teenager [[Nevin Harrison]] of the United States qualified for the event.<ref name=Harrison/>

==Qualification==
{{main|Canoeing at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}

A [[National Olympic Committee]] (NOC) could only qualify one boat (and thus earn one women's canoe quota place) in the event; however, NOCs could enter up to 2 boats in the event if they had enough women's canoe quota places from other events (that is, the C-2). A total of 12 qualification places were available, initially allocated as follows:

* 1 place for the host nation, Japan, if it did not qualify any women's canoe quota places
* 5 places awarded through the [[2019 ICF Canoe Sprint World Championships]]
* 5 places awarded through continental tournaments, 1 per continent
* 1 place awarded through the 2021 [[Canoe Sprint World Cup]] Stage 2.

Qualifying places were awarded to the NOC, not to the individual canoeist who earned the place.<ref name=Qualifying/>

The Oceania spot was re-allocated to the World Championships, going to China. As the Americas continental tournament was cancelled, that place was also allocated through the World Championships, but the place ultimately went to Bulgaria because insufficient athletes had entered from the Americas.<ref name=Americas>{{cite news |url=https://www.canoeicf.com/news/mexico-make-olympic-slalom-debut-after-quota-allocations |publisher=ICF |title=Mexico to make Olympic slalom debut after quota allocations |date=30 April 2021 |access-date=25 June 2021}}</ref>

As Japan qualified a boat in the [[Canoeing at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Women's C-2 500 metres|women's C-2 event]],  its host nation place was also reallocated through the World Championships. ==Competition format==
Sprint canoeing uses a four-round format for events with at least 11 boats, with heats, quarterfinals, semifinals, and finals. The details for each round depend on how many boats ultimately enter.<ref name=Progression>{{cite web |url=https://www.canoeicf.com/sites/default/files/tokyo_2020_canoe_sprint_progression_system.pdf |publisher=ICF |title=Canoe Sprint Progression System |access-date=25 June 2021}}</ref>

The course is a flatwater course 9 metres wide. The name of the event describes the particular format within sprint canoeing. The "C" format means a canoe, with the canoeist kneeling and using a single-bladed paddle to paddle and steer (as opposed to a kayak, with a seated canoeist, double-bladed paddle, and foot-operated rudder). The "1" is the number of canoeists in each boat. The "200 metres" is the distance of each race.<ref name=Tokyo2020Summary>{{cite web |url=https://olympics.com/tokyo-2020/en/sports/canoe-sprint/ |title=Canoe Sprint |publisher=IOC |access-date=25 June 2021 |archive-date=3 July 2021 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210703154152/https://olympics.com/tokyo-2020/en/sports/canoe-sprint/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

==Schedule==
The event was held over two consecutive days, with two rounds per day. All sessions started at 9:30&nbsp;a.m. local time, though there are multiple events with races in each session.<ref>{{cite web |title=Canoe Sprint Competition Schedule |url=https://tokyo2020.org/en/schedule/canoe-sprint-schedule |publisher=[[2020 Summer Olympics|Tokyo 2020]] |access-date=1 March 2020}}</ref>
