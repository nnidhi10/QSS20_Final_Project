[[Australia]] competed at the [[2016 Summer Paralympics]] in [[Rio de Janeiro]], Brazil, from 7 to 18 September 2016. Australia repeated its [[2012 Summer Paralympics]] achievement in finishing fifth of the medal tally.

Notable achievements at the Games:

* [[Jessica Gallagher]] became the first Australian Paralympian to win medals at both the [[Summer Paralympics]] and [[Winter Paralympics]] by winning a bronze medal in cycling.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Lulham|first1=Amanda|title=Jessica Gallagher creates Australian Paralympic history as first Summer and Winter medallist|url=http://www.dailytelegraph.com.au/sport/swoop/jessica-gallagher-creates-australian-paralympic-history-as-first-summer-and-winter-medallist/news-story/a072d403cb1d641b94d48f05f890152a|access-date=21 September 2016|work=Daily Telegraph|date=12 September 2016}}</ref>
* [[Kurt Fearnley]] finished Paralympic his career with 13 medals including a silver and bronze in Rio. His silver medal in the Men's Marathon T52-54 meant he had won medals in this event at four successive Paralympics (2004-2016).<ref>{{cite news|last1=Spits|first1=Scott|title=Rio Paralympics 2016: Kurt Fearnley's agonising near miss for wheelchair marathon gold|url=https://www.smh.com.au/sport/rio-paralympics-2016-kurt-fearnleys-agonising-near-miss-for-wheelchair-marathon-gold-20160918-grj5ac.html|access-date=20 September 2016|work=Sydney Morning Herald|date=19 September 2016}}</ref>
*[[Ellie Cole]] joined the list of [[Australia at the Paralympics#Leading Australian Summer Paralympians 1960–2016|leading Australian Paralympic medallists]] by winning six medals - 2 gold, 3 silver and 1 bronze. Her medal tally at the end of Rio was 6 gold, 4 silver and bronze.
*[[Kieran Modra]] won a bronze medal in cycling and this meant that he had medalled at six Paralympics.<ref>{{cite news|title=Another medal for our Paralympic pro|url=http://www.portlincolntimes.com.au/story/4175016/another-medal-for-our-paralympic-pro/|access-date=21 September 2016|work=Port Lincoln Times|date=19 September 2016}}</ref>
*[[Samuel Von Einem]] won Australia first medal in table tennis since [[Terry Biggs]] won gold in 1984.<ref name=wrap/>
*[[Jonathon Milne]] won Australia's first medal in archery since 1968.<ref name="wrap">{{cite web|last1=Blood|first1=Greg|title=That's a wrap: Australia at the Rio Paralympics|url=http://www.theroar.com.au/2016/09/20/thats-wrap-australia-rio-paralympics/|website=The Roar|access-date=20 September 2016}}</ref>
*[[Curtis McGrath]] and [[Katie Kelly (paratriathlete)|Katie Kelly]] became Australia's first gold medallists in paracanoe and paratriathlon respectively.<ref name=wrap/>
* [[Dylan Alcott]] became only the fifth Australian Paralympian to win gold medals in two sports by winning two gold medals in wheelchair tennis. He previously won gold in wheelchair basketball.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Blood|first1=Greg|title=Dylan Alcott: Rare Australian record of gold in two sports|url=http://www.theroar.com.au/2016/09/16/dylan-alcott-rare-australian-record-gold-two-sports/|website=The Roar|access-date=20 September 2016}}</ref>
*Athletes and teams that won their event again in Rio were: [[Ellie Cole]] (swimming), [[Brenden Hall]] (swimming), [[David Nicholas]] (cycling), [[Carol Cooke]] (cycling), [[Daniel Fitzgibbon]]/[[Liesl Tesch]] (sailing) and the [[Australia national wheelchair rugby team|wheelchair rugby team]].

==Fundraising==
The APC stated that it needed to raise  $7 million to fund its Rio 2016 campaign. The majority of APC's funding to send Australian teams to major events originates from fundraising. In January 2016, it launched the Australian Paralympic Foundation to manage its fundraising. [[Cadbury]] made the first major donation of $1 million.<ref>{{cite web|title=1million donation from CADBURY kicks off launch of Australian Paralympic Foundation |url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/1million-donation-from-cadbury-kicks-off-launch-of-australian-paralympic-foundation/ |website=Australian Paralympic Committee News |date=20 January 2016 |access-date=20 January 2016 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160311001412/https://www.paralympic.org.au/1million-donation-from-cadbury-kicks-off-launch-of-australian-paralympic-foundation/ |archive-date=11 March 2016 }}</ref> In the four-year period leading up to Rio, the [[Australian Sports Commission]] (ASC) provided $62 million in funding.<ref name="hpost">{{cite news|last1=Koerber|first1=Rebecca|title=Paralympians Need To Raise Another $2 Million To Get To Rio|url=http://www.huffingtonpost.com.au/2016/03/02/paralympics-athletes-rio_n_9371086.html|access-date=6 March 2016|work=Huffington Post|date=4 March 2016}}</ref> At the Team Launch, [[Prime Minister of Australia]] [[Malcolm Turnbull]] stated that the Australian Government had provided nearly $65 million direct funding for the athletes as they prepared to compete in Brazil.<ref>{{cite web|title=Paralympic Team Lunch, Sydney Olympic Park|url=https://www.malcolmturnbull.com.au/media/paralympic-team-lunch-sydney-olympic-park|website=Malcolm Turnbull MP website|access-date=21 June 2016}}</ref> To assist with funding raising, its broadcast partner [[Seven Network]] organized a Parathon on 5 March 2016.<ref name=hpost/>

Australia was given additional qualification slots in the sports of goalball (six athletes), athletics (two athletes) and wheelchair tennis (one athlete) after Russia was suspended from the Games by the [[International Paralympic Committee]].<ref name=russia>{{cite web|title=2016 Australian Paralympic Team receives nine extra spots|url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/2016-australian-paralympic-team-receives-nine-extra-spots/|website=Australian Paralympic Committee News, 29 August 2016|date=28 August 2016 |access-date=29 August 2016}}</ref> Two athletes from the original team of 178 did not attend - [[Emily Tapp]] due to injury and [[Michael Gallagher (cyclist)|Michael Gallager]] due to doping violation.<ref name=burn/>

Several team members have changed their previous Paralympic sports at these Games: [[Dylan Alcott]] (wheelchair tennis), [[Jessica Gallagher]] (cycling), [[Daniela Di Toro]] (table tennis) and [[Claire McLean]] (paratriathlon).

==Cycling==
{{main|Cycling at the 2016 Summer Paralympics}}
[[File:Gallagher and Janssen on the blocks.jpg|thumb|right|Australian cyclists [[Madison Janssen]] (right) and [[Jessica Gallagher]] prepare to race in the Women’s B/VI 1000m time trial final, in which they won bronze. This made Gallagher the first Australian to win medals at both the summer and winter Paralympics|alt=two women on a tandem bicycle]]
The cycling team was announced on 30 May 2016. The team consisted of 13 athletes and three sighted pilots:<ref name="cycling">{{cite web|url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/australian-paralympic-cycling-team-announced/ |title=Australian Paralympic Cycling Team announced |publisher=Australian Paralympic Committee |access-date=31 May 2016 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160810162020/https://www.paralympic.org.au/australian-paralympic-cycling-team-announced/ |archive-date=10 August 2016 }}</ref> For Modra, this would be his eighth Paralympic Games.<ref name="cycling" /> [[Michael Gallagher (cyclist)|Michael Gallagher]] was originally selected but on 2 September 2016 he was withdrawn from the team due to a positive doping test sample.<ref>{{cite news|title=Paralympics: Doping ban rules Michael Gallagher out of Rio|url=https://www.smh.com.au/sport/paralympics-doping-ban-rules-michael-gallagher-out-of-rio-20160902-gr7ts1.html|access-date=3 September 2016|work=Sydney Morning Herald|date=2 September 2016}}</ref>

===Men's tournament===
{{main|Wheelchair basketball at the 2016 Summer Paralympics – Men}}
The Rollers qualified by winning the 2015 Asia Oceania Qualifying Tournament.<ref name=gliders2015>{{cite web|title=Heartbreak and Elation for Gliders and Rollers at AOZ qualifying tournament|url=http://www.basketball.net.au/heartbreak-and-elation-for-gliders-and-rollers-at-aoz-qualifying-tournament/|website=Basketball Australia|access-date=20 October 2015}}</ref> On 19 July 2016, the APC announced a team of twelve players with five of them making their Paralympic debut.<ref>{{cite web|title=Australian Rollers ready for Rio 2016 revenge|url=https://www.paralympic.org.au/australian-rollers-ready-for-rio-2016-revenge/|website=Australian Paralympic Committee News, 19 July 2016|date=19 July 2016 |access-date=19 July 2016}}</ref> During the draw, Brazil had the choice of which group they wanted to be in.  They were partnered with Spain, who would be in the group Brazil did not select.  Brazil chose Group B, which included Iran, the United States, Great Britain, Germany and Algeria.  That left Spain in Group A with Australia, Canada, Turkey, the Netherlands and Japan.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.rio2016.com/en/news/groups-drawn-for-wheelchair-basketball-at-rio-2016-paralympic-games |title=Groups drawn for wheelchair basketball at Rio 2016 Paralympic Games |website=Rio 2016 |access-date=2016-08-25 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160826075720/https://www.rio2016.com/en/news/groups-drawn-for-wheelchair-basketball-at-rio-2016-paralympic-games |archive-date=2016-08-26 }}</ref>

==Broadcasting==
The Australian Paralympic Committee sold the rights to the [[Seven Network]].<ref name="adnews">{{cite journal|last1=Hickman |first1=y Arvind|title=Paralympics move to commercial TV a win for Seven and sponsors |journal=AdNews|date=29 September 2016|url=http://www.adnews.com.au/news/paralympics-move-to-commercial-tv-a-win-for-seven-and-sponsors|access-date=30 September 2016}}</ref> Previously the [[Australian Broadcasting Corporation]] broadcast the Games.<ref name=adnews/> There were 20 per cent more hours broadcast than the London Paralympics.

Major advertisers and sponsors were: [[Optus]], [[Samsung]], [[Visa Inc]], [[Swisse]], [[Woolworths Supermarkets]] and [[Toyota]].<ref name=adnews/>
