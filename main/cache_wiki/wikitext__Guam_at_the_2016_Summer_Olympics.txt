[[Guam]] competed at the [[2016 Summer Olympics]] in [[Rio de Janeiro]], Brazil, from August 5 to 21, 2016. This was the territory's eighth consecutive appearance at the Summer Olympics.

Guam National Olympic Committee sent the territory's smallest delegation to the Games since [[2004 Summer Olympics|2004]]. Five athletes, three men and two women, were selected to the Guamanian team to compete only in athletics, mountain biking, and swimming.<ref>{{cite news|first=Jerick|last=Sablan|title=Guam's athletes for the Olympics|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2016/07/23/guams-athletes-olympics/87329658/|publisher=[[Pacific Daily News]]|date=July 23, 2016|access-date=September 14, 2016}}</ref> Three of them made their Olympic debut in Rio de Janeiro, with swimmers [[Pilar Shimizu]] and [[Benjamin Schulte]], who led the squad as Guam's flag bearer in the opening ceremony, returning for their second appearance from London 2012.<ref name=flag-bearer/>

None of the athletes made it past the first heat or won a medal. Guam has yet to win its first ever Olympic medal.

==Background==
Guam was officially recognized on November 25, 1987, which they had worked towards for six years. Now officially recognized, Guam was able to send athletes to train in Australia and the United States, and had funding to improve its sports programs. Guam's first Olympic appearance was at the [[1988 Winter Olympics]] where [[Judd Bankert]] became Guam's first Olympic athlete competing in the biathlon.  Guam's first summer appearance was later that same year in [[Seoul, South Korea]]. Guam sent its largest delegation to the 1992 Summer Olympics, with 22 athletes.<ref name=gpdns>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2016/07/09/guams-history-olympics/86791100/|title=Guam's history at the Olympics|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick|publisher=Pacific Daily News|access-date=January 20, 2018|date=July 9, 2016}}</ref>

Starting with the 1996 Olympic Games in Atlanta, Guam began sending fewer athletes. The NOC's sponsors were concerned about the cost of sending large delegations and wanted to send athletes who were more likely to be competitive. The IOC also asked countries to reduce the size of their delegations, since there were so many countries competing in those Olympics.<ref name=gpdns />

==Athletics==
{{main|Athletics at the 2016 Summer Olympics|Athletics at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}
Guam received universality slots from [[IAAF]] to send two athletes (one male and one female) to the Olympics.<ref>{{cite web|title=iaaf.org – Top Lists|url=http://www.iaaf.org/statistics/toplists/index.html|publisher=IAAF|access-date=April 18, 2015|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110604220856/http://www.iaaf.org/statistics/toplists/index.html|archive-date=June 4, 2011|df=mdy-all}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=IAAF Games of the XXX Olympiad – Rio 2016 Entry Standards|url=http://www.insidethegames.biz/media/file/559/IAAF%20Rio%202016%20standards.pdf|publisher=[[IAAF]]|access-date=April 18, 2015|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418232819/http://www.insidethegames.biz/media/file/559/IAAF%20Rio%202016%20standards.pdf|archive-date=April 18, 2015|df=mdy-all}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|title=GNOC announces Team Guam delegation to Rio2016 Olympic Games|url=http://www.foxsportspulse.com/assoc_page.cgi?client=2-3846-0-0-0&sID=334655&&news_task=DETAIL&articleID=44865920|publisher=Guam National Olympic Committee|access-date=July 14, 2016|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160807145238/http://www.foxsportspulse.com/assoc_page.cgi?client=2-3846-0-0-0&sID=334655&&news_task=DETAIL&articleID=44865920|archive-date=August 7, 2016|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

[[Joshua Ilustre]] competed in the 800&nbsp;m race, finishing with a time of 1:58.85. He found out an hour after the race that he was disqualified due to a lane violation. Ilustre appealed the ruling, but after twelve hours learned it was unsuccessful.<ref name=pdn>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/news/2016/12/30/no-7-team-guam-shines-stumbles-rio-olympics/95991670/|title=No. 7: Team Guam shines, stumbles at Rio Olympics|publisher=Pacific Daily News|access-date=February 18, 2018|date=December 31, 2016}}</ref> He thought the video evidence was unclear on whether he stepped on the line. The time would have been a personal best for Ilustre. He said, "I wish things would have turned out better. It is what it is. I can't do much about it, but make it into a learning experience".<ref name=dsq>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2016/08/18/ilustre-disqualification-like-slap-face/88900148/|title=Ilustre: Disqualification like 'slap in the face'|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick|publisher=Pacific Daily News|access-date=February 18, 2018|date=August 18, 2016}}</ref>

[[Regine Tugade]] ran the 100&nbsp;m sprint. She finished third in her heat with a time of 12.52 seconds, just short of her national record time of 12.26 seconds.<ref name=pdn /> She placed third in her heat, failing to advance to the next round. She wore a red hair tie, a tradition she has kept since high school, which along with her red spikes match the color of Guam's flag. About her performance, she said "I didn't run my personal best, but I honestly felt like I ran my hardest and I felt like I performed well. It may not show time-wise, but my body and my mentality feels like I pushed it to my limit".<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/olympics/rio-2016/2016/08/12/tugade-finishes-3rd-heat-rio-olympics/88639176/|publisher=USA Today|access-date=February 18, 2018|date=August 13, 2016|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick|title=Tugade finishes 3rd in heat at Rio Olympics|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180219151221/https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/olympics/rio-2016/2016/08/12/tugade-finishes-3rd-heat-rio-olympics/88639176/|archive-date=February 19, 2018|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

==Cycling==
{{main|Cycling at the 2016 Summer Olympics|Cycling at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}

===Mountain biking===
Guam qualified one mountain biker for the men's Olympic cross-country race, as Oceania's sole representative outside the world's top twenty-five nations in the UCI Olympic Ranking List of May 25, 2016. [[Peter Lombard (cyclist)|Peter Lombard]] was the only Guamanian athlete to qualify for the 2016 Olympics on merit.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2017/08/19/2016-rio-de-janeiro-olympics-cyclist-peter-lombard-wants-compete-again-but-first-needs-heal/582169001/|title=Rio de Janeiro Olympics cyclist Peter Lombard wants to compete again, but needs to heal|last1=Tomas|first1=Jojo|access-date=February 18, 2018|publisher=Pacific Daily News|date=August 19, 2017}}</ref> Lombard is an eye surgeon, and rearranged his work schedule to allow for more training. He also took a week off of work to train on mountains in Japan.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.postguam.com/sports/local/olympic-profile-cyclist-peter-lombard/article_5ca2f60e-53cf-11e6-a351-5f4c76cdeca0.html|title=Olympic profile: Cyclist Peter Lombard|last1=Balajadia|first1=Robert|publisher=The Guam Daily Post|date=July 28, 2016|access-date=February 25, 2018}}</ref>

Lombard was one of five athletes who did not finish the race. He had issues with his bike pedal that made it hard to clip in. At one point, the course was so slippery from rain riders had to dismount. The faulty clip made it difficult for Lombard to re-mount his bike. He crashed on both the first and second lap before being pulled from the race after the second crash.<ref name=cycling>{{cite web|url=https://www.rio2016.com/en/cycling-mountain-bike-mens-cross-country|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160821181604/https://www.rio2016.com/en/cycling-mountain-bike-mens-cross-country|title=Men's Cross Country|publisher=Rio 2016|access-date=February 18, 2018|archive-date=August 21, 2016|url-status=dead}}</ref> Once the motorcycle pulled him from the race, he was at the top of a mountain and unsure where to go. He decided to cheer on the cyclists still in the race, which drew attention and was well received by the crowd. About his Olympic experience, he said, "I'm happy to be done, happy to be in one piece".<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2016/08/21/battered-and-bruised-but-lombard-comes-out-smiling-after-olympics-race/89077078/|title=Olympic mountain bike course gets best of Lombard|publisher=Pacific Daily News|date=August 22, 2016|access-date=February 18, 2018|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick}}</ref>

==Swimming==
{{main|Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics}}
Guam received a universality invitation from [[FINA]] to send two swimmers (one male and one female) to the Olympics.<ref>{{cite web|title=Swimming World Rankings |url=http://www.fina.org/H2O/index.php?option=com_wrapper&view=wrapper&Itemid=805 |publisher=[[FINA]] |access-date=March 14, 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20110615135327/http://www.fina.org/H2O/index.php?option=com_wrapper&view=wrapper&Itemid=805 |archive-date=June 15, 2011 |df=dmy-all }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Rio 2016 – FINA Swimming Qualification System |url=http://www.fina.org/H2O/docs/events/rio2016/2014-07%20-%20Rio%202016%20-%20Qualification%20System%20-%20FINAL%20-%20Swimming%20-%20EN.pdf |work=[[Rio 2016]] |publisher=[[FINA]] |access-date=January 23, 2015 |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150221025939/http://www.fina.org/H2O/docs/events/rio2016/2014-07%20-%20Rio%202016%20-%20Qualification%20System%20-%20FINAL%20-%20Swimming%20-%20EN.pdf |archive-date=February 21, 2015 |df=dmy-all }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|first=Braden|last=Keith|title=Guam Names 2 NCAA-Based Swimmers to 2016 Olympic Roster|url=https://swimswam.com/guam-names-2-ncaa-based-swimmers-2016-olympic-roster/|publisher=SwimSwam|date=July 11, 2016|access-date=July 12, 2016|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160712134515/https://swimswam.com/guam-names-2-ncaa-based-swimmers-2016-olympic-roster/|archive-date=July 12, 2016|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

[[Pilar Shimizu]] competed in the 100&nbsp;m breaststroke. She held the Guamanian national record for the event at the time of competition.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.lohud.com/story/sports/2016/07/23/shimizu-use-experience-compete-rio/87329788/|title=Shimizu will use experience to compete in Rio|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick|publisher=Iohud|access-date=February 18, 2018|date=July 23, 2016}}</ref> Shimizu previously placed 42nd in the event during the 2012 London Olympics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://hub.jhu.edu/2016/07/28/rio-olympics-swimming-hopkins/|title=Johns Hopkins Olympians swimming with the big fish in Rio|publisher=Johns Hopkins University|access-date=February 18, 2018|date=July 28, 2016|last1=O'Shea|first1=Dennis|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170830201936/https://hub.jhu.edu/2016/07/28/rio-olympics-swimming-hopkins/|archive-date=August 30, 2017|df=mdy-all}}</ref> She finished her event with a time of 1:16.65, which was not sufficient to advance.<ref name=shioly />

[[Benjamin Schulte]], the flagbearer for the opening and closing ceremonies, swam in the 100&nbsp;m breaststroke.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/news/2016/08/05/schulte-leads-guam-olympics-opening/88283562/|title=Schulte leads Guam in Olympics opening|last1=George|first1=Duane|date=August 5, 2016|publisher=Pacific Daily News|access-date=February 18, 2018}}</ref> He broke his own national record with a time of 1:03.29 (previous record was 1:03.42).<ref name=pdn /> He was happy with his result and plans on competing in the 2020 Olympics in Tokyo.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.guampdn.com/story/sports/2016/08/06/schulte-breaks-national-record-rio/88339752/|title=Schulte breaks Guam national record in Rio|date=August 7, 2016|access-date=February 18, 2018|publisher=Pacific Daily News|last1=Sablan|first1=Jerick}}</ref>
