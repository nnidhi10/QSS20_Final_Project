'''Lawrence Cherono''' (born August 7, 1988) is a Kenyan long-distance runner. He is currently the 8th fastest marathon performer of all time with his 2:03:04 clocking at the [[2020 Valencia Marathon]].<ref>{{cite web |title=Marathon - men - senior - outdoor |date=19 September 2021 |publisher=Worldathletics.Org. |url=https://www.worldathletics.org/records/all-time-toplists/road-running/marathon/outdoor/men/senior?regionType=world&drop=regular&fiftyPercentRule=regular&page=1&bestResultsOnly=true&firstDay=1899-12-31&lastDay=2021-09-19}}</ref> Cherono is a past winner of both the Boston and Chicago marathons.

In 2024, Cherono received a seven year competition ban backdated to 2022 following an admission of breaching anti-doping rules.

== Career ==
He clocked 2:09:39 to win the [[Honolulu Marathon]] on December 11, 2016. <ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.honolulumarathon.org/kenyan-cherono-sets-smashes-honolulu-marathon-course-record|title=Kenyan Cherono Sets Smashes Honolulu Marathon Course Record|website=Honolulu Marathon|language=en-US|date=2016-12-11|access-date=2025-10-27}}</ref>

He clocked 2:04:06 to win the [[Amsterdam Marathon]] on October 21, 2018.  He also won the Amsterdam Marathon in 2017, running a time of 2:05:09, setting the course record, which he broke in 2018.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.iaaf.org/news/report/cherono-clocks-course-record-amsterdam-marath|title=Cherono clocks 2:04:06 Dutch all-comers record in Amsterdam{{!}} News {{!}} iaaf.org|website=www.iaaf.org|access-date=2019-04-17}}</ref>

On 15 April 2019, Cherono won the [[2019 Boston Marathon|Boston Marathon]] with a time of 2:07:57.  He out-sprinted two-time Boston winner and [[2013 World Championships in Athletics – Men's marathon|World Championship Silver medalist]] [[Lelisa Desisa]] on the final block of [[Boylston Street]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.iaaf.org/news/report/worknesh-degefa-lawrence-cherono-boston-marat|title=Degefa dominates, Cherono outsprints Desisa in Boston {{!}} News {{!}} iaaf.org|website=www.iaaf.org|access-date=2019-04-17}}</ref>

On 13 October 2019, Cherono won the [[2019 Chicago Marathon#Men|2019 Chicago Marathon]] with a time of 2:05:45. He out-kicked three other runners in the final 200 meters to win the race. In 2020 Cherono raced the [[Valencia Marathon]], where he placed 2nd in a new personal best time of 2:03:04.<ref>Lawrence CHERONO | Profile | World Athletics. (2021). Worldathletics.Org. https://www.worldathletics.org/athletes/kenya/lawrence-cherono-14687675</ref>

He qualified to represent [[Kenya at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Kenya]] at the [[2020 Summer Olympics]].<ref>{{Cite web|last=Olobulu|first=Timothy|date=2021-06-19|title=Conseslus, Timothy Cheruiyot out as Kenya names team for Tokyo Olympics|url=https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/sports/2021/06/19/280500/|access-date=2021-06-20|website=Capital Sports|language=en-US}}</ref> At the [[2020 Tokyo Olympic Games]] men's marathon Cherono placed 4th getting out sprinted by [[Abdi Nageeye]] and [[Bashir Abdi]], while [[Eliud Kipchoge]] won.<ref>{{cite web |title=Athletics CHERONO Lawrence - Tokyo 2020 Olympics |date=6 September 2021 |publisher=Olympics.Com |url=https://olympics.com/tokyo-2020/olympic-games/en/results/athletics/athlete-profile-n1337398-cherono-lawrence.htm}}</ref>

== Doping violation and competition ban ==
On July 16, 2022, Cherono was suspended from major marathons after testing positive for [[trimetazidine]], a heart medication listed on the 2022 World Anti-Doping Agency Prohibited List as a metabolic modulator and performance-enhancing drug.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/sports/kenyas-cherono-american-ross-provisionally-suspended-miss-world-championships-2022-07-16//|title = Kenya's Cherono, American Ross provisionally suspended, to miss worlds| website=[[Reuters]] |date = 16 July 2022}}</ref> On June 12, 2024, the Athletics Integrity Unit announced an additional charge against Cherono of tampering with the anti-doping process.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.athleticsintegrity.org/disciplinary-process/provisional-suspensions-in-force//|title = Provisional Suspensions in Force| website=[[Athletics Integrity Unit]] |date = 16 July 2022}}</ref>

Following a two-year investigation during which Cherono denied the allegations, nevertheless in July 2024, Lawrence Cherono admitted to breaching three anti-doping regulations.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.athleticsintegrity.org/downloads/pdfs/disciplinary-process/en/AIU-22-149-CHERONO-Decision-FINAL.pdf |title=Decision of the Athletics Integrity Unit in the case of Mr Lawrence Cherono |date=2 July 2024 |publisher=Athletics Integrity Unit |access-date=11 July 2024}}</ref> He received a four-year ban for the use and presence of Trimetazidine and a four-year ban for tampering with the rules.<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/athletics/articles/crgrqln8vkxo |title=Marathon runner Cherono given seven-year doping ban |date=10 July 2024 |work=BBC News |access-date=11 July 2024}}</ref> The combined eight-year ban was reduced to seven years by the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) due to his early admission and acceptance of the sanction.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://apnews.com/article/lawrence-cherono-doping-boston-chicago-marathon-d048605ee27c281348aacdb96ba16315|title =Former Boston and Chicago marathon winner Lawrence Cherono banned for doping and fake documents | website=AP News |date = 10 July 2024}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=https://athleticsweekly.com/athletics-news/lawrence-cherono-banned-for-seven-years-for-doping-and-tampering-1039989380/ |title=Lawrence Cherono banned for seven years for doping and tampering |work=Athletics Weekly |date=19 July 2024 |first=Tim |last=Adams |access-date=11 July 2024}}</ref>
