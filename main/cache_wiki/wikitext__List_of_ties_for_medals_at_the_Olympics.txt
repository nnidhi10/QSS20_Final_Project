This article lists all '''ties for medals at the [[Olympics]]'''. A tie occurs when two or more individual or teams achieve identical results in the [[Olympics]]. In these cases, there are multiple winners awarded the same medal.

==Ties for medals at the Olympics==
Ties occasionally occur during the games, and since medals are complicated to produce, the Olympic organising committee makes advance arrangements for extra medals to be produced in the event of a tie.<ref>{{cite web|last1=Germano|first1=Sara|title=When Two People Win Gold, Where Do They Get the Extra Medal?|url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB10001424052702304434104579378733694348114|website=Wall Street Journal|access-date=28 August 2016|date=12 February 2014}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|last1=Eveleth|first1=Rose|author-link=Rose Eveleth|title=What Happens When There's a Tie at the Olympics?|url=http://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/what-happens-when-theres-tie-olympics-180949754|website=Smithsonian.com|access-date=2016-11-15|archive-date=2016-11-15|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161115193330/http://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/what-happens-when-theres-tie-olympics-180949754/|url-status=live}}</ref>

=== Number of ties for medals in Olympics history ===
'Total' shows the number of ties for medals in Olympics history, while 'Events' shows the number of events with at least one medal tie. There can be more than one tie for medals for one event as there can be ties for gold, silver, and bronze.

The silver medals were transferred to [[Debbi Wilkes]] and [[Guy Revell]] and the bronze medals to [[Vivian Joseph]] and [[Ronald Joseph (figure skater)|Ronald Joseph]]. Kilius and Bäumler were re-awarded the silvers in 1987, after appealing that other pairs had signed similar contracts but had not been exposed and disqualified. The placements of Wilkes/Revell and Joseph/Joseph remained unclear for many years. In December 2013, the IOC clarified that Wilkes/Revell had not been stripped of their silver after the Germans regained their medals. In November 2014, the IOC stated that both Kilius/Bäumler and Wilkes/Revell are the silver medalists and Joseph/Joseph are the bronze medalists.</ref>

| <ref>{{cite web|title=Salt Lake City 2002 Pairs mixed - Olympic Figure skating|url=https://www.olympic.org/salt-lake-city-2002/figure-skating/pairs-mixed|website=International Olympic Committee|access-date=24 November 2017|language=en|archive-date=1 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171201050636/https://www.olympic.org/salt-lake-city-2002/figure-skating/pairs-mixed|url-status=live}}</ref><ref group=note>The Russian pair of [[Yelena Berezhnaya]] and [[Anton Sikharulidze]] competed against the Canadian pair of [[Jamie Salé]] and [[David Pelletier]] for the gold medal. The Canadians appeared to have skated well enough to win, yet the Russians were awarded the gold. The judging broke along Cold War lines with judges from former Communist countries favouring the Russian pair and judges from Western nations voting for the Canadians. The only exception was the French judge, [[Marie-Reine Le Gougne]], who awarded the gold to the Russians. An investigation revealed that Le Gougne had been pressured to give the gold to the Russian pair regardless of how they skated; in return the Russian judge would look favourably on the French entrants in the ice dancing competition. The IOC decided to award both pairs the gold medal in a second medal ceremony held later in the Games.</ref>

In addition, two finals were held for one [[Rowing at the 1900 Summer Olympics|rowing]] event at the 1900 Olympics, [[Rowing at the 1900 Summer Olympics – Men's coxed four|men's coxed four]]. This was due to a controversy about which boats should advance to the final; thus two separate finals were held, awarding two sets of medals for the same event, both which are considered Olympic championships by the [[International Olympic Committee]].

Similarly, two finals were held for some [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics|sailing]] events at the 1900 Olympics, namely [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics&nbsp;– 0 to .5 ton|0 to .5 ton]], [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics&nbsp;– .5 to 1 ton|.5 to 1 ton]], [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics&nbsp;– 1 to 2 ton|1 to 2 ton]], [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics&nbsp;– 2 to 3 ton|2 to 3 ton]] and [[Sailing at the 1900 Summer Olympics&nbsp;– 3 to 10 ton|3 to 10 ton]].<ref group=note>In this event, however, only the second race winners are listed in the IOC's database.</ref>
