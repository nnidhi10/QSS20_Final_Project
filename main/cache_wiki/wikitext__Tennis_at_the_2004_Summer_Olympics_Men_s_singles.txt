{{main|Tennis at the 2004 Summer Olympics}}

[[Chile at the 2004 Summer Olympics|Chile]]'s [[Nicolás Massú]] defeated the [[United States at the 2004 Summer Olympics|United States]]' [[Mardy Fish]] in the final, 6–3, 3–6, 2–6, 6–3, 6–4 to win the gold medal in Men's Singles [[tennis]] at the [[2004 Summer Olympics]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2004/SPORT/08/22/tennis.massu/index.html
|title=Massu claims second gold in a day|publisher=CNN|accessdate=10 July 2025}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport1/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3589322.stm|title=Massu seals golden double|publisher=BBC News|accessdate=10 July 2025}}</ref> In the bronze-medal match, Chile's [[Fernando González]] defeated the United States' [[Taylor Dent]], 6–4, 2–6, 16–14. Massú became the only man to win both the singles and [[Tennis at the 2004 Summer Olympics – Men's doubles|men's doubles]] gold medals at the same Olympic Games. The two medals were Chile's first in men's singles, and Massú's gold plus the Chileans' doubles gold were the only two gold medals for [[Chile at the Olympics]], until shooter Francisca Crovetto's gold obtained at the [[2024 Summer Olympics]].

The tournament was held at the [[Athens Olympic Tennis Centre|Olympic Tennis Centre]] in [[Athens]], [[Greece]]. There were 64 players from 32 nations.<ref name=Olympedia>{{cite web|url=https://www.olympedia.org/results/45876 |title=Singles, Men |work=Olympedia |access-date=22 December 2020}}</ref> The limit on players per nation had been four since the 2000 Games. Only the final match was best-of-five-sets; all others were best-of-three-sets.

[[Yevgeny Kafelnikov]] of [[Russia at the 2000 Summer Olympics|Russia]] was the reigning gold medalist from [[Tennis at the 2000 Summer Olympics – Men's singles|2000]], but he retired from the sport in 2003.

[[Switzerland at the 2004 Summer Olympics|Switzerland]]'s [[Roger Federer]] was the [[List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players|world No. 1]], but he lost to world No. 74 [[Tomáš Berdych]] of the [[Czech Republic at the 2004 Summer Olympics|Czech Republic]] in the second round.

==Background==

The men's singles tournament of the [[2004 Summer Olympics]] was held at the [[Athens Olympic Tennis Centre]] in [[Athens]], Greece from 15 to 22 August 2004.<ref name="2004TennisOLYMediaGuide" /> [[Tennis at the Summer Olympics|Tennis]] was one of the original sports of the [[1896 Summer Olympics]]. It was withdrawn after the [[1924 Summer Olympics]] due to disagreements between the sport's governing body, the [[International Tennis Federation]] (ITF), and the independent Olympic organisation, the [[International Olympic Committee]] (IOC) over how to define amateur athletes. The IOC reinstated Tennis to the Olympic programme as a demonstration sport at the [[1968 Summer Olympics|1968 Games]] and the [[1984 Summer Olympics|1984 Olympics]] before returning as a full medal sport open to all players at the [[1988 Summer Olympics|1988 Games]].<ref name=2004TennisOLYMediaGuide>{{cite web|title=Tennis Event at the 2004 Olympic Games – Media Guide|url=http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3821_original.PDF|publisher=International Tennis Federation|date=2004|access-date=20 March 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823231019/http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3821_original.PDF|archive-date=23 August 2004|url-status=dead}}</ref> The 2004 tournament was the 12th official medal event in men's singles.

Five of the eight quarterfinalists from the 2000 tournament returned: silver medalist [[Tommy Haas]] of Germany, fourth-place finisher [[Roger Federer]] of Switzerland, and three men eliminated in the quarterfinals: [[Max Mirnyi]] of Belarus, [[Juan Carlos Ferrero]] of Spain, and [[Gustavo Kuerten]] of Brazil.

Algeria, Chinese Taipei, and Cyprus each made their debut in the event. France made its 11th appearance, most among all nations, having missed only the 1904 event.

==Qualification==

Qualification for the single tournament was restricted to four players per [[National Olympic Committee]] (NOC), an organisation representing a country at the Olympics. [[List of national tennis associations|National Tennis Associations]] who were members of the ITF before 1 January 2004 were allowed to nominate players for entry into the competition. The tournament featured a total of 64 players with 48 qualifying on their ITF World Ranking on 14 June and two received invitations from the Tripartite Commission. Any NOC who had more than four players able to qualify by this method were encouraged to choose their highest ranked players eligible to compete in the tournament. The remaining 14 qualified via [[Wild card (sports)|wild card]] places: eight were selected on their world ranking and the remaining six were chosen on the basis of his world ranking, whether his country has representation in tennis, the number of players who were in Athens and his geographical location.<ref name="Qualifying">{{cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3118_original.PDF|title=Qualification|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823220622/http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3118_original.PDF|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref><ref name="Tennis2004OLYRegs">{{cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3110_original.PDF|title=Olympic Tennis Event 2004 – Regulations|publisher=International Tennis Federation|pages=4–7, 11 & 17–18|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823230818/http://www.itftennis.com/shared/medialibrary/pdf/original/IO_3110_original.PDF|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref>

Players who earned automatic entry into the draw and who withdrew from the competition due to illness, injury or bereavement before midnight on 7 August were replaced by one from his own country or the next highest ranking entry. Had this not been the case, then the ITF selected the highest ranked nominated player or eligible competitor if a country had more than four players to the tournament.<ref name="2004TennisOLYMediaGuide" /><ref name="Tennis2004OLYRegs" />

==Tournament summary==

The tournament was a single elimination competition with a bronze-medal match. The competition was played on [[Hardcourt|hard courts]] as the [[Tennis scoring system|best-of-three sets]] in every match until the gold medal game, which was held to the best-of-five sets.<ref name="Tennis2004OLYRegs" /><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.espn.com/olympics/summer04/tennis/news/story?id=1857401|title=ATP, WTA eye rankings points for Olympics|date=11 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040821144118/http://sports.espn.go.com/oly/summer04/tennis/news/story?id=1857401|archive-date=21 August 2004|publisher=ESPN|agency=Associated Press}}</ref>

===Round 1===

The draw for the first round of the championship was made on 12 August in Building H of the Athens 2004 Conference Room at ATHOC Headquarters. The first round of the competition, in which 64 players participated, took place from 15 to 16 August.<ref name="2004TennisOLYMediaGuide" /> Roddick took a 6–3, 7–6 straight sets victory over [[Flávio Saretta]] as wild card entrant Arthurs defeated [[Victor Hănescu]] 6–4, 7–6. [[Marcos Baghdatis]], the 2003 ITF  Junior World champion and a player who received funding from Olympic Solidarity, recovered from one set behind to win 5–7, 7–6, 7–5 over [[Grégory Carraz]].A 6–3, 6–1 triumph came for the fifth seed [[Juan Carlos Ferrero]] against [[Hicham Arazi]] before [[Max Mirnyi]] caused an [[Upset (competition)|upset]] over the 11th seed [[Juan Ignacio Chela]] by taking a 3–6, 7–6, 6–4 win.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12381|title=Men's Round-up, Day One Day Session: Roddick Shakes off Edgy Start|last=Gabriel|first=Craig|date=15 August 2004|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823215450/http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12381|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref> Henman, a silver medallist in the [[Tennis at the 1996 Summer Olympics – Men's doubles|1996 Olympic doubles event]], was the highest placed seed to lose in the first round when he lost to world number 27 [[Jiří Novák]] 6–3, 6–3 due to an inconsistent and error-prone play in a 68-minute match that took place in blustery weather.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport1/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3565030.stm|title=Henman crashes out|date=15 August 2004|publisher=BBC Sport|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040825184150/http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport1/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3565030.stm|archive-date=25 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|last=Mochlinski|first=Kaz|url=https://www.telegraph.co.uk/sport/tennis/2384818/Henmans-dream-ends-early-again.html|title=Henman's dream ends early again|date=16 August 2004|work=The Daily Telegraph|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref>

[[Marat Safin]], a seeded player regarded as one who was in danger of elimination, won the first set of his match against [[Karol Kučera]] within 20 minutes. After Kučera played less cautiously in the second set, a break in the fifth game saw Safin claim a 6–0, 6–4 victory.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12383|title=Men's Round-up, Day One Night Session: Wind & Novak Blow Henman Away|last=Bowers|first=Chris|date=15 August 2004|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823215332/http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12383|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=20 March 2020}}</ref> Moyá, the [[1998 French Open – Men's Singles|1998 French Open]] champion, took almost three hours and a total of five match points in the final two sets of his game against [[Thomas Enqvist]] to win 7–6, 6–7, 9–7.<ref name=":0">{{Cite news|url=https://www.rediff.com/sports/2004/aug/16oly-ten4.htm|title=Moya wins, Dementieva out|date=16 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020|work=[[Rediff.com]]|agency=[[Reuters]]}}</ref> [[Paradorn Srichaphan]], the 12th seed, was upset by Joachim Johansson in a 6–3, 6–3 straight sets defeat,<ref name=":0" /><ref name=":1">{{Cite news|url=https://www.voanews.com/archive/olympic-results-monday-august-16-2004-08-16-0|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200321121220/https://www.voanews.com/archive/olympic-results-monday-august-16-2004-08-16-0|url-status=dead|archive-date=21 March 2020|title=Olympic Results: Monday August 16 – 2004-08-16|date=16 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020|publisher=[[Voice of America]]}}</ref> and another upset victory occurred when the 13th seed [[Andrei Pavel]] lost to [[Ivo Karlović]].<ref name=":1" /> [[Olivier Rochus]] came from a set behind [[Mark Philippoussis]], who played in his first match in four weeks since after receiving artificial cartilage injections into his left knee, to claim a 3–6, 6–0, 6–1 victory. Philippoussis had tendinitis in his left knee that caused discomfort during the match and affected his movement.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12393|title=Men's Round-Up, Day Two Night Session: Federer Starts Olympic Run|last=Gabriel|first=Craig|date=16 August 2004|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823214420/http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12393|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020}}</ref> Federer took 1¾ hours to defeat [[Nikolay Davydenko]] 6–3, 5–7, 6–1.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3569656.stm|title=Federer battles through|date=16 August 2004|publisher=BBC Sport|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040829072345/http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3569656.stm|archive-date=29 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020}}</ref> He received an official warning for [[List of tennis violations|ball abuse]] when he struck the [[Tennis ball|ball]] onto the roof of the centre court in frustration over his play in the second set.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2004/SPORT/08/16/olympics.federer/index.html|title=Federer through after rare warning|date=16 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020|publisher=CNN}}</ref>

===Round 2===

The second round occurred on 17 August.<ref name="2004TennisOLYMediaGuide" /> Calleri withdrew at this stage of the tournament because of strain to his left abdominal, giving his opponent [[Igor Andreev]] a [[walkover]] into the third round and no players from Argentina left in the competition.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12394|title=Calleri withdraws, Andreev advances by walkover|date=17 August 2004|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040825090714/http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12394|archive-date=25 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020}}</ref> Unseeded player and world number 49 [[Mardy Fish]] came from one set and a break point behind to claim a 4–6, 7–6 <small>(7-5)</small>, 6–4 victory over former world number one [[Juan Carlos Ferrero]].<ref name=":2">{{Cite news|url=https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/A120776132/GPS?u=wikipedia&sid=GPS&xid=42344b70|title=Fish surprises Ferrero in Olympic tennis|date=17 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020|agency=United Press International|via=Gale In Context: Biography|url-access=subscription}}</ref> Fish said of his win, "There were a lot of Spanish people out there cheering for him. So whoever was cheering for me, I was pointing at them."<ref name=":2" /> Another second round winner from the United States came in the form of [[Taylor Dent]], who recovered from a slow start to defeat [[Dominik Hrbatý]] 7–6, 6–4.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12395|title=Men's Round-up, Day Three Day Session: Fish Nets Biggest Catch of his Career|last=Bowers|first=Chris|date=17 August 2004|publisher=International Tennis Federation|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040823212818/http://www.itftennis.com/olympics/news/newsarticle.asp?id=12395|archive-date=23 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020}}</ref>

Roddick made 17 [[Ace (tennis)|aces]], 30 [[Glossary of tennis terms#W|winners]] and 57 [[Glossary of tennis terms#U|unforced errors]] in coming back from two match points behind and defeating the 2000 silver medallist [[Tommy Haas]] 6–4, 3–6, 9–7 in a 2-hour and 19 minute match.<ref>{{Cite news|last=Crouse|first=Karen|url=https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/A120917587/GPS?u=wikipedia&sid=GPS&xid=1d5966c8|title=Roddick, Fish Scrap For Wins|date=18 August 2004|work=[[The Palm Beach Post]]|access-date=21 March 2020|page=7C|via=Gale OneFile: News|url-access=subscription}}</ref> Federer was the highest ranked player to lose in the second round when world number 74 [[Tomáš Berdych]] beat him 4–6, 7–5, 7–5 due to a poor serve and a series of unforced errors.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://link.gale.com/apps/doc/A120875903/GPS?u=wikipedia&sid=GPS&xid=614011cd|title=Federer flops; Tennis|date=18 August 2004|work=[[The Times]]|access-date=21 March 2020|page=32|via=Gale Academic OneFile|url-access=subscription}}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Cite web|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3572960.stm|title=Federer crashes out|date=17 August 2004|publisher=BBC Sport|url-status=live|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20040828192321/http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/tennis/3572960.stm|archive-date=28 August 2004|access-date=21 March 2020}}</ref> Berdych said of the victory, "It was everything. This guy won Wimbledon and the Australian Open and now you are the player who beats him. Unbelievable."<ref name=":3" /> Moyá defeated [[Olivier Rochus]] 6–0, 7–6 and tenth seed [[Nicolás Massú]] won 7–6, 6–2 over [[Vince Spadea]].<ref name="SRDraw" />
