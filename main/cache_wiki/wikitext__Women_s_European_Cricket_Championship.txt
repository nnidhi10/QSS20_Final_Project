The '''Women's European Cricket Championship''' was a [[women's cricket]] tournament for teams representing European countries. The first edition was contested in 1989.

==History==

The first Women's European Championship was held in Denmark in July 1989. The teams that took part were [[England women's cricket team|England]], [[Ireland women's cricket team|Ireland]] and the [[Netherlands national women's cricket team|Netherlands]] in addition to the hosts [[Denmark national women's cricket team|Denmark]]. England won all three of their matches, with the other teams winning one match each. England thus won the tournament.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Events/Tables/Womens_European_Championship_1989.html 1989 Women's European Championship Points table at Cricket Archive]</ref> All matches were official [[Women's One-day International cricket|Women's One-Day Internationals]]<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/DNMRK/1989_DNMRK_Womens_European_Championship_1989.html |title=Scorecards for 1989 European Women's Championship at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=24 July 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080724065429/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/DNMRK/1989_DNMRK_Womens_European_Championship_1989.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> and Denmark's match against Ireland was their first such game.<ref name="DWODI">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Records/Denmark/WOdis/Odi_List.html |title=Women's ODIs played by Denmark at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=30 September 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930065619/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Records/Denmark/WOdis/Odi_List.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>

The second edition was held in [[Leicester]], [[Nottingham]] and [[Northamptonshire]] in England in July 1990.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/1990_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_1990.html |title=Scorecards for 1990 European Women's Championship at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=4 December 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081204133734/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/1990_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_1990.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> The same teams as 1989 again competed, and England again won all their games. Ireland won two matches, the Netherlands one and Denmark lost all three of their games.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Events/Tables/Womens_European_Championship_1990.html Points Table for 1990 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive]</ref> After the initial group stage, England and Ireland played in a final, which England won by 65 runs.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Scorecards/53/53190.html Scorecard] of England Women v Ireland Women match, 22 July 1990 at CricketArchive</ref>

The third Championship, again featuring the same four teams, was played in [[Haarlem]] in the Netherlands in July 1991.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/NL/1991_NL_Womens_European_Championship_1991.html |title=Scorecards for the 1991 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=24 July 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080724132646/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/NL/1991_NL_Womens_European_Championship_1991.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> Like the 1989 event, England again won all three of their games, with the other three teams winning one each. Denmark finished second on [[run rate]] and met England in the final, which was won by England by 179 runs.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Scorecards/54/54642.html Scorecard] of Denmark Women v England Women, 20 July 1991 at Cricket Archive</ref>

After a four-year break, the Championship returned in July 1995 in [[Dublin]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/IRELAND/1995_IRELAND_Womens_European_Championship_1995.html |title=Scorecards for 1995 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=30 May 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070530111244/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/IRELAND/1995_IRELAND_Womens_European_Championship_1995.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> The same teams took part as in the previous three tournaments, with England and Ireland topping the points table for the group stage.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Events/Tables/Womens_European_Championship_1995.html Points Table for 1995 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive]</ref> England beat Ireland by seven wickets in the final.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Scorecards/60/60877.html Scorecard] for England Women v Ireland Women, 22 July 1995 at Cricket Archive</ref>

In 1999, the tournament returned to the venue of the first edition, again with the same four teams. England topped the table after the group stage, winning all three of their games, winning the tournament as no final was played.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/1999/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |title=1999 Women's European Championship at CricketEurope |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=29 August 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090829112751/http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/1999/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |url-status=dead }}</ref> Denmark's game against the Netherlands is their last Women's ODI to date.<ref name="DWODI" />

The 2001 tournament, played at [[Bradfield College]], England saw an England Under-19 team replace England.<ref name="2001WEC">{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2001/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |title=2001 Women's European Championship at CricketEurope |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=12 February 2009 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090212123400/http://cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2001/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |url-status=dead }}</ref> Despite England being represented by their Under-19 team, their matches still counted as official Women's ODIs for England.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/2001_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_2001.html Scorecards for 2001 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081204142619/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/2001_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_2001.html |date=4 December 2008 }} – the "wo" code indicates an official women's ODI.</ref> [[Scotland national women's cricket team|Scotland]] replaced Denmark in the event,<ref name="2001WEC" /> and their match against England was their first Women's ODI.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Records/Scotland/WOdis/Odi_List.html |title=List of Scotland's women's ODIs at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=30 September 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930031702/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Records/Scotland/WOdis/Odi_List.html |url-status=dead }}</ref> Ireland won the event, winning all three of their games, the first time England had not won the tournament.<ref name="2001WEC" />

The 2005 event was held in Wales, and saw the first appearance of the [[Wales national women's cricket team|Welsh women's team]]. An England development squad replaced the England Under-19 team. Only the match between Ireland and the Netherlands was an official women's ODI.<ref>[http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/2005_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_2005.html Scorecards for 2005 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070930123525/http://www.cricketarchive.co.uk/Archive/Seasons/ENG/2005_ENG_Womens_European_Championship_2005.html |date=30 September 2007 }} – the "wo" code indicates an official women's ODI.</ref> England won all four of their matches, thus winning the tournament.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2005/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |title=2005 Women's European Championship at Cricket Archive |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=9 July 2008 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20080709050649/http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2005/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |url-status=dead }}</ref>

The tournament became biannual in 2007, when was held in Netherlands.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2007/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |title=2007 Women's European Championship at CricketEurope |access-date=23 May 2007 |archive-date=2 April 2007 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20070402031559/http://www.cricketeurope4.net/CRICKETEUROPE/DATABASE/2007/TOURNAMENTS/EUROWOMEN/about.shtml |url-status=dead }}</ref> England sent a Development team, which won the championship. Also participated Ireland, Netherlands and Scotland.

The 2009 Tournament was held in Dublín, Ireland in three grounds,<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.icc-europe.org/EUROWOMEN/index.shtml|title=2009 Women's European Championship|publisher=ICC Europe|access-date=5 August 2009}}</ref> in this event took part Ireland, Netherlands and Scotland. Ireland won the championship after won their two games.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.icc-europe.org/DATABASE/ARTICLES/articles/000050/005038.shtml|title=Ireland claim European Championships|publisher=ICC/Cricket Europe|date=5 August 2009}}</ref> Also will play the first T20 European championship.
