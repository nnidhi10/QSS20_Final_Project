'''Deontay Leshun Wilder''' ({{IPAc-en|ˈ|w|aɪ|l|d|ər}} {{respell|WYLE|dər}}; born October 22, 1985) is an American [[professional boxer]]. He held the [[World Boxing Council]] (WBC) [[heavyweight]] title from 2015 to 2020. By winning the title, Wilder became the first American world heavyweight champion since 2007, which was the longest period of time in boxing history without an American heavyweight champion.

Wilder had a late start to boxing, taking up the sport at 20 years of age. As an [[amateur boxing|amateur]], he won a bronze medal in the heavyweight division at the [[2008 Olympics]]. This led to his nickname of "the Bronze Bomber", which Wilder coined after [[Joe Louis]], who was known by the nickname of "the Brown Bomber".

Wilder is known for his exceptional punching power, and has been described as one of the hardest punchers in boxing history.<ref>{{cite web |last=Rafael |first=Dan |date=November 25, 2019 |title=Does Deontay Wilder have the most powerful punch in boxing history? |url=https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/28161477 |access-date=August 13, 2025 |publisher=ESPN}}</ref><ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.sportingnews.com/us/boxing/news/artur-beterbiev-ko-rate-record-best-punchers-2023/cebqnjxfm4xumh0dpiyztfaa | title=Where Naoya Inoue's impressive KO rate & record places him among boxing's best punchers in 2023 &#124; Sporting News | date=July 21, 2023 }}</ref> His [[knockout]]-to-win percentage stands at 97.72%,<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://boxrec.com/media/index.php/Highest_Knockout-To-Win_%26_Knockout-To-Beat-Opponent_Percentage_in_World_Heavyweight_Title_Fights#Highest_Knockout-To-Win_Percentage_in_World_Heavyweight_Title_Fights|title=Highest Knockout-To-Win & Knockout-To-Beat-Opponent Percentage in World Heavyweight Title Fights|access-date=11 December 2020}}</ref> the highest in heavyweight history,<ref>{{cite web | url=https://talksport.com/sport/boxing/1410239/anthony-joshua-ko-boxing-deontay-wilder-mike-tyson/ | title=Charles Martin ranks above Anthony Joshua in top ten heavyweight champ KO percentages in history | date=May 2, 2023 }}</ref> with 20 of his knockouts (48%) in the first round. He is a three-time winner of the ''[[Premier Boxing Champions]]'' [[Premier Boxing Champions#Knockout of the Year|Knockout of the Year]] award (2016, 2017, 2019) and a winner of the [[The Ring magazine Knockout of the Year|''Ring'' magazine Knockout of the Year]] award (2019).

==Amateur career==
Wilder started boxing in October 2005, when he entered Skyy Boxing Gym in [[Northport, Alabama]], and began training under Jay Deas. Wilder was 20 at that time.

By 2007, he upset the favorites to win both the [[National Golden Gloves]] and the U.S. [[National Amateur Heavyweight Champions|championships]] at 201&nbsp;lb (91&nbsp;kg).

At the Golden Gloves, he defeated highly touted cadet world champion [[Isiah Thomas (boxer)|Isiah Thomas]], a southpaw from Detroit, and David Thompson, of Brooklyn, in the finals. At the U.S. championship he defeated Quantis Graves and won the final 31–15 over southpaw James Zimmerman of San Jose, California.

At the Olympic trials, he beat Graves twice more and won the Olympic trials in only 21 bouts. Early in 2008 he scored a career-best win by edging out world championship silver medalist and future Olympic champion [[Rakhim Chakhkeiv|Rakhim Chakhiyev]] in Russia. He qualified for the Olympics by beating [[Deivi Julio Blanco|Deivis Julio]] 6:5 [[Jorge Quiñonez|Jorge Quinones]] from Ecuador on double countback and Brazilian [[Rafael Lima (boxer)|Rafael Lima]] 6:5 at the qualifier.

In a Russia-USA dual match on February 29, 2008, in [[Novosibirsk]], Wilder was knocked out by [[Evgenyi Romanov]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://fr.sports.yahoo.com/news/boxe-evgeny-romanov-dernier-homme-084117921.html|title=Boxe: Evgeny Romanov, le dernier homme à avoir mis KO le surpuissant Deontay Wilder|website=fr.sports.yahoo.com|language=fr-FR|access-date=2019-11-26}}</ref>

Wilder then competed at heavyweight in the [[2008 Summer Olympics|2008 Olympics]], defeating Abdelaziz Touilbini of Algeria and Mohamed Arjaoui of Morocco before losing to [[Clemente Russo]] of Italy in the semi-final to earn a bronze medal.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.olympic.org/olympic-results/beijing-2008/boxing/81-91kg-heavyweight-m?event=31432|title=81 - 91kg (heavyweight) men results - Boxing - Beijing 2008 Olympics|website=olympic.org|access-date=2016-04-20}}</ref>

Wilder had an approximate amateur record of 30–5.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.doghouseboxing.com/Gabriel/Montoya_110112.htm|title=Developing Deontay Wilder|access-date=2016-12-28|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130114125605/http://doghouseboxing.com/Gabriel/Montoya_110112.htm|archive-date=2013-01-14|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=http://boxrec.com/media/index.php/Deontay_Wilder#Amateur_Highlights|title=Deontay Wilder - BoxRec|access-date=2017-01-17|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170118052830/http://boxrec.com/media/index.php/Deontay_Wilder#Amateur_Highlights|archive-date=2017-01-18|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Professional career==

===Early career===
Wilder made his professional debut at the age of 23 on November 15, 2008, at the Vanderbilt University Memorial Gymnasium in [[Nashville, Tennessee|Nashville]], [[Tennessee]]. He faced Ethan Cox (2–2–1), winning by second-round [[technical knockout]] (TKO) after knocking Cox down three times. Wilder fought seven times in 2009, winning all the fights in round one.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.tuscaloosanews.com/sports/20090315/another-easy-win-for-wilder|title=Another easy win for Wilder|date=March 15, 2009|website=Tuscaloosa News|access-date=January 4, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170111005558/http://www.tuscaloosanews.com/sports/20090315/another-easy-win-for-wilder|archive-date=January 11, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> By October 2012, Wilder racked up a 25-fight win streak, winning all by knockout and all within four rounds. Some notable stoppages included former [[World Boxing Association|WBA]] [[heavyweight]] title challenger [[Owen Beck (boxer)|Owen Beck]] (27–10, 20 KOs), former [[World Boxing Organization|WBO]] [[light heavyweight]] title challenger [[DeAndrey Abron]] (15–6, 10 KOs) and former WBO heavyweight challenger Damon Reed (46–15, 32 KOs).<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.boxingnews24.com/2012/06/deontay-wilder-destroys-owen-beck-in-3rd-round/|title=Deontay Wilder destroys Owen Beck in 3rd round!|access-date=January 4, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170111000745/http://www.boxingnews24.com/2012/06/deontay-wilder-destroys-owen-beck-in-3rd-round/|archive-date=January 11, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/deontay-wilder-knocks-damon-reed-out-two-rounds--40528|title=Deontay Wilder Knocks Damon Reed Out in Two Rounds|date=June 19, 2011|access-date=January 4, 2017}}</ref>

Wilder won his first title in professional boxing when he knocked out 37-year-old Kelvin Price (13–0, 6 KOs) at the [[Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena|Memorial Sports Arena]] in [[Los Angeles]], on December 15, 2012.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/wilder-price-deal-place-khan-molina-sho-card--58701|title=Wilder-Price Deal in Place For Khan-Molina SHO Card|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref> The fight was originally slated to take place in August 2012.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/deontay-wilder-vs-kelvin-price-set-8-4-on-fox-sports--54605|title=Deontay Wilder vs. Kelvin Price Set For 8/4 On Fox Sports|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref> Wilder fought patiently through the first two rounds and mostly measured Price. The fight ended when a right hand from Wilder caught Price on the jaw and sent him back into the ropes and down. He attempted to get to his feet, but struggled which led referee Ray Corona to wave an end to the bout. Wilder claimed the vacant [[World Boxing Council|WBC]] Continental Americas heavyweight title, which he went on to successfully defend twice.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.boxingnews24.com/2012/12/deontay-wilder-destroys-kelvin-price-in-3rd-round-tko/|title=Deontay Wilder destroys Kelvin Price in 3rd round TKO!|date=December 15, 2012|access-date=January 4, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170112182836/http://www.boxingnews24.com/2012/12/deontay-wilder-destroys-kelvin-price-in-3rd-round-tko/|archive-date=January 12, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/alfredo-angulo-struggles-wilder-blasts-price-three--60514|title=Alfredo Angulo Struggles, Wilder Blasts Price in Three|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref>

=== Rise up the ranks ===
On April 3, 2013, [[Golden Boy Promotions]] announced that Wilder would fight former European heavyweight champion and 2000 Olympic gold medalist [[Audley Harrison]] (31–6, 23 KOs) at the [[Sheffield Arena|Motorpoint Arena]] in [[Sheffield]], his UK debut, on April 27. Harrison was coming into this fight looking to rebuild after winning the [[Prizefighter series|prizefighter heavyweight tournament]] two months earlier. The card, which was being shown live on [[Showtime (TV network)|Showtime]] in USA, was headlined by [[Amir Khan (boxer)|Amir Khan]]'s return in the UK against [[Julio Díaz (boxer)|Julio Díaz]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/deontay-wilder-audley-harrison-finalized-khan-diaz--64016|title=Deontay Wilder-Audley Harrison Finalized For Khan-Diaz|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref> Harrison stated that he would retire if he lost.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/audley-if-wilder-beats-me-its-over-risk-worth-it--64046|title=Audley: If Wilder Beats Me It's Over, But Risk Worth It!|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref> Wilder knocked out Harrison in round one. Wilder found an opening 49 seconds into the opening round and caught Harrison with a big right hand that rocked him back into the ropes. A follow-up barrage from Wilder sent Harrison into the corner, crumpling onto the floor. Harrison used the ropes to get to his feet at the count of eight, but referee Terry O'Connor called off the bout. The official time of stoppage was 1:22 of the first round.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.badlefthook.com/2013/4/27/4275666/wilder-vs-harrison-results-deontay-wilder-scores-first-round-tko-boxing-news|title=Deontay Wilder scores first round TKO|date=April 27, 2013|access-date=January 10, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170112182827/http://www.badlefthook.com/2013/4/27/4275666/wilder-vs-harrison-results-deontay-wilder-scores-first-round-tko-boxing-news|archive-date=January 12, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2013/apr/27/audley-harrison-crumbles-defeat-70-seconds|title=Audley Harrison crumbles to defeat to Deontay Wilder in 70 seconds|date=April 27, 2013|website=theguardian.com|access-date=January 10, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170112191025/https://www.theguardian.com/sport/2013/apr/27/audley-harrison-crumbles-defeat-70-seconds|archive-date=January 12, 2017|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/anthony-ogogo-wins-big-debut-wilder-kos-harrison--64896|title=Anthony Ogogo Wins Big In Debut; Wilder KO's Harrison|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-03|language=en-us}}</ref> Four days after the fight, Harrison announced his retirement at the age of 41.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/boxing/22324777|title=Audley Harrison hints at retirement after defeat by Deontay Wilder|date=April 27, 2013|website=BBC Boxing|access-date=January 10, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160509183302/http://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/boxing/22324777|archive-date=May 9, 2016|url-status=live}}</ref>

On May 9, [[Frank Warren (promoter)|Frank Warren]] announced a card that would take place at the [[Wembley Arena]] in [[London]] on June 15, 2013, which would feature Wilder and British boxer [[Derek Chisora]] as the main event. Prior to the announcement, Golden Boy and Wilder's camp stated there was no deal in place.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/frank-warren-wilder-chisora-set-6-15-wembley-arena--65339|title=Frank Warren: Wilder-Chisora Set, 6/15 At Wembley Arena|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-05|language=en-us}}</ref> The fight fell through after Wilder was arrested in May following a domestic assault in [[Las Vegas]], [[Nevada]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rafael |first=Dan |date=June 26, 2013 |title=Deontay Wilder to fight on Aug. 9 |url=https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/9423373 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130729174057/http://espn.go.com/boxing/story/_/id/9423373/deontay-wilder-face-sergei-liakhovich |archive-date=July 29, 2013 |access-date=August 13, 2025 |website=ESPN}}</ref> Days later, Wilder signed [[Al Haymon]] as his new advisor.<ref>[https://www.boxingscene.com/wilder-brings-al-haymon-reach-next-level--65599 Wilder Brings in Al Haymon To Reach "The Next Level"] - Boxing Scene, 16 May 2016</ref>

==== Wilder vs. Duhaupas ====

Duhaupas had never been stopped before in his career inside the distance. In the post fight, Wilder praised Duhaupas' toughness, saying "We knew he was tough. We knew he was mentally tough. We knew he was going to come. That's why you can't criticize nobody you don't know. ==== Wilder vs. Szpilka ====
[[File:Deontay Wilder WBC Champion.jpg|thumb|Deontay Wilder poses with the WBC Championship Belt.]]
In December 2015, Showtime confirmed that terms had been agreed for Wilder to defend his WBC title against Polish boxer [[Artur Szpilka]] (20–1, 15 KOs) on January 16, 2016, at the [[Barclays Center]], [[Brooklyn]], [[New York City|New York]], where the winner would be scheduled to fight WBC mandatory challenger [[Alexander Povetkin]].<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/wilder-szpilka-confirmed-jan-16-barclays-center--99125|title=Wilder-Szpilka Confirmed, Jan. 16 At Barclays Center|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-05|language=en-us}}</ref> A crowd of 12,668 mostly pro-Polish fans was announced. Szpilka looked very strong as the bout began, and won the first three rounds with his awkward southpaw stance, rapid foot movement, and talent for slipping punches. Szpilka made Wilder appear wild with his punches, as Wilder missed 175 punches thrown at Szpilka, mostly head punches. Entering round nine, Szpilka, aware from the ring commentators he could no longer win the fight on the cards, changed strategy and took a gamble. Going to the inside, Szpilka swung for the rafters, but Wilder read Szpilka and connected first with a powerful right hand to the face. Szpilka fell to the canvas, and his head jerked backwards in a reflex motion, abruptly ending the competitive contest and sending the ringside physicians and emergency medical personnel immediately into the ring. At the time of the stoppage, Wilder was winning the bout with the scores of 78–74 (twice) and 77–75.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rafael |first=Dan |date=January 18, 2016 |title=Scorecard: Wilder produces KO of year candidate |url=https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/14595368 |access-date=August 13, 2025 |website=ESPN.com}}</ref> Wilder reportedly earned a career-high $1.5 million compared to Szpilka who had a $250,000 purse.<ref name=":0"/> The fight averaged 500,000 viewers and peaked at 623,000 viewers.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/wilder-szpilka-tv-ratings-not-indicative-overall-exposure--100632|title=Wilder-Szpilka TV Ratings Not Indicative Of Overall Exposure|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-05|language=en-us}}</ref>

After the fight, newly crowned heavyweight champion [[Tyson Fury]] entered the ring going face-to-face in a heated verbal exchange with Wilder, calling him out. In the post fight press conference, Wilder rated his performance at 5/10. Wilder was not in a celebratory mood and said his concerns for Szpilka meant he was not in the mood for the confrontation with Fury. Wilder explained "We risk our lives in there for your entertainment. I want to knock my opponents out, but not hurt them. I want them to be able to go home to their family." Szpilka regained consciousness before leaving the ring on a stretcher, and recovered.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Rafael |first=Dan |date=January 17, 2016 |title=Deontay Wilder retains title with devastating KO of Artur Szpilka |url=https://www.espn.com/boxing/story/_/id/14586613 |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160408025536/http://www.espn.co.uk/boxing/story/_/id/14586613/deontay-wilder-ko-artur-szpilka-9th-round |archive-date=2016-04-08 |access-date=August 13, 2025 |website=ESPN.com}}</ref> The knockout was voted "Knockout of The Year" by [[Premier Boxing Champions]].<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/wilder-vs-szpilka-pbc-knockout-year--112162|title=Wilder vs. Szpilka - PBC's Knockout of The Year|date=December 28, 2016|website=Boxing Scene|access-date=December 29, 2016}}</ref>

==== Wilder vs. Arreola ====
Following Wilder's return to the U.S., on May 26, former two-time world title challenger Chris Arreola (36–4–1–2, 31 KOs) became the frontrunner to challenge Wilder. At the time, Arreola was not ranked by the WBC because he had tested positive for [[Cannabis (drug)|marijuana]] after his fight against [[Travis Kauffman]]. Arreola's 90-day suspension ended in March 2016 and he was likely to re-enter the WBC rankings.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/deontay-wilder-chris-arreola-play-summer-showdown--105034|title=Deontay Wilder-Chris Arreola in Play For Summer Showdown|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-14|language=en-us}}</ref> By June 13, the fight was confirmed and would take place on July 16, 2016, at the [[Legacy Arena]], Birmingham, Alabama.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.boxingscene.com/chris-arreolas-trainer-confirms-deontay-wilder-fight-716--105651|title=Chris Arreola's Trainer Confirms Deontay Wilder Fight For 7/16|work=BoxingScene.com|access-date=2018-01-14|language=en-us}}</ref> For the fight, Wilder wore white trunks with black trim, featuring a portrait of his personal idol and the late heavyweight boxing legend Muhammad Ali who had died recently. The attendance at the arena was announced as being 11,974. Wilder dominated the entire fight with his sharp jab, which caused severe swelling to Arreola's left eye. ==== Wilder vs. Ortiz II ====
{{Main|Deontay Wilder vs. Luis Ortiz II}}

In a rematch of their 2018 fight, Wilder faced [[Luis Ortiz (Cuban boxer)|Luis Ortiz]] again on November 23, 2019, winning by seventh-round knockout to retain his WBC heavyweight title after being behind on all three of the judges' scorecards 58–56 and 59–55 (twice).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.badlefthook.com/2019/11/24/20980021/wilder-vs-ortiz-2-scorecards-judges-all-had-luis-ortiz-ahead-deontay-wilder-ko-pbc-fox-boxing-news|title=Wilder vs Ortiz 2 scorecards: Judges all had Luis Ortiz ahead at time of Deontay Wilder KO|first=Scott|last=Christ|date=November 24, 2019|website=Badlefthook.com|access-date=December 21, 2021}}</ref><ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/live/2019/nov/23/deontay-wilder-v-luis-ortiz-ii-live-fight|title=Deontay Wilder v Luis Ortiz II: WBC heavyweight championship – as it happened|last=Graham|first=Bryan Armen|date=2019-11-24|work=The Guardian|access-date=2019-12-05|language=en-GB|issn=0261-3077}}</ref> After the fight, Wilder was full of praise for his opponent, calling Ortiz "the most dangerous heavyweight in the division" and "an amazing fighter".<ref name=":3">{{Cite web|date=2019-11-24|title=Deontay Wilder ties Muhammad Ali with 10 consecutive title defenses|url=https://www.boxingnews24.com/2019/11/deontay-wilder-ties-muhammad-ali-with-10-consecutive-title-defenses/|access-date=2021-05-21|website=Boxing News 24|language=en-US}}</ref> Ortiz was ranked #3 by the WBC at the time.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Wilder vs Ortiz II - News, Tape, Ringwalk, TV, Streaming & Tickets|url=https://box.live/fights/wilder-vs-ortiz-ii/|access-date=2021-11-12|website=Box.Live|language=en-US}}</ref> Wilder's knockout of Ortiz was selected as the winner of [[The Ring magazine Knockout of the Year|''The Ring'' Magazine Knockout of The Year]] award for 2019.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Knockout of the Year|url=https://www.ringtv.com/article/2019knockout-2/|access-date=2021-05-26|website=The Ring}}</ref>

His rematch with Ortiz had been the sixth time Wilder had fought a boxer who was ranked in the global heavyweight top ten by [[BoxRec]] within a year of the bout. The others were [[Malik Scott]] (KO 1), [[Bermane Stiverne]] (UD 12), Luis Ortiz in their first match (TKO 10), Tyson Fury (SD 12), and Dominic Breazeale (KO 1).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://boxrec.com/media/index.php/BoxRec%27s_Annual_Ratings:_Heavyweight_Annuals|title=BoxRec's Annual Ratings: Heavyweight Annuals - BoxRec|website=Boxrec.com|access-date=December 21, 2021}}</ref>

The win marked Wilder's tenth successful consecutive defense of his heavyweight title, matching [[Muhammad Ali]]'s record of 10 consecutive defenses. Wilder was happy to accomplish the feat: "It feels amazing. Muhammad Ali is one of my idols". Wilder and Ali's feat is only bettered by [[Joe Louis]], [[Larry Holmes]], [[Wladimir Klitschko]] and [[Tommy Burns (Canadian boxer)|Tommy Burns]].<ref name=":3"/> Wilder failed to surpass Ali's total, as he went on to lose his next fight.<ref name=":4">{{Cite news |last=Graham |first=Bryan Armen |date=2020-02-23 |title=Tyson Fury beats Deontay Wilder to win WBC heavyweight championship – as it happened |url=https://www.theguardian.com/sport/live/2020/feb/22/deontay-wilder-tyson-fury-2-heavyweight-boxing-rematch-las-vegas |access-date=2021-05-21 |work=The Guardian |language=en-GB |issn=0261-3077}}</ref>

Wilder's attorney Paul Patterson claimed Wilder had "instinctively acted under the false impression that someone was stealing from him", and that he "regrets his actions".
