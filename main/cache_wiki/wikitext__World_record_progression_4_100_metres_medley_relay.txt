This article includes the '''world record progression for the 4×100 metres medley relay''', and it shows the chronological history of world record times in that competitive [[swimming (sport)|swimming]] event. The 4×100 metres medley relay is a [[medley swimming|medley]] race in which each of four swimmers on a team swims a 100-metre leg of the relay, each swimming a different [[List of swimming styles|stroke]], in the following sequence:
# [[Backstroke]] (this can only be the first stroke, due to the necessity of starting this leg in the pool rather than by diving in)
# [[Breaststroke]]
# [[Butterfly stroke|Butterfly]]
# [[Freestyle swimming|Freestyle]] ("freestyle" means anything swum on the front – most swimmers use the [[front crawl]]).

Swimming world records are recognized by and maintained by [[FINA]] ("Fédération Internationale de Natation"), the international competitive swimming and aquatics federation that oversees the sport in international competition.  World records in the medley relay event were first recognized by FINA in 1953.  The long course (50-metre pool) world records are historically older than the short-course (25-metre pool) records.  FINA amended its regulations governing the recognition of world records in 1956; specifically, FINA mandated that only record times that were contested in 50-metre (or 55-yard) pools were eligible for recognition after that time.<ref>Craig Lord, "[http://www.swimvortex.com/a-time-of-change-for-world-records/  A Time of Change for World Records] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170608130928/http://www.swimvortex.com/a-time-of-change-for-world-records/ |date=2017-06-08 }}," SwimVortex.com (28 May 2013).  Retrieved 20 March 2015.</ref>  The short-course world records have been separately recognized since 1991. On July 25, 2013 FINA Technical Swimming Congress voted to allow world records in the long course mixed 400 free relay and mixed 400 medley relay, as well as in six events in short course meters: the mixed 200 medley and 200 free relays, as well as the men's and women's 200 free relays and the men's and women's 200 medley relays.<ref>{{cite web|title=FINA Swimming Rules 2013 - 2017 incl. World Record Application Form|url=http://www.fina.org/H2O/docs/rules/sw_rules_20132017_inclwrform.pdf|publisher=[[FINA]]|date=16 August 2013|access-date=5 December 2015|archive-date=24 August 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150824112202/http://www.fina.org/H2O/docs/rules/sw_rules_20132017_inclwrform.pdf|url-status=dead}}</ref> In October 2013 FINA decided to establish "standards" before something can be recognized as the first world record in these events.<ref>{{cite news|title=FINA To Set World Best Standards For Mixed Relays|url=http://www.swimvortex.com/fina-to-set-world-best-standards-for-mixed-relays/|publisher=swimvortex.com|author=Craig Lord|date=3 October 2013|access-date=5 December 2015|archive-date=8 December 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20151208091537/http://www.swimvortex.com/fina-to-set-world-best-standards-for-mixed-relays/|url-status=dead}}</ref> But later on March 13, 2014 FINA has officially ratified the eight world records set by Indiana University swimmers at the IU Relay Rally held on September 26, 2013 in Bloomington.<ref name="Swimming World Magazine">{{cite magazine|title=FINA Goes Back in Time to Ratify Indiana Hoosier Swims as World Records|url=http://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/lane9/news/World/38199.asp?q=FINA-Goes-Back-in-Time-to-Ratify-Indiana-Hoosier-Swims-as-World-Records|magazine=[[Swimming World Magazine]]|date=13 March 2014|access-date=5 December 2015}}</ref>

It is possible to set an individual world record in the [[World record progression 100 metres backstroke|100 metres backstroke]] by swimming the backstroke first leg of the 4×100-metre medley relay, except in the mixed relay.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=http://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/news/ryan-murphys-blistering-backstroke-leads-usa-to-mixed-400-medley-relay-world-record-at-2015-fina-world-championships/ |title=Ryan Murphy's Blistering Backstroke Leads USA to Mixed 400 Medley Relay World Record at 2015 FINA World Championships |magazine=[[Swimming World Magazine]] |access-date=December 5, 2015 }}</ref>  Swimming the other three legs of the medley relay cannot qualify as world records as FINA criteria require a "static start" for world record recognition – swimming relay exchanges are characterized as "dynamic".

The first Olympics at which the 4×100&nbsp;metres medley relay event was contested was the [[Swimming at the 1960 Summer Olympics|1960 Summer Olympics]] in Rome.
