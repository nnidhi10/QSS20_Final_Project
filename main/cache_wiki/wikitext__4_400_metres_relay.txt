The '''4&nbsp;×&nbsp;400&nbsp;metres relay''' or long relay is an athletics [[track and field|track event]] in which teams consist of four runners who each complete 400&nbsp;metres or one lap, totaling 1600 meters. It is traditionally the final event of a track meet. The first leg and the first bend of the second leg are run in lanes. Start lines are thus staggered over a greater distance than in an individual [[400 metres|400&nbsp;metres]] race; the runners then typically move to the inside of the track. The slightly longer '''4 × 440 yards relay''', on an [[Imperial units|Imperial distance]], was a formerly run British Commonwealth and American event, until [[metrication]] was completed in the 1970s.

==Format==
[[Relay race]] runners typically carry a relay baton which they must transfer between teammates. Runners have a 20&nbsp;m box (usually marked with blue lines) in which to transfer the baton. The first transfer is made within the staggered lane lines; for the second and third transfers, runners typically line up across the track despite the fact that runners are usually running in line on the inside of the track. This prevents confusion and collisions during transfer. Unlike the [[4 × 100 metres relay|4&nbsp;×&nbsp;100&nbsp;m]] relay, runners in the 4&nbsp;×&nbsp;400 typically look back and grasp the baton from the incoming runner, due to the fatigue of the incoming runner, and the wider margins allowed by the longer distance of the race. Consequently, disqualification is rare.

As runners have a running start, split times cannot be compared to individual 400 m performances. Internationally, the U.S. men's team has dominated the event, but have been challenged by [[Jamaica]] in the 1950s and Britain in the 1990s.  The current men's Olympic champions are from the United States.

According to the IAAF rules, world records in relays can only be set if all team members have the same nationality.

=== Mixed ===
{{main|Mixed 4 × 400 metres relay}}

[[Mixed 4 × 400 metres relay|Mixed-sex 4 × 400 metres relays]] were introduced at the [[2017 IAAF World Relays]], with the IAAF first recognizing a world record in that event at the [[2019 World Athletics Championships]]. In March 2022 World Athletics Council decided a set order – man, woman, man, woman – at future championships.<ref>{{cite news|title=Key competition decisions made at 227th World Athletics Council Meeting|url=https://www.worldathletics.org/news/press-releases/competition-decisions-227th-council-meeting|publisher=World Athletics|date=15 March 2022|access-date=9 April 2022}}</ref>  Eventually, the format was added to the Olympics, starting with the [[Athletics at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Mixed 4 × 400 metres relay|Tokyo Olympics]] in 2021.

==Notable splits==

===Men===
* [[Herb McKenley]] ([[Jamaica]]) ran a 44.6 split in the [[Athletics at the 1952 Summer Olympics|1952 Helsinki Olympic]] final.
* [[Ron Freeman]] (USA) ran a 43.2 split in the [[Athletics at the 1968 Summer Olympics|1968 Mexico Olympic]] final.
* [[Julius Sang]] ([[Kenya]]) ran a 43.6 split in the [[Athletics at the 1972 Summer Olympics|1972 Munich Olympic]] final.
* [[Alberto Juantorena]] (Cuba) ran a 43.7 split in the [[1977 IAAF World Cup]] event as part of the Americas team.
* [[Quincy Watts]] ran a 43.1 split and [[Steve Lewis (sprinter)|Steve Lewis]] (USA) ran a 43.4 split in the [[Athletics at the 1992 Summer Olympics|1992 Barcelona Olympic]] final.
* [[Butch Reynolds]] ran a 43.23 split and [[Michael Johnson (sprinter)|Michael Johnson]] (both USA) ran a 42.91 split in the [[1993 World Championships in Athletics|1993 Stuttgart World Championship]] final; the USA team's final time of 2:54.29 is the current world record.
* [[Mark Richardson (sprinter)|Mark Richardson]] (GBR) ran a 43.57 split and [[Davian Clarke]] (JAM) ran a 43.51 split in the [[1997 World Championships in Athletics|1997 Athens World Championship]] final.
* [[Jeremy Wariner]] (USA) ran a 43.10 split in the [[2007 World Championships in Athletics – Men's 4 × 400 metres relay|2007 Osaka World Championship]] final.
* [[Jeremy Wariner]] (USA) ran a 43.18 split in the [[Athletics at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's 4 × 400 metres relay|2008 Beijing Olympic]] final.
* [[Michael Norman (sprinter)|Michael Norman]] (USA) ran a 43.06 split in the 2018 NCAA West Preliminaries final.<ref>{{cite news|title=Norman and Benjamin the latest stars to shine in the IAAF Diamond League|url=https://www.iaaf.org/news/feature/michael-norman-rai-benjamin-diamond-league|access-date=15 January 2019|newspaper=IAAF|date=1 July 2018}}</ref>
* [[Matthew Hudson-Smith]] (GBR) ran a 43.00 split, [[Letsile Tebogo]] (BOT) ran a 43.04 split, and [[Rai Benjamin]] (USA) ran a 43.13 in the [[Athletics at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Men's 4 × 400 metres relay|2024 Paris Olympics]] final.<ref>{{cite web |date=10 August 2024 |title=Men's 4 x 400m Relay - Final results |url=https://olympics.com/OG2024/pdf/OG2024/ATH/OG2024_ATH_C73D_ATHM4X400M------------FNL-000100--.pdf |access-date=11 August 2024 |website=Olympics}}</ref>

===Women===
* [[Jarmila Kratochvílová]] ([[Czechoslovakia|CZE]]) ran a 47.6 split in the [[1982 European Athletics Championships|1982 Athens European Championship final]], a 47.75 split in the [[1983 World Championships in Athletics|1983 Helsinki World Championship final]], and a 47.9 split in the 1983 Europa Cup in London.
* [[Marita Koch]] ([[East Germany|GDR]]) ran a 47.70 split in Erfurt 1984, a 47.9 split in the 1982 European Championship final, and a 47.9 split at the [[1985 IAAF World Cup|1985 Canberra World Cup]].
* [[Allyson Felix]] (USA) ran a 47.72 split in the [[2015 World Championships in Athletics|2015 Beijing World Championships final]], a 48.01 split in the [[2007 World Championships in Athletics|2007 Osaka World Championships final]], and a 48.20 split in the [[Athletics at the 2012 Summer Olympics|2012 London Olympic final]].
* [[Olga Nazarova]] and [[Olga Bryzgina]] ([[USSR]]) both ran a 47.80 split in the [[Athletics at the 1988 Summer Olympics|1988 Seoul Olympics]] final; the Soviet team's final time of 3:15.17 is the current world record.
* [[Sydney McLaughlin]] (USA) ran a 47.70<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/olympics/2024/08/10/usa-womens-4x400-olympics-gold-medal/74751695007/ | title=Team USA wins women's 4x400 for eighth consecutive Olympic gold medal | website=[[USA Today]] }}</ref> split in the [[Athletics at the 2024 Summer Olympics|2024 Paris Olympics]], and a 47.91 split at the [[2022 World Athletics Championships|2022 World Championships]] in Eugene.
* [[Femke Bol]] (NED) ran a 47.93 split in the [[Athletics at the 2024 Summer Olympics – Mixed 4 × 400 metres relay|2024 Paris Olympics]] mixed 4 × 400 final.
* [[Florence Griffith Joyner]] (USA) ran a 48.08 split in the 1988 Seoul Olympic final.
