[[Canada]] competed at the [[2020 Summer Olympics]] in [[Tokyo]]. Originally scheduled to take place from 24 July to 9 August 2020, the Games were postponed to 23 July to 8 August 2021, because of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Joint Statement from the International Olympic Committee and the Tokyo 2020 Organising Committee|url=https://www.olympic.org/news/joint-statement-from-the-international-olympic-committee-and-the-tokyo-2020-organising-committee|access-date=28 March 2020|publisher=[[Olympics]]|date=24 March 2020|archive-date=19 April 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200419063520/https://www.olympic.org/news/joint-statement-from-the-international-olympic-committee-and-the-tokyo-2020-organising-committee|url-status=live}}</ref> Since the nation's debut in [[1900 Summer Olympics|1900]], [[Canadians|Canadian]] athletes have appeared in every edition of the Summer Olympic Games, with the exception of the [[1980 Summer Olympics]] in Moscow because of the country's support for [[1980 Summer Olympics boycott|United States-led boycott]].

Before the official postponement, the [[Canadian Olympic Committee]] and [[Canadian Paralympic Committee]] initially announced their intention not to send teams to both the Olympics and Paralympics.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://olympic.ca/press/team-canada-will-not-send-athletes-to-games-in-summer-2020-due-to-covid-19-risks/|title=Team Canada will not send athletes to Games in summer 2020 due to COVID-19 risks|website=olympic.ca|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=23 March 2020|access-date=23 March 2020|archive-date=16 April 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200416035739/https://olympic.ca/press/team-canada-will-not-send-athletes-to-games-in-summer-2020-due-to-covid-19-risks/|url-status=live}}</ref> Following the announcement on the postponement, the COC and CPC issued a statement that says, in part, that Team Canada "will rise to the challenge to showcase our very best on the international stage," without explicitly saying that Canadian athletes will take part in the games.<ref>{{cite web|title=Canadian Olympic Committee COVID-19 Updates|url=https://olympic.ca/press/canadian-olympic-committee-covid-19-updates/|website=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|access-date=15 May 2020|date=25 March 2020|archive-date=7 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200807161114/https://olympic.ca/press/canadian-olympic-committee-covid-19-updates/|url-status=live}}</ref>

As part of the [[Canada Day]] celebrations held on [[Parliament Hill]], [[Ottawa]] in 2019, former three time Olympic gold medalist in rowing, [[Marnie McBean]] was named as the [[Chef De Mission]] for the team.<ref>{{cite news|date=1 July 2019|title=Marnie McBean named Canada's chef de mission for 2020 Games|url=https://www.ctvnews.ca/sports/article/marnie-mcbean-named-canadas-chef-de-mission-for-2020-games/|work=[[CTV News]]|location=Toronto, Ontario, Canada|access-date=13 June 2021|archive-date=17 August 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210817185212/https://www.ctvnews.ca/sports/marnie-mcbean-named-canada-s-chef-de-mission-for-2020-games-1.4489482|url-status=live}}</ref>

On July 13, 2021, the [[Canadian Olympic Committee]] officially announced the full team of 370 athletes (145 men and 225 women) competing in 30 sports, the largest team the country has sent to the games since [[1984 Summer Olympics|Los Angeles 1984]] and an increase of 56 from [[2016 Summer Olympics|Rio 2016]].<ref name=press>{{cite press release|orig-date=15 July 2021|date=13 July team.<ref name=press/> A total of eight squads qualified in team sports, tied for the most ever with [[1976 Summer Olympics|Montreal 1976]].<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/team-canada-taking-371-athletes-to-tokyo-largest-games-contingent-since-los-angeles-1984-1.6100533|title=Canada taking 371 athletes to Tokyo, largest A total of 227 athletes competed at their first Olympics, and 134 of them returned from Rio 2016.<ref>{{cite news|date=13 July 2021|title=Canada to send largest Olympic team in 37 years to Tokyo|url=https://www.reuters.com/lifestyle/sports/canada-send-largest-olympic-team-37-years-tokyo-2021-07-13/|work=[[Reuters]]|location=London, England, United Kingdom|access-date=13 July Due to the effects of the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], the [[International Olympic Committee]] announced in July 2021, that travelling alternates would be allowed to compete in the following team sports: field hockey, football (soccer), handball, rugby sevens and water polo. The alternates for these team sports are listed below and consisted of a further nine athletes (two in field hockey, four in soccer, one per rugby sevens and water polo teams). However, these nine do not officially count towards the team size.<ref>{{cite news|date=3 July 2021|title=Flexibility introduced for team rosters in several sports at Olympic Games Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympics.com/ioc/news/flexibility-introduced-for-team-rosters-in-several-sports-at-olympic-games-tokyo-2020|work=[[International Olympic Committee]]|location=Lausanne, Switzerland|access-date=4 July 2021|archive-date=3 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210703085437/https://olympics.com/ioc/news/flexibility-introduced-for-team-rosters-in-several-sports-at-olympic-games-tokyo-2020|url-status=live}}</ref> Canada's Olympic team contested all sports on the Olympic program except [[Handball at the 2020 Summer Olympics|handball]], [[Modern pentathlon at the 2020 Summer Olympics|modern pentathlon]] and [[Surfing at the 2020 Summer Olympics|surfing]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Calderone|first=Andrew|date=19 June 2021|title=Team Canada falls short of sending a surfer to the 2020 Tokyo Olympic Games|url=https://www.ubyssey.ca/sports/surf-canada-tokyo-olympic|work=[[The Ubyssey]]|location=Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada|access-date=19 June 2021|archive-date=20 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210620050120/https://www.ubyssey.ca/sports/surf-canada-tokyo-olympic/|url-status=live}}</ref> Alternates in soccer, rugby sevens and water polo competed, and are reflected in the [[#Competitors|table below]]. This increased the team size to 378. Two athletes in fencing and one in triathlon was added during the competition due to injury replacements. This meant the final team size was 381 athletes (148 men and 233 women).

The 24 medals won at the 2020 Summer Olympics mark the country's best-ever total medals result after the 1984 Games, surpassing the 22 medals won in [[Canada at the 1996 Summer Olympics|1996]] and [[Canada at the 2016 Summer Olympics|2016]], while also equalling the most number of gold medals won in [[Canada at the 1992 Summer Olympics|1992]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Lao|first=David|date=8 August 2021|title=Kelsey Mitchell wins gold for Canada in women's track cycling sprint|url=https://globalnews.ca/news/8095599/kelsey-mitchell-womens-cycling/|work=[[Global News]]|location=Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada|access-date=8 August 2021|archive-date=8 August 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210808031633/https://globalnews.ca/news/8095599/kelsey-mitchell-womens-cycling/|url-status=live}}</ref> At the [[1984 Summer Olympics]], which were [[1984 Summer Olympics boycott|boycotted]] by the [[Soviet Bloc]], Canada [[Canada at the 1984 Summer Olympics|won 44 medals]].

==Artistic swimming==
{{main|Artistic swimming at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Artistic swimming at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}
Canada is scheduled to enter a team of eight artistic swimmers to compete in the women's duet and team event. ==Badminton==
{{main|Badminton at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Badminton at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}
Canada entered eight badminton players (four per gender) for the following events based on the [[Badminton World Federation|BWF]] Race to Tokyo Rankings of 15 June 2021: one entry each in the men's and women's singles; and a pair each in the men's, women's, and mixed doubles. The eight qualified athletes represents the largest badminton team Canada has sent to the Olympics, and marks the first time since [[Badminton at the 2000 Summer Olympics|Sydney 2000]] the country will compete in all five events.<ref name=bda>{{cite web|last=Awad|first=Brandi|title=Team Canada to have its largest Olympic badminton team ever at Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/06/16/team-canada-to-have-its-largest-olympic-badminton-team-ever-at-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=16 June 2021|access-date=16 June 2021|archive-date=16 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210616140158/https://olympic.ca/2021/06/16/team-canada-to-have-its-largest-olympic-badminton-team-ever-at-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> The team was officially named on June 16, 2021.<ref name=bda/>

==Boxing==
{{main|Boxing at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Boxing at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}
Canada qualified five boxers, one man and four women. With the cancellation of the [[2021 Pan American Boxing Olympic Qualification Tournament|2021 Pan American Qualification Tournament]] in [[Buenos Aires]], [[Caroline Veyre]] (women's featherweight), [[Myriam Da Silva]] (women's welterweight), and [[Tammara Thibeault]] (women's middleweight) finished among the top three of their respective weight divisions in the IOC's Boxing Task Force Rankings for the Americas.<ref>{{cite news|first=Dan|last=Barnes|title=Qualifying for Tokyo Olympics an unprecedented challenge because of COVID-19|url=https://torontosun.com/sports/olympics/qualifying-for-tokyo-olympics-an-unprecedented-challenge-because-of-covid-19|publisher=[[Toronto Sun]]|date=27 May 2021|access-date=4 June 2021|archive-date=4 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210604072208/https://torontosun.com/sports/olympics/qualifying-for-tokyo-olympics-an-unprecedented-challenge-because-of-covid-19|url-status=live}}</ref> On June 9, 2021, [[Wyatt Sanford]] earned a spot on the team, as the highest ranked boxer from the Americas not already qualified.<ref>{{cite news|last=Palmeter|first=Paul|date=9 June 2021|title=Kennetcook boxer lands spot on Canada's Olympic team bound for Tokyo|url=https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/kennetcook-boxer-olympic-team-1.6059232|work=[[CBC News]]|location=Toronto, Ontario, Canada|access-date=9 June 2021|archive-date=9 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210609192207/https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/kennetcook-boxer-olympic-team-1.6059232|url-status=live}}</ref>

On June 30, 2021, [[Mandy Bujold]] won her appeal at the [[Court of Arbitration for Sport]] to compete at the Games. The court ruled that the qualification system must accommodate pregnant or postpartum women during the qualification period.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/boxing/canada-s-mandy-bujold-wins-fight-to-box-in-tokyo-olympics-1.6085623|title=Canada's Mandy Bujold wins battle to box in Tokyo Olympics|date=30 June 2021|website=www.cbc.ca/|publisher=[[CBC Sports]]|access-date=30 June 2021|archive-date=2 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210702025811/https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/boxing/canada-s-mandy-bujold-wins-fight-to-box-in-tokyo-olympics-1.6085623|url-status=live}}</ref> The full team of five athletes was officially named to the team on July 7, 2021.<ref>{{cite web|last=Awad|first=Brandi|title=Team Canada to have five boxers in the ring at Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/07/07/team-canada-to-have-five-boxers-in-the-ring-at-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=7 July 2021|access-date=7 July 2021|archive-date=7 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210707180202/https://olympic.ca/2021/07/07/team-canada-to-have-five-boxers-in-the-ring-at-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Cycling==

Canada qualified a total of 24 cyclists (11 men and 13 women), the largest cycling team the county has ever qualified for the games.<ref name=fcc>{{cite web|title=Team Canada Heads to Tokyo 2020 With Largest Cycling Team in Canadian Olympic History|url=https://www.cyclingcanada.ca/sport/general/news/team-canada-heads-to-tokyo2020-with-largest-cycling-team-in-canadian-olympic-history/|publisher=Cycling Canada|date=6 July 2021|access-date=6 July 2021|archive-date=6 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210706150839/https://www.cyclingcanada.ca/sport/general/news/team-canada-heads-to-tokyo2020-with-largest-cycling-team-in-canadian-olympic-history/|url-status=live}}</ref> This is broken down further into five in road, 13 in track, three in mountain biking and two in BMX racing.<ref name=fc>{{cite web|last=Awad|first=Brandi|title=Team Canada's Tokyo 2020 cycling squad completed with mountain bike and BMX athletes|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/07/06/team-canadas-tokyo-2020-cycling-squad-completed-with-mountain-bike-and-bmx-athletes/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=6 July 2021|access-date=6 July 2021|archive-date=18 August 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210818014148/https://olympic.ca/2021/07/06/team-canadas-tokyo-2020-cycling-squad-completed-with-mountain-bike-and-bmx-athletes/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|date=6 July 2021|title=Woods, four-time Olympian Pendrel headline Canada's cycling team for Tokyo Olympics|url=https://www.timescolonist.com/woods-four-time-olympian-pendrel-headline-canada-s-cycling-team-for-tokyo-olympics-1.24339156|work=[[Canadian Press]]|location=Toronto, Ontario, Canada|access-date=7 July 2021|archive-date=9 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210709184259/https://www.timescolonist.com/woods-four-time-olympian-pendrel-headline-canada-s-cycling-team-for-tokyo-olympics-1.24339156|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Road===
Canada entered a team of six road cyclists (three per gender). The men qualified three athletes by finishing 20th in the [[UCI World Ranking|UCI Nation Ranking]], while the women qualified two competitors, by finishing in eighth.<ref>{{cite news|title=Athletes' quotas for Road Cycling events at the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games|url=https://www.uci.org/inside-uci/press-releases/athletes--quotas-for-road-cycling-events-at-the-tokyo-2020-olympic-games|publisher=[[International Cycling Union|UCI]]|date=18 November 2019|access-date=23 November 2019|archive-date=25 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210725053151/https://www.uci.org/inside-uci/press-releases/athletes--quotas-for-road-cycling-events-at-the-tokyo-2020-olympic-games|url-status=live}}</ref>

On July 29, 2020, cyclists [[Hugo Houle]], [[Michael Woods (cyclist)|Michael Woods]], [[Karol-Ann Canuel]], and [[Leah Kirchmann]] were officially named to the Canadian roster for their second consecutive Games.<ref name=cycling>{{cite web|url=https://olympic.ca/2020/07/29/track-and-road-cyclists-nominated-to-team-canada-for-tokyo-2020/|title=The first athletes officially nominated to Team Canada for the Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games include 13 track cyclists and four road cyclists.|last=Csepio|first=Simone|date=29 July 2020|website=www.olympic.ca/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]] (COC)|access-date=29 July 2020|archive-date=19 December 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201219055339/https://olympic.ca/2020/07/29/track-and-road-cyclists-nominated-to-team-canada-for-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> The final cyclist named to the team was [[Guillaume Boivin]] on July 6, 2021.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://cyclingmagazine.ca/sections/news/cycling-canada-just-announced-the-third-member-of-the-tokyo-olympics-mens-road-squad/|title=Cycling Canada just announced the third member of the Tokyo Olympics men's road squad|last=Hansen-Gillis|first=Lily|date=6 July 2021|website=www.cyclingmagazine.ca/|publisher=Gripped Publishing Inc.|access-date=6 July 2021|archive-date=9 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210709190128/https://cyclingmagazine.ca/sections/news/cycling-canada-just-announced-the-third-member-of-the-tokyo-olympics-mens-road-squad/|url-status=live}}</ref> On July 13, 2021, it was announced that a third women's quota was reallocated to Canada. This quota was given to [[Alison Jackson (cyclist)|Allison Jackson]] for the road race event.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://twitter.com/CyclingCanada/status/1415108889146707968|title=We have an exciting last minute addition to our #TeamCanada road team!|date=13 July 2021|website=www.twitter.com/|publisher=[[Twitter]]|access-date=13 July 2021|archive-date=14 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210714004039/https://twitter.com/CyclingCanada/status/1415108889146707968|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://cyclingmagazine.ca/sections/news/alison-jackson-gets-last-minute-olympic-invite/|title=Alison Jackson gets last minute Olympic invite|last=Hansen-McKall|first=Terry|date=14 July 2021|website=www.cyclingmagazine.ca/|publisher=Gripped Publishing Inc.|access-date=16 July 2021|archive-date=11 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210711165853/https://cyclingmagazine.ca/sections/news/alison-jackson-gets-last-minute-olympic-invite/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Track===
Following the completion of the [[2020 UCI Track Cycling World Championships]], Canadian riders accumulated spots for both men and women in the team pursuit and madison, as well as the women's omnium, based on their country's results in the final UCI Olympic rankings. Canada also entered cyclists to compete each in the men's and women's sprint, as well as the men's and women's keirin, based on the final individual UCI Olympic rankings.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.uci.org/docs/default-source/official-documents/tokyo-2020---olympic-games/tokyo-2020-olympic-games---track-cycling---athletes-quota.pdf|title=Athletes Quota Places for Track Cycling At the 2020 Tokyo Olympic Games|date=1 March 2020|website=www.uci.org/|publisher=[[Union Cycliste Internationale]]|access-date=4 June 2021|archive-date=15 March 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200315050448/https://www.uci.org/docs/default-source/official-documents/tokyo-2020---olympic-games/tokyo-2020-olympic-games---track-cycling---athletes-quota.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref> Cycling Canada later decided to not enter a team in the women's madison, due to a lack of competition experience in the event, coupled with the fact it would take place before the omnium.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cyclingcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2021-05-05-Olympic-selection-criteria-WEB.pdf|title=Cycling Canada Amended XXXII Olympic Games Team Selection Policy|date=5 May 2021|website=www.cyclingcanada.ca/|publisher=Cycling Canada|access-date=4 June 2021|quote=Due to the risks inherent in the event, its place prior to the Omnium in the Olympic program and the lack of athletes with sufficient competition experience, Canada will not be entering the women’s Madison|archive-date=7 July 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210707064218/https://www.cyclingcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2021-05-05-Olympic-selection-criteria-WEB.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref>

The full Canadian track team of 13 cyclists (six men and seven women) was officially named on July 29, 2020, featuring Rio 2016 bronze medallists [[Allison Beveridge]], [[Jasmin Duehring]], and [[Georgia Simmerling]] in the women's team pursuit and [[Vincent De Haître]], a speed skater and two-time Winter Olympian slated to compete at his first summer Games.<ref name=cycling/>

==Equestrian==
{{main|Equestrian at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Equestrian at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}}
Canada qualified a team of six equestrians. Canadian equestrians qualified a full team in team dressage competition through the [[Equestrian at the 2019 Pan American Games|2019 Pan American Games]] in [[Lima]], Peru.<ref>{{cite news|first=Louise|last=Parkes|title=Pan American Games Lima 2019: It's Dressage team gold and a Tokyo ticket for Canada|url=https://inside.fei.org/content/pan-american-games-lima-2019-it%E2%80%99s-dressage-team-gold-and-tokyo-ticket-canada|publisher=[[International Federation for Equestrian Sports|FEI]]|date=30 July 2019|access-date=30 July 2019|archive-date=14 February 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210214033443/https://inside.fei.org/content/pan-american-games-lima-2019-it%E2%80%99s-dressage-team-gold-and-tokyo-ticket-canada|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|first=Catie|last=Staszak|title=Pan American Games Lima 2019: Brazil punches ticket to Tokyo with team gold|url=https://inside.fei.org/content/pan-american-games-lima-2019-brazil-punches-ticket-tokyo-team-gold|publisher=[[International Federation for Equestrian Sports|FEI]]|date=9 August 2019|access-date=10 August 2019|archive-date=9 August 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190809215841/https://inside.fei.org/content/pan-american-games-lima-2019-brazil-punches-ticket-tokyo-team-gold|url-status=live}}</ref> Meanwhile, two eventing spots were awarded to the Canadian equestrians based on the results in the individual FEI Olympic rankings for Group E (North America and Caribbean).<ref>{{cite news|title=Tokyo 2020 team and individual quota places confirmed by FEI|url=https://inside.fei.org/content/tokyo-2020-team-and-individual-quota-places-confirmed-fei|publisher=[[International Federation for Equestrian Sports|FEI]]|date=17 February 2020|access-date=1 March 2020|archive-date=7 August 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200807092526/https://inside.fei.org/content/tokyo-2020-team-and-individual-quota-places-confirmed-fei|url-status=live}}</ref>

On 17 December 2019, the show jumping team was officially dropped from the Games over a positive drug test by rider Nicole Walker for cocaine; hence, one individual jumping spot was awarded to the Canadian equestrians by securing the last of four available quotas at the Pan American Games.<ref>{{cite news|first=Andrew|last=Willis|title=Canadian equestrian show jumping team dropped from Tokyo Olympics; rider Nicole Walker appealing|url=https://www.theglobeandmail.com/sports/olympics/article-canadian-equestrian-show-jumping-team-dropped-from-tokyo-olympics/|work=[[The Globe and Mail]]|date=17 December 2019|access-date=27 December 2019|archive-date=19 December 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191219232722/https://www.theglobeandmail.com/sports/olympics/article-canadian-equestrian-show-jumping-team-dropped-from-tokyo-olympics/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The full team, consisted of two men and four women, was named on July 6, 2021.<ref>{{cite web|last=Awad|first=Brandi|title=Team Canada to have six riders and horses competing in equestrian at Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/07/06/team-canada-to-have-six-riders-and-horses-competing-in-equestrian-at-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=6 July 2021|access-date=6 July 2021|archive-date=18 August 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210818020815/https://olympic.ca/2021/07/06/team-canada-to-have-six-riders-and-horses-competing-in-equestrian-at-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref>

Canada's fencing team consisted of nine athletes (five men and four women), the largest delegation in the sport since [[Beijing 2008]].<ref name=fence>{{cite web|last=Awad|first=Brandi|title=Nine fencers en garde for Team Canada at Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/05/20/nine-fencers-en-garde-for-team-canada-at-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=20 May 2021|access-date=20 May 2021|archive-date=20 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210520140142/https://olympic.ca/2021/05/20/nine-fencers-en-garde-for-team-canada-at-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> For the first time since [[1988 Summer Olympics|Seoul 1988]], Canadian fencers qualified a full team each in the men's and women's team foil at the Games, as the highest-ranked nation from the Americas outside the world's top four in the FIE Olympic Team Rankings.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/fending-canada-olympic-foil-teams-qualify-1.5473291|title=Canada's fencing team is back en garde for the Olympics|publisher=[[CBC Sports]]|date=23 February 2020|access-date=25 February 2020|archive-date=28 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200228011504/https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/fending-canada-olympic-foil-teams-qualify-1.5473291|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Shaul Gordon]] (men's sabre) and [[Gabriella Page]] (women's sabre) secured additional spots on the Canadian team as one of the two highest-ranked fencers vying for qualification from the Americas region in their respective individual events of the FIE Adjusted Official Rankings. Meanwhile, Marc-Antoine Blais-Belanger completed the Canadian roster by winning the individual men's épée competition at the Pan American Zonal Qualifier in [[San José, Costa Rica]].<ref>{{cite news|title=Fencing Zonal Qualifying Event for Pan America Finishes in San Jose, Costa Rica|url=https://fie.org/articles/1069|publisher=[[International Fencing Federation]]|date=3 May 2021|access-date=10 May 2021|archive-date=10 May 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210510173941/https://fie.org/articles/1069|url-status=live}}</ref> The official team was named on May 20, 2021.<ref name=fence/> Both substitutes for the team events, Blake Broszus and Kelleigh Ryan came on in the team event, which increased the team size to eleven athletes (six men and five women).<ref>{{cite web|url=https://ici.radio-canada.ca/jeux-olympiques/nouvelle/1813377/escrime-canada-maximilien-van-haaster-alex-cai-blake-broszus|title=Les escrimeurs canadiens limités à un ===Women's tournament===
{{main|Football at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Women's tournament|Football at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Women's qualification}}
Canada women's football team qualified for the Olympics by reaching the finals of the [[2020 CONCACAF Women's Olympic Qualifying Championship]] in [[Carson, California]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Canada book ticket to Tokyo 2020|url=https://www.fifa.com/womensolympic/news/canada-book-ticket-to-tokyo-2020|publisher=[[FIFA]]|date=8 February 2020|access-date=8 February 2020|archive-date=8 February 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200208222547/https://www.fifa.com/womensolympic/news/canada-book-ticket-to-tokyo-2020|url-status=live}}</ref> The team went onto win the gold medal, marking the first women's [[team sport]] gold medal earned by the country ever, and the first overall since [[1908 Summer Olympics|1908]].<ref>{{cite web|first=Devin|last=Heroux|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/soccer/womens-soccer-canada-sweden-gold-medal-aug-6-1.6131828|title=Canadian women's soccer team delivers thrilling Olympic gold-medal victory over Sweden|date=6 August 2021|website=www.cbc.ca/|publisher=[[CBC Sports]]|access-date=6 August 2021|quote=It's only the fourth time in Canada's history at the Summer Games that a team has won gold – the first two times happened in 1904 at the St. Louis Olympics when the men's soccer and lacrosse teams won. The men's lacrosse team defended its gold medal and won in London in 1908.|archive-date=17 April 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220417100133/https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/soccer/womens-soccer-canada-sweden-gold-medal-aug-6-1.6131828|url-status=live}}</ref>

Canadian swimmers achieved qualifying standards in the following events (up to a maximum of 2 swimmers in each event at the Olympic Qualifying Time (OQT), and potentially 1 at the Olympic Selection Time (OST)):<ref>{{cite web|title=FINA – Olympic Games Tokyo 2020 Qualification|url=https://www.fina.org/competitions/5/olympic-games-tokyo-2020/qualifications?gender=M&distance=50&stroke=FREESTYLE&standard=all&regionId=all&countryId=|publisher=[[FINA]]|access-date=23 March 2019|archive-date=12 April 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210412101727/https://www.fina.org/competitions/5/olympic-games-tokyo-2020/qualifications?gender=M&distance=50&stroke=FREESTYLE&standard=all&regionId=all&countryId=|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Tokyo 2020 – FINA Swimming Qualification System|url=https://www.fina.org/sites/default/files/final_-_2020_07_15_-_tokyo_2020_-_revised_qualification_system_-_swimming_-_eng.pdf|work=[[2020 Summer Olympics|Tokyo 2020]]|publisher=[[FINA]]|access-date=6 March 2021|archive-date=30 July 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200730071900/https://www.fina.org/sites/default/files/final_-_2020_07_15_-_tokyo_2020_-_revised_qualification_system_-_swimming_-_eng.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref> To secure their nomination to the Olympic team, swimmers must have finished in the top two of each individual pool event under the FINA Olympic qualifying A standard at the Canadian Olympic Trials (19 to 23 June 2021) in [[Toronto]], Ontario. Swimmers not meeting the standard were named as relay only swimmers.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.swimming.ca/content/uploads/2019/03/Tokyo-2020-Olympic-Games-Pool-Swimming-INP-20-Mar2019-v.13-FINAL.pdf|title=Swimmer and Coach Nomination Criteria Tokyo 2020 Olympic Games – Pool Swimming|date=20 March 2019|website=www.swimming.ca/|publisher=[[Swimming Canada]]|access-date=2 September 2019|archive-date=2 September 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190902224255/https://www.swimming.ca/content/uploads/2019/03/Tokyo-2020-Olympic-Games-Pool-Swimming-INP-20-Mar2019-v.13-FINAL.pdf|url-status=live}}</ref>

At the 2020 Olympic Marathon Swim Qualifier in [[Setúbal]], Portugal, [[Kate Sanderson]] qualified for the games with a third place finish.<ref>{{cite web|last=Faria|first=Chris|title=Kate Sanderson qualifies for Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/06/19/kate-sanderson-qualifies-for-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=19 June 2021|access-date=19 June 2021|archive-date=19 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210619182646/https://olympic.ca/2021/06/19/kate-sanderson-qualifies-for-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> The next day, [[Hau-Li Fan]] qualified as the highest finisher from the Americas not yet qualified.<ref>{{cite web|last=Faria|first=Chris|title=Hau-Li Fan qualifies for Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/06/20/hau-li-fan-qualifies-for-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|date=20 June 2021|access-date=20 June 2021|archive-date=21 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210621173329/https://olympic.ca/2021/06/20/hau-li-fan-qualifies-for-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref>

On January 22, 2021, [[Swimming Canada]] nominated six swimmers to the Olympic team, including [[Penny Oleksiak]] (women's 200 m freestyle); and world champions [[Kylie Masse]] (women's 100 m backstroke) and [[Maggie MacNeil]] (women's 100 m butterfly).<ref>{{cite web|last=Sutherland|first=James|url=https://swimswam.com/six-swimmers-nominated-to-canadian-olympic-team-trials-moved-back-to-may/|title=Six Swimmers Nominated To Canadian Olympic Team; Trials Moved Back To May|date=22 January 2021|publisher=[[SwimSwam]]|access-date=23 January 2021|archive-date=22 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210122200042/https://swimswam.com/six-swimmers-nominated-to-canadian-olympic-team-trials-moved-back-to-may/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/01/22/six-swimmers-provisionally-nominated-to-team-canada-for-tokyo-2020/|title=Six swimmers provisionally nominated to Team Canada for Tokyo 2020|date=22 January 2021|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|access-date=23 January 2021|archive-date=22 January 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210122214726/https://olympic.ca/2021/01/22/six-swimmers-provisionally-nominated-to-team-canada-for-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> These swimmers were named based on their performances at the [[2019 World Aquatics Championships]] in [[Gwangju]], South Korea. The rest of the team was named on June 24, 2021.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/aquatics/canadian-olympic-swimming-team-announced-june-24-1.6078127|title=26 athletes nominated to Canada's Olympic swimming team|date=24 June 2021|website=www.cbc.ca/|publisher=[[CBC Sports]]|access-date=24 June 2021|archive-date=25 December 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20211225155655/https://www.cbc.ca/sports/olympics/summer/aquatics/canadian-olympic-swimming-team-announced-june-24-1.6078127|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.swimming.ca/en/news/2021/06/24/canadas-tokyo-2020-swimming-team-announced/|title=Canada's Tokyo 2020 Swimming Team Announced|date=24 June 2021|website=www.swimming.ca/|publisher=[[Swimming Canada]]|access-date=24 June 2021|archive-date=24 June 2021|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20210624141120/https://www.swimming.ca/en/news/2021/06/24/canadas-tokyo-2020-swimming-team-announced/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://olympic.ca/2021/06/24/team-canada-to-have-26-swimmers-at-tokyo-2020/|title=Team Canada to have 26 swimmers at Tokyo 2020|last=Nichols|first=Paula|date=24 June 2021|website=www.olympic.ca/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|access-date=24 June 2021|archive-date=22 February 2022|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20220222174839/https://olympic.ca/2021/06/24/team-canada-to-have-26-swimmers-at-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref>

==Table tennis==
{{main|Table tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Table tennis at the 2020 Summer Olympics – Qualification}} 
Canada qualified three athletes into the table tennis competition. [[Eugene Wang]] secured a men's spot for his third Olympics, with [[Zhang Mo (table tennis)|Zhang Mo]] going to her fourth in the women's side, by winning the singles competition at the 2020 ITTF North American Olympic Qualification Tournament in [[Kitchener, Ontario|Kitchener]], [[Ontario]], Canada. The duo also won the inaugural mixed doubles competition to qualify for that event.<ref>{{cite news|title=Together and apart, Eugene Wang and Zhang Mo book Tokyo places|url=https://www.ittf.com/2020/03/09/together-apart-eugene-wang-zhang-mo-book-tokyo-places/|publisher=[[International Table Tennis Federation|ITTF]]|date=10 March 2020|access-date=10 March 2020|archive-date=14 March 2020|archive-url=https://archive.today/20200314111231/https://www.ittf.com/2020/03/09/together-apart-eugene-wang-zhang-mo-book-tokyo-places/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|title=Mo Zhang and Eugene Wang qualify for Tokyo 2020|url=https://olympic.ca/2020/03/07/table-tennis-mo-zhang-and-eugene-wang-qualify-for-tokyo-2020/|publisher=[[Canadian Olympic Committee]]|last=Awad|first=Brandi|date=7 March 2020|access-date=8 March 2020|archive-date=21 March 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200321020839/https://olympic.ca/2020/03/07/table-tennis-mo-zhang-and-eugene-wang-qualify-for-tokyo-2020/|url-status=live}}</ref> Wang later gave up his singles spot in favour of [[Jeremy Hazin]] to focus on the mixed doubles.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.ittf.com/2020/05/28/present-start-canada-olympic-games/|title=Present from the start, once again Canada, Olympic Games bound|last1=Marshall|first1=Ian|last2=Nagpal|first2=Kabir|date=30 May 2020|website=www.ittf.com/|publisher=[[International Table Tennis Federation]]|access-date=29 November 2020|archive-date=23 September 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200923080521/https://www.ittf.com/2020/05/28/present-start-canada-olympic-games/|url-status=live}}</ref>

* PP – Decision by points – the loser with technical points.
* PO – Decision by points – the loser without technical points.
* ST – Great superiority – the loser without technical points and a margin of victory of at least 8 (Greco-Roman) or 10 (freestyle) points.
* SP – Technical superiority – the loser with technical points and a margin of victory of at least 8 (Greco-Roman) or 10 (freestyle) points.
