'''[[Ice hockey]]''' is a [[Olympic sports|sport]] that is contested at the '''[[Winter Olympic Games]]'''. A men's ice hockey tournament has been held every Winter Olympics (starting in [[1924 Winter Olympics|1924]]); an ice hockey tournament was also held at the [[1920 Summer Olympics]].<ref name="IOC">{{cite web|url=http://www.olympic.org/uk/sports/programme/index_uk.asp?SportCode=IH|title=Ice hockey|publisher=International Olympic Committee|access-date=17 February 2009|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090323022731/http://www.olympic.org/uk/sports/programme/index_uk.asp?SportCode=IH|archive-date=23 March 2009 }}</ref> From 1920 to [[1968 Winter Olympics|1968]], the Olympics also acted as the [[Ice Hockey World Championships]], and the two events occurred concurrently.<ref name="Timeline">{{cite news|title = International hockey timeline|publisher = [[International Ice Hockey Federation]]|url = http://www.iihf.com/iihf-home/history/the-iihf/timeline.html|access-date = 17 February 2009|archive-date = 14 July 2018|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20180714164454/http://www.iihf.com/iihf-home/history/the-iihf/timeline.html|url-status = live}}</ref> From 1920 until [[1984 Winter Olympics|1984]], only amateur athletes were allowed to compete in the tournament, and players from the [[National Hockey League]] (NHL) were not allowed to compete. events have been held at every Olympic Games since.<ref name="IOC"/>

In men's hockey, eight athletes have won four medals: Russians [[Vladislav Tretiak]] (three gold, one silver) and [[Igor Kravchuk]] (two gold, one silver, one bronze), Czech [[Jiří Holík]] (two silver, two bronze) and five players from Finland, each with one silver and three bronze: [[Teemu Selänne]], [[Kimmo Timonen]], [[Saku Koivu]], [[Jere Lehtinen]] and [[Ville Peltonen]]. Six have won three gold medals (all from Russia): Tretiak, [[Anatoli Firsov]], [[Viktor Kuzkin]], [[Andrei Khomutov]], [[Alexander Ragulin]] and [[Vitali Davydov]].<ref>{{cite press release|title=Factsheet: Records and medals at the Olympic Winter Games|publisher=International Olympic Committee|date=1 August 2008|url=http://multimedia.olympic.org/pdf/en_report_1136.pdf|access-date=17 February 2009|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20090304060426/http://multimedia.olympic.org/pdf/en_report_1136.pdf|archive-date=4 March 2009}}</ref>

In women's hockey, Canadians [[Jayna Hefford]], [[Hayley Wickenheiser]] (both four gold and one silver) and [[Marie-Philip Poulin]] (three gold and two silver), and American [[Hilary Knight]] (two gold and three silver) hold the record for total medals, with five each. Canadian teammate [[Caroline Ouellette]] also won four gold medals. Twelve other athletes have won four medals: seven Canadians and five Americans.

From 1920 to [[1952 Winter Olympics|1952]], teams from [[Canada at the Winter Olympics|Canada]] dominated the men's tournament, winning six gold and one silver medal. Canada's dominance was broken only by [[Great Britain at the Olympics|Great Britain]] in [[1936 Winter Olympics|1936]]. The [[Soviet Union at the Olympics|Soviet Union]] began competing at the Olympics in [[1956 Winter Olympics|1956]] and won nine straight Olympic medals, including seven gold. The USSR's dominance was only broken by the [[United States at the Winter Olympics|United States]] in [[1960 Winter Olympics|1960]] and [[1980 Winter Olympics|1980]]. The Soviet Union dissolved in 1991, and in [[1992 Winter Olympics|1992]], the [[Unified Team at the Olympics|Unified Team]] composed of former Soviet players won gold. Since then, the competition has been more even, with Canada winning three times, [[Sweden at the Olympics|Sweden]] twice and the [[Czech Republic at the Olympics|Czech Republic]], [[Russia at the Olympics|Russia]], [[Finland at the Olympics|Finland]] and the [[United States at the Olympics|United States]] once each. Teams from Canada have won the most medals, with sixteen, including nine gold. As of the [[2018 Winter Olympics]], 90 medals (30 of each color) have been awarded to teams from 14 [[National Olympic Committee]]s.
