'''Mireia Belmonte García''' (born 10 November 1990) is a Spanish Olympic, world, and European champion [[Swimming (sport)|swimmer]]. She is the former [[List of world records in swimming|world record]] holder in the [[Short course (swimming)|short course]] [[World record progression 200 metres butterfly|200 metre butterfly]], [[World record progression 400 metres individual medley|400 metre individual medley]], [[World record progression 400 metres freestyle|400 metre freestyle]], [[World record progression 800 metres freestyle|800 metre freestyle]], and [[World record progression 1500 metres freestyle|1500 metre freestyle]]. She was the first Spanish woman to win a [[Olympic medal|gold medal]] in swimming at an [[Olympic Games]] and is widely considered to be the greatest Spanish swimmer of all time.

At the [[Olympic Games|2016 Olympic Games]] in Rio de Janeiro she became [[List of Olympic medalists in swimming (women)|Olympic champion]] in the 200-metre [[Butterfly stroke|butterfly]], and also won the bronze medal in the 400-metre [[Medley swimming|individual medley]]. She previously competed at the [[2008 Summer Olympics|2008 Beijing Olympic Games]], where she debuted at 17 years old, and at [[2012 Summer Olympics|2012 London]], where she won two silver medals, one in the [[Swimming at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Women's 200 metre butterfly|200 metre butterfly]] with a time of 2:05.25 and one in the 800-metre [[Freestyle swimming|freestyle]] with a time of 8:18.76.

==Early years==
Mireia's parents, José Belmonte (from [[Freila]], Granada) and Paqui García (from [[Huelma]], Jaén), moved to Catalonia and the family has always lived in the La Salut quarter of [[Badalona]].

She was advised to swim at the age of 5 by her doctor. ''[https://www.cnbadalona.cat/ Club Natació Badalona]'' (in Catalan) was the swimming club where she started to compete. In 2003, she obtained a scholarship from [https://aquatics.cat/ Federación Catalana de Natación] (in Catalan) to train at the [http://www.csd.gob.es/csd/instalaciones/4CenAltRend/1CenAltRen/04card/03cardbarcelona/ Centro de Alto Rendimiento] (High Performance Center, CAR) in Sant Cugat del Vallès. Her coach was Jordi Murio when she was signed by [https://cnlh.es/ Club Natació L'Hospitalet] (in Catalan). After 6 years in this club, she was signed in the 2007/08 season by [https://www.nataciosabadell.com Club Natació Sabadell] (in Catalan). She continued training at the CAR with her new coach, Carles Subirana. In 2009 she left the CAR and moved to the Club Natación Sabadell to train with Michael Piper and later Fred Vernoux.<ref name="CNA11Aug16"/>

==Swimming career==
===2006 Youth World Championships===
At the [[2006 FINA Youth World Swimming Championships|2006 FINA Youth World Championships]], she was the [[FINA Youth World Swimming Championships|Junior World Champion]] in the 400-metre freestyle and in the 400-metre individual medley, and the [[European Junior Swimming Championships|Junior European Champion]] in the 200-metre freestyle and in the 400-metre individual medley.

===2007 European Championships===
The following year, training with Carles Subirana, she reached the second place in the 400-metre individual medley at the [[2007 European Short Course Swimming Championships]] with 4:31.06, just behind the [[Italy|Italian]] [[Alessia Filippi]]. She also qualified to compete at the 2008 Summer Olympics.

===2008===
====2008 European Aquatics Championships====
On 21 March, at the [[2008 European Aquatics Championships|2008 European Long Course Championships]] held in [[Eindhoven]], [[Netherlands]], Belmonte won the 200-metre individual medley breaking the [[List of European Championships records in swimming|Championships record]] with her time of 2:11.16. She also placed eighth in the 400-metre individual medley. Later in the year, at the [[2008 European Short Course Swimming Championships]] held in [[Rijeka]], [[Croatia]] in December, she won the 400 metre individual medley gold medal in a new world record time of [[World record progression 400 metres individual medley|4:25:06]]. The record was previously held by the American swimmer [[Julia Smit]], who set a time of 4:25.87 on 28 November 2008 in Toronto, Canada.

===2010 World Swimming Championships===
Although the season 2009–2010 was not very good for her, Belmonte recovered her best form and appeared strengthened when she arrived at the [[2010 FINA World Swimming Championships (25 m)|2010 FINA World Swimming Championships]], where she achieved a very remarkable performance, winning three gold medals: the 200-metre butterfly, the 200-metre medley and the 400-metre medley and a silver medal in the 800-metre freestyle, in which she was only surpassed by her teammate [[Érika Villaécija]].

===2013===
====2013 World Aquatics Championships====
After four months without training, she was presented as a member of the team of the ''Universidad Católica San Antonio de Murcia'' on 21 January, with a federative license for the ''UCAM Club Natación Fuensanta'' {{Clarify|reason=|date=June 2020}}. Thus, she could combine her studies in Business Management with her training in Sabadell (Barcelona) through a blended learning program.

That year, at the [[2013 World Aquatics Championships]] held in [[Barcelona]], Belmonte competes in six events during the eight days of competition. On 29 July, she won the bronze medal in the [[Swimming at the 2013 World Aquatics Championships – Women's 200 metre individual medley|200-metre individual medley]]. On 1 August, she won the silver medal in the [[Swimming at the 2013 World Aquatics Championships – Women's 200 metre butterfly|200-metre butterfly]]. On 4 August, the last day of the championships, she won her second silver medal, this time in the [[Swimming at the 2013 World Aquatics Championships – Women's 400 metre individual medley|400-metre individual medley]]. On 5 August, she was honored in her birthplace, Badalona, where he was received by hundreds of fans and decorated by the Mayor Xavier García Albiol in an institutional act.

====2013 Swimming World Cup====
At the [[2013 FINA Swimming World Cup]] held in [[Berlin]], [[Germany]] in August 2013 and conducted in short course metres, Belmonte set two world records in distance freestyle events. On 10 August, she broke the world record in the [[World record progression 800 metres freestyle|800-metre freestyle]], becoming the first woman to achieve a time under 8 minutes. Her time of 7:59.34 broke the former world record held by [[Camille Muffat]] by more than 1.5 seconds.<ref name="OT10Aug13">{{Cite web |title=FINA Swimming World Cup 2013 - Berlin - Women's 800 m freestyle |url=https://www.omegatiming.com/File/Download?id=00010D020601001802FFFFFFFFFFFF02 |website=omegatiming.com |access-date=12 June 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191210053826/https://www.omegatiming.com/File/Download?id=00010D020601001802FFFFFFFFFFFF02 |archive-date=10 December 2019 |url-status=live }}</ref> The next day, 11 August, she broke the world record for the [[World record progression 400 metres freestyle|400-metre freestyle]] with a time of 3:54.52, more than 3 tenths of a second faster than the previous world record set by Camille Muffat.<ref name="OT11Aug13">{{Cite web |title=FINA Swimming World Cup 2013 - Berlin - Women's 400 m freestyle |url=https://www.omegatiming.com/File/Download?id=00010D020601001702FFFFFFFFFFFF02 |website=omegatiming.com |access-date=12 June 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200306165859/https://www.omegatiming.com/File/Download?id=00010D020601001702FFFFFFFFFFFF02 |archive-date=6 March 2020 |url-status=live }}</ref>

On 3 December 2014 at the [[2014 FINA World Swimming Championships (25 m)|12th FINA World Swimming Championships]] in [[Doha]], [[Qatar]], Belmonte won two gold medals. One in the [[2014 FINA World Swimming Championships (25 m) – Women's 200 metre butterfly|200-metre butterfly]] and one in the [[2014 FINA World Swimming Championships (25 m) – Women's 400 metre individual medley|400-metre individual medley]].<ref>{{cite news |url=https://www.nytimes.com/2014/12/05/sports/two-golds-for-spanish-swimmer.html?_r=0 |title=Two Golds for Spanish Swimmer |date=4 December 2014 |newspaper=[[The New York Times]] |access-date=2 August 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170804214653/https://www.nytimes.com/2014/12/05/sports/two-golds-for-spanish-swimmer.html?_r=0 |archive-date=4 August 2017 |url-status=live }}</ref> At the same time she achieved the world record with substantial time improvements, making her the first female swimmer to go under 2 minutes in the 200-metre butterfly with her time of 1:59.61. Belmonte lowered the [[World record progression 200 metres butterfly|world record]] by 1.17 seconds and finished ahead of second-place finisher [[Katinka Hosszú]] by more than 1.5 seconds.<ref name="OTFWSC3Dec14f200fly">[https://www.omegatiming.com/File/00010E010D01032202FFFFFFFFFFFF02.pdf "12th FINA World Swimming Championships (25m) Doha (QAT): Women's 200m Butterfly Final Results"]. ''[[Omega Timing]]''. 3 December 2014. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref> Approximately 45 minutes later, she also set a new world record with her gold medal-winning time of 4:19.86 in the 400-metre individual medley, lowering the [[World record progression 400 metres individual medley|world record]] by 99 hundredths of a second and finishing over 3 seconds ahead of second-place finisher Katinka Hosszú.<ref name="OTFWSC3Dec14f400IM">[https://www.omegatiming.com/File/00010E010D01042502FFFFFFFFFFFF02.pdf "12th FINA World Swimming Championships (25m) Doha (QAT): Women's 400m Individual Medley Final Results"]. ''[[Omega Timing]]''. 3 December 2014. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref>

One day later, 4 December, Belmonte won her third gold medal, this time in the [[2014 FINA World Swimming Championships (25 m) – Women's 800 metre freestyle|800-metre freestyle]]. Her time of 8:03.41 in the final was a new [[List of World Championships records in swimming|Championship Record]] and a few seconds off her [[World record progression 800 metres freestyle|world record]] of 7:59.34 in the event.<ref name="OTFWSC4Dec14f800FR">[https://www.omegatiming.com/File/00010E010D01001802FFFFFFFFFFFF02.pdf "12th FINA World Swimming Championships (25m) Doha (QAT): Women's 800m Freestyle Final Results Summary"]. ''[[Omega Timing]]''. 4 December 2014. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref> The following day, she won her fourth gold medal. In [[Rio de Janeiro]], [[Brazil]] at the [[2016 Summer Olympics]] Belmonte won a bronze medal in the [[Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's 400 metre individual medley|400-metre individual medley]] on 6 August. Her bronze medal was the first medal for Spain in any sport at the 2016 Summer Olympics.<ref name="Romano7Aug16">Romano, Javier (7 August 2016). Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref> In the prelims heats of the [[Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's 400 metre freestyle|400-metre freestyle]] on 7 August, Belmonte ranked 15th with a time of 4:08.12 and did not advance to the final. The next day, in the [[Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's 200 metre individual medley|200-metre individual medley]] on 8 August, Belmonte ranked 15th in the prelims heats and qualified for the semifinals where she ranked 16th overall and did not advance to the final.<ref name="Mendes19Aug16">Mendes, Rodrigo (19 August 2016). Belmonte achieved a historic victory for Spain by winning the gold medal in the [[Swimming at the 2016 Summer Olympics – Women's 200 metre butterfly|200-metre butterfly]] on 10 August in a time of 2:04.85, which was 3 hundredths of a second ahead of the silver medalist, Australian swimmer [[Madeline Groves]].<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.sbnation.com/2016/8/10/12433334/olympics-2016-swimming-results-200m-butterfly-womens-gold-mireia-belmonte |title=Olympic swimming results 2016: Spain's Mireia Belmonte wins gold in women's 200m butterfly |date=10 August 2016 |publisher=[[SB Nation]] |access-date=10 August 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160812173209/http://www.sbnation.com/2016/8/10/12433334/olympics-2016-swimming-results-200m-butterfly-womens-gold-mireia-belmonte |archive-date=12 August 2016 |url-status=live }}</ref> She was the first Spanish female swimmer to become an Olympic champion.<ref name="Baldwin11Aug16">Baldwin, Alan (11 August 2016). [https://www.reuters.com/article/us-olympics-rio-swimming-idUSKCN10M0Q7 "Swimming: Night of individual firsts in Rio pool"]. ''[[Reuters]]''. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref><ref name="Arribas22Aug16">Arribas, Carlos (22 August 2016). ''[[El País]]''. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref> Her gold medal was the first gold medal in the swimming pool at an Olympic Games for a Spanish swimmer of any gender since 1992.<ref name="CNA11Aug16">[https://www.catalannews.com/sports/item/catalan-swimmer-mireia-belmonte-brings-first-gold-for-spain-at-rio-olympics "Catalan swimmer Mireia Belmonte brings first gold for Spain at Rio Olympics"]. Belmonte's two new [[Olympic medal]]s added to her previous two medals won at the 2012 Summer Olympics to amount to four total Olympic medals. Only three Spaniards before her had won four Olympic medals, making her the fourth to do so.<ref name="Marca11Aug16">[https://www.marca.com/en/olympic-games/2016/08/11/57acc7dd268e3e097c8b4661.html "Belmonte becomes fourth Spaniard to win four Olympic medals"]. ''[[Marca (newspaper)|Marca]]''. 11 August 2016. ==Awards and honours==
* In 2013, Belmonte received the Premio Reina Sofía, also known as Best Spanish Sportswoman, award of the annual set of [[Premios Nacionales del Deporte]] awarded to Spanish athletes.<ref name="EP23Oct14">[https://elpais.com/deportes/2014/10/23/actualidad/1414076484_423538.html "Gómez Noya y Mireia Belmonte, Premios Nacionales del Deporte"] (in Spanish). ''[[El País]]''. 23 October 2014. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref><ref name="RTVE4Dec14">[https://www.rtve.es/deportes/20141204/mireia-belmonte-gomez-noya-reciben-premios-como-mejores-deportistas-espanoles-2013/1061300.shtml "Premios Nacionales del Deporte: Mireia Belmonte y Gómez Noya, premiados como Mejores Deportistas españoles de 2013"] (in Spanish). ''[[RTVE]]''. 4 December 2014. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref> The award ceremony was held in [[Madrid]] at the [[Royal Palace of El Pardo]].<ref name="RTVE4Dec14"/>
* In 2017, the award for "Best Spanish Sportswoman in History" from newspaper ''[[Diario AS]]'' went to Belmonte for her accomplishments in the sport of swimming. The award was part of the newspaper's 50th Anniversary Awards gala that were held in celebration of the newspaper's 50th anniversary.<ref name="Martínez2Dec17">Martínez, Alberto (2 December 2017). [https://en.as.com/en/2017/12/02/other_sports/1512231112_590997.html "Mireia Belmonte: "I don't feel less popular for being a woman""]. ''[[Diario AS]]''. Retrieved 22 June 2021.</ref>
* On 1 January 2020, [[SwimSwam]] revealed they ranked Belmonte as #11 in the Top 20 Swimmers internationally for the 2010 to 2019 decade.<ref name="SS1Jan20">[https://swimswam.com/swimswams-top-20-swimmers-of-the-2010s/ "SwimSwam's Top 20 Swimmers of the 2010s"]. ''[[SwimSwam]]''. 1 January 2020. Retrieved 26 June 2021.</ref>
* For the 2021 year, [[SwimSwam]] ranked Belmonte as #73 on their list of Top 100 female swimmers for the year.<ref name="Ortegon10Feb2021">Ortegon, Karl (10 February 2021). [https://swimswam.com/swimswams-top-100-for-2021-womens-75-51/ "SwimSwam's Top 100 For 2021: Women's #75 — #51"]. ''[[SwimSwam]]''. Retrieved 11 October 2021.</ref>
