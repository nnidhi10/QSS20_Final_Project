The '''World Triathlon Championship Series''' is an annual series of [[triathlon]] events organised by [[World Triathlon]]. The series is used to crown an annual world champion since 2008.  Previously, the ITU (the former name of World Triathlon) world champion between 1989 and 2008 had been decided in a single annual championship race.

The Championship Series consists of multiple rounds of competitions culminating in a Grand Final race. Athletes compete head-to-head for points in these races that will determine the overall World Triathlon champion. The elite championship races are held, with one exception, over one of two distances, the standard or 'Olympic' distance (1.5 km swim, 40 km bike, 10 km run) which lasts between 1.5 and two hours, and the sprint distance, which is half of the standard distance and lasts around one hour (750 m swim, 20 km bike, 5 km run). 

Since 2018 a mixed relay series has been run in tandem, where national teams compete in mixed team relays for prize money and Olympic qualifying points.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web|url=https://media.triathlon.org/uploads/2018-WTS-Media-Guide.pdf|title=2018-WTS-Media-Guide|date=23 August 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180311022841/https://media.triathlon.org/uploads/2018-WTS-Media-Guide.pdf|archive-date=11 March 2018|url-status=live}}</ref> From these races, one is denominated as the [[World Triathlon Mixed Relay Championships]]. Relays typically consist of four 'super-sprint' legs.

Since 2021 the leg holding the Mixed Relay Championships has also included the reinstated [[World Triathlon Sprint Distance Championships|World Triathlon Sprint Championships]] under the combined branding of '''World Triathlon Sprint & Relay Championships'''. The stand-alone Sprint championship had previously been discontinued in 2011. Races in the sprint world championships are held over ''Super-sprint'' distances (300 m swim, 5 km bike, 2.5 km run) using a multi-race eliminator format which is unique to that event.

The final leg of the overall series is designated and marketed as the Grand Final, and has a greater points allocation, which must be included in the athletes final score ranking for the season along with a fixed number of best other results. In addition, the week of the Grand Final event sees other stand alone World Championship events held, including an elite under-23 event, single-race elite para-triathlon championship events, and a series of non-elite age-grade championships, similar to [[UCI Gran Fondo World Championships|Gran Fondo World Championships]] in road cycling and [[World Athletics Road Running Championships]].

==History==

With the establishment of the International Triathlon Union (ITU, now World Triathlon) in 1989 it was quickly established that the governing body should host a yearly world championship to establish the men's and women's world champion. With the creation and hosting of the first '''ITU Triathlon World Championship''' in 1989 the ITU had established itself and the sports premier event but the sport overall lacked cohesion with races of varying lengths and prize pools, which increased the difficulty for triathletes to train and plan for seasons ahead. So in 1991 the ITU created the '''ITU Triathlon World Cup''' a year long series of races all hosted by the ITU with regular distances  and prize money. With a world championship and a regular season established the ITU's  attention moved onto other issues including earning the sport a place at the Olympics.

Then in 2008 the day after the [[Triathlon at the 2008 Summer Olympics|2008 men's Olympic triathlon race]] the ITU announced starting next year it would be replacing the single race world championship with a six-race World Championship points super series culminating in a Grand Final, it was to be called the World Championship Series (WCS).<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.triathlon.org/news/article/itu_world_championship_series|title=ITU World Championship Series|last=Union|first=International Triathlon|date=2008-10-17|website=Triathlon.org|language=en|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref> The ITU believed it would help grow the sport and increase the reach to the level of major sports whilst gaining a bigger TV audience.<ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.slowtwitch.com/News/ITU_replaces_one-day_Elite_World_Championship_with_new_six-race_Super_Series__520.html/http://www.slowtwitch.com/News/ITU_replaces_one-day_Elite_World_Championship_with_new_six-race_Super_Series__520.html|title=ITU replaces one-day Elite World Championship with new six-race 'Super Series'|last=Slowtwitch.com|website=Slowtwitch.com|language=en|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref> Most athletes and professional coaches were happy at the announcement believing it would help the sport become more popular and increase professionalism and pay for the top level athletes. However, there were major monetary concerns one week after the announcement as the ITUs main sponsor [[BG Group|BG]] had pulled out of its nine-year sponsorship deal after only two years.<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.insidethegames.biz/articles/5672/bg-drop-triathlon-sponsorship|title=BG drop triathlon sponsorship|date=26 August 2009|website=www.insidethegames.biz|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.triathlon.org/news/article/the_bg_legacy|title=The BG Legacy|last=Union|first=International Triathlon|date=2008-12-20|website=Triathlon.org|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref>

By its start in 2009 the series had gained a title sponsor in Dextro Energy<ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.sportspromedia.com/news/dextro_energy_sign_up_with_triathlon|title=Dextro Energy sign up with triathlon - SportsPro Media|website=www.sportspromedia.com|date=10 February 2009 |access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=http://www.dextro-energy.com/brand-history|title=Brand history|website=Dextro Energy|language=en|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web|url=https://www.sportindustry.biz/news/dextro-sponsor-new-triathlon-series|title=Dextro to sponsor new triathlon series|date=2014-09-18|website=www.sportindustry.biz|language=en|access-date=2019-01-29}}</ref> in a $2 million deal allowing for each World Championship event to feature a $150,000 prize purse and for the Grand final to have  $250,000, this also meant that $700,000 was available at the end of the series. This influx of cash meant that athletes would be to earn almost triple what they had previously helping to draw more into the sport. In 2011 the [[ITU Sprint Distance Triathlon World Championships|sprint distance world championship]] was incorporated into the series giving the same points and prize money as any other event, from this point on sprint distance events would make up a part of the series.<ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.triathlete.com/2011/04/news/sprint-and-team-championships-added-to-the-2011-dextro-energy-triathlon-itu-world-championship-series_27331|title=Sprint And Team Championships Added To The 2011 Dextro Energy Triathlon ITU World Championship Series {{!}} Triathlete|date=2011-04-27|work=Triathlete|access-date=2018-08-23|language=en-US}}</ref> In 2012 Dextro Energy ended their title sponsorship in tandem with the series rebranding itself as the World Triathlon Series.<ref name="2013Guide">{{cite web|url=http://c1303192.r92.cf0.rackcdn.com/2013-WTS-SERIES-GUIDE.pdf|title=2013 Series Guide|publisher=International Triathlon Union|access-date=17 September 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131007164611/http://c1303192.r92.cf0.rackcdn.com/2013-WTS-SERIES-GUIDE.pdf|archive-date=7 October 2013|url-status=dead}}</ref> Then in 2013 the prize pool saw an increase to $2.25 million certifying the world triathlon series as the  richest series in triathlon.<ref name="2013Guide" /> In 2018 with the growing popularity of the [[World Triathlon Mixed Relay Championships]] and the disciplines' addition to the Olympic program <ref>{{Cite news|url=https://www.bbc.co.uk/sport/olympics/40226990|title=Tokyo 2020: Mixed-gender events added to Olympic Games|date=2017-06-09|work=BBC Sport|access-date=2018-08-23|language=en-GB}}</ref> it was decided that at three of the events on the 2018 calendar a mixed relay event would be held alongside the men's and women's competition; these three events would grant points towards Olympic qualification and constitute the new mixed relay series.<ref name=":0" />

Only two triathletes succeeded in winning World Championships under both formats, [[Francisco Javier Gómez Noya|Javier Gomez]] of Spain, and [[Helen Jenkins]] (née Tucker) of Great Britain and Wales. In 2020, in response to multiple race cancellations as a result of [[COVID-19 pandemic|COVID-19]] the Championship was once more decided on the basis of a single Championship race, won by [[Vincent Luis]] of France and [[Georgia Taylor-Brown]] of Great Britain and England. As Luis had already won a world title in 2019 under the now established season-long format, he in effect became only the third triathlete to win World Championships in both the single race and season-long formats.{{citation needed|date=August 2022}}

During the 2023 events, at least 57 participants fell ill after swimming off [[Roker|Roker Beach]] in [[Sunderland]]. An [[Environment Agency]] sample taken three days before the event indicated 3,900 ''[[E. coli]]'' colonies per 100ml, over 39 times higher than readings taken the previous month, but the results were not published until after the competition. [[Northumbrian Water]] reported that no discharges that would have affected water quality off Roker Beach were recorded since October 2021.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Ungoed-Thomas |first1=Jon |last2=Jenz |first2=Maximillian |title=Fifty-seven swimmers fall sick and get diarrhoea at world triathlon championship in Sunderland |url=https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2023/aug/05/investigation-after-57-world-triathlon-championship-swimmers-fall-sick-and-get-diarrhoea-in-sunderland-race |access-date=26 August 2023 |date=5 Aug 2023}}</ref>

==Disciplines==
Currently there are three different distance disciplines:
*Standard- A 1500m swim followed by a 40&nbsp;km cycle followed by a 10&nbsp;km run.
*Sprint- A 750m swim followed by a 20&nbsp;km cycle followed by a 5&nbsp;km run.
*Mixed Team Relay- A 4 x (300m swim followed by a 7.5&nbsp;km cycle followed by a 1.5&nbsp;km run) where each athlete completes the swim bike run before tagging the next athlete,  with the order of the athletes always being female, male, female, male.

In all instances the swim will be a mass start in [[Open water swimming|open-water]] and the cycling will be  [[Drafting (aerodynamics)|draft]]-legal. There is an allowed leniency of 10% on each segment of courses route for the standard and sprint distances, with more discretion being allowed for the mixed relay. The standard distance was also known as the Olympic distance as it was the only distance competed for in the Olympics, however the World Triathlon has tried to enforce the use of the name standard distance saving the name Olympic on for official Olympic events.{{citation needed|date=September 2021}}
