The '''men's 4 × 100 meter medley relay''' took place on 20–21 August at the [[Athens Olympic Aquatic Centre|Olympic Aquatic Centre]] of the [[Athens Olympic Sports Complex]] in [[Athens]], [[Greece]].<ref name="Swimming schedule">{{cite web |url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/swimming/schedule/3534120.stm |title=Swimming schedule |publisher=[[BBC Sport]] |date=5 August 2004|access-date=17 June 2007}}</ref>

The U.S. team added two new world records to the books in the final men's event of the Olympic swimming program. [[Aaron Peirsol]], [[Brendan Hansen]], [[Ian Crocker]], and [[Jason Lezak]] lowered their time set at the [[Swimming at the 2003 World Aquatics Championships|2003 FINA World Championships]] in [[Barcelona, Spain]], stopping the clock in 3:30.68. At the opening of the race, Peirsol led off a backstroke leg with a new world record of 53.45, beating a 0.15-second mark set by [[Lenny Krayzelburg]] (53.60) from the Pan Pacific Championships in 1999.<ref name=400mmr>{{cite news|last=Thomas|first=Stephen|title=Two World Records to the USA in One Race: American Men Set New 400m Medley Standard, Aaron Peirsol Lowers the 100 Backstroke Mark|url=http://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/lane9/news/7941.asp|publisher=[[Swimming World Magazine]]|date=21 August 2004|access-date=10 May 2013|url-status=dead|archive-url=https://archive.today/20130630094342/http://www.swimmingworldmagazine.com/lane9/news/7941.asp|archive-date=30 June 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=Michaelis|first=Vicky|title=Medley relay world record hands Phelps his eighth medal|url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/sports/olympics/athens/swimming/2004-08-21-mens-roundup_x.htm|publisher=[[USA Today]]|date=21 August 2004|access-date=10 May 2013}}</ref>

Meanwhile, the Germans earned a silver medal in a European record of 3:33.62, 11-hundredths of a second under the old Olympic record set by Team USA in 2000. Japan finished third in 3:35.22 to hold off the strong Russian team anchored by double Olympic champion [[Alexander Popov (swimmer)|Alexander Popov]], who made up more than 1.5 seconds, but fell short of a medal in his last Olympic final.<ref name=400mmr/><ref>{{cite news|title=USA win 4×100m swim relay|url=http://news.bbc.co.uk/sport2/hi/olympics_2004/swimming/3586784.stm|publisher=[[BBC Sport]]|date=21 August 2004|access-date=10 May 2013}}</ref>
