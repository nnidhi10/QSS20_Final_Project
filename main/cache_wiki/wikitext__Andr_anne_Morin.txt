'''Andréanne Morin''' (born August 9, 1981) is a Canadian [[rowing (sport)|rower]] and Olympian.

==Career==
Morin was an integral part of Canada's women's eights rowing team. She won medals at three world championships, a bronze medal at the [[2003 World Rowing Championships]]<ref name=row /> and silver at the [[2010 World Rowing Championships]]<ref name=row /> and [[2011 World Rowing Championships]].<ref name=row>{{cite web|title=Andréanne Morin {{!}} Rowing Canada|url=http://rowingcanada.org/national-team/athletes/heavyweight-women/2012/andreanne-morin|website=rowingcanada.org|accessdate=June 1, 2017|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171222051502/http://rowingcanada.org/national-team/athletes/heavyweight-women/2012/andreanne-morin|archive-date=December 22, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref> She was the [[NCAA]] champion in rowing 2006.<ref name=row />

Morin was a three time Olympian finishing seventh in the [[2004 Summer Olympics]] and in fourth place at the [[2008 Summer Olympics|2008 Summer Olympic Games]] in [[Beijing]], China.<ref name=oly>{{cite web|title=Andréanne Morin|url=http://olympic.ca/team-canada/andreanne-morin/|website=Official Canadian Olympic Team Website {{!}} Team Canada {{!}} 2016 Olympic Games|accessdate=June 1, 2017|date=October 5, 2015}}</ref> At the [[2012 Summer Olympics]] she was part the Canadian women's eight that won the silver medal.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.olympic.org/olympic-results/london-2012/rowing/eight-with-coxswain-8-w |title=London 2012 eight with coxswain 8 women – Olympic Rowing |website=Olympic.org |date= |accessdate=September 10, 2016}}</ref>

===Other work===
Morin was a member of the Athlete Committee at the [[World Anti Doping Agency]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Andréanne Morin selected to WADA Athlete Committee {{!}} Rowing Canada|url=http://rowingcanada.org/news-events/news/andreanne-morin-selected-wada-athlete-committee|website=rowingcanada.org|accessdate=June 1, 2017|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171222053218/http://rowingcanada.org/news-events/news/andreanne-morin-selected-wada-athlete-committee|archive-date=December 22, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Education==
She attended [[The Study]], graduating in grade 11 in 1998, and later attended [[Phillips Exeter Academy]], graduating in 2000, followed by a bachelor's degree in political science at [[Princeton University]] in 2006.<ref name=oly /><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.peasummertimes.com/2016/07/that-guy-from-facebook-yeah-he-went-here/ |title=That Guy From Facebook? Yeah, He Went Here |website=peasummertimes.com|date=July 21, 2016 }}</ref> She also later studied law at [[Université de Montréal]].<ref>{{cite web|title=Alumnae – (Class of '98) Andréanne Morin – Olympic Silver Medal Winner {{!}} The Study|url=http://www.thestudy.qc.ca/alumnae/profiles/class-of-98-andreanne-morin-olympic-athlete|website=www.thestudy.qc.ca|accessdate=June 1, 2017|language=en|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171222052759/http://www.thestudy.qc.ca/alumnae/profiles/class-of-98-andreanne-morin-olympic-athlete|archive-date=December 22, 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref>

==Honours==
In 2012 Morin was awarded the [[Queen Elizabeth II Diamond Jubilee Medal]].<ref>{{cite web|title=London 2012 Olympians, Paralympians and builders honoured with Diamond Jubilee Medal in Montreal|url=http://olympic.ca/press/london-2012-olympians-paralympians-and-builders-honoured-with-diamond-jubilee-medal-in-montreal-2/|website=Official Canadian Olympic Team Website {{!}} Team Canada {{!}} 2016 Olympic Games|accessdate=June 1, 2017|date=March 1, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160313091634/http://olympic.ca/press/london-2012-olympians-paralympians-and-builders-honoured-with-diamond-jubilee-medal-in-montreal-2/|archive-date=March 13, 2016|url-status=dead|df=mdy-all}}</ref>

In 2019, an eponymous shell in her name, the A. Morin 2000, was christened and added to the Phillips Exeter Academy boathouse.
