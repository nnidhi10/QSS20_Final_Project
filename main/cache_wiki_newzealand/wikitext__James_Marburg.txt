'''James Marburg''' (born 27 December 1982) is an Australian retired rower. He is a dual Olympian, Olympic silver medallist and has represented Australia at five World Rowing Championships.

==Education==
Marburg attended St Patrick's College, Ballarat. He undertook undergraduate studies at [[Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology]] and legal studies at [[University of Melbourne]].

==Club and national rowing==
Marburg's senior rowing was done from the Melbourne University Boat Club.

He was selected in the Victorian state representative eights who raced for the King's Cup at the [[Australian Rowing Championships]] on eight consecutive occasions from 2005 to 2012. Those Victorian crews saw King's Cup victories in 2006 and 2007. In 2006 he teamed up with [[Cameron McKenzie-McHarg]] to win the Australian national [[coxless pair]] championship beating out the fancied veteran duo of [[Drew Ginn]] and [[Duncan Free]].

==International representative rowing==
He gained selection in Australian VIIIs who competed at the [[2006 World Rowing Championships|2006]] and [[2007 World Rowing Championships|2007 World Championships]]<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2007-Munich.php |title=2007 World Championships at Guerin Foster |access-date=7 April 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180612140525/http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2007-Munich.php |archive-date=12 June 2018 |url-status=dead}}</ref> and considered that he was in contention for the Beijing Olympics.

In 2008 Marburg was selected in the men's coxless four, with [[Francis Hegerty]], [[Matt Ryan (rower)|Matt Ryan]] and [[Cameron McKenzie-McHarg]].  The crew won their event at the 2008 World Rowing Cup I in [[Lucerne]], [[Switzerland]]. The crew then turned their attention to the Olympic Qualification Regatta in Poznan. Ryan was struck down by illness and had to be replaced for the race by Terrence Alfred and the crew achieved qualification for the Olympic Games.<ref>[http://corporate.olympics.com.au/athlete/james-marburg</Marburg's Olympic career]</ref> In Beijing, the crew led for a large part of the race before placing second behind the race favourites from Great Britain to take the silver medal.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/olympic-games/2008-Beijing.php |title=Olympic Games – Australian Rowing History |website=rowinghistory-aus.info |access-date=6 April 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180427025253/http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/olympic-games/2008-Beijing.php |archive-date=27 April 2018 |url-status=dead}}</ref>

Marburg was selected in an unchanged men's coxless four for the [[2009 World Rowing Championships]] in [[Poznań|Poznan]], [[Poland]] and won a silver medal.<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2009-Poznan.php |title=World Championships – Australian Rowing History |website=rowinghistory-aus.info |access-date=6 April 2016 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180618052906/http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2009-Poznan.php |archive-date=18 June 2018 |url-status=dead}}</ref> The following year he moved into the [[Eight (rowing)|VIII]] which won bronze at the [[Lake Karapiro]], [[New Zealand]] at the [[2010 World Rowing Championships|2010 World Championships]].<ref>{{Cite web |url=http://www.rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2010%20-%20Lake%20Karapiro.php |title=Australian Rowing Team at the 2010 World Rowing Championships at Lake Karapiro, New Zealand |accessdate=19 November 2017 |website=Rowinghistory-aus.info |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170422094819/http://rowinghistory-aus.info/world-championships/2010%20-%20Lake%20Karapiro.php |archive-date=22 April 2017 |url-status=dead}}</ref> In 2011 he was again selected into the [[Eight (rowing)|VIII]] which placed fourth at the [[2011 World Rowing Championships|2011 World Championships]] in [[Bled]], [[Slovenia]].

For the [[2012 Summer Olympics]], Marburg was selected into the men's pair with team-mate [[Brodie Buckland]]. The pair reached the final of the men's pair, placing fifth.<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.olympic.org/olympic-results/london-2012/rowing/coxless-pair-2-m |title=London 2012 pair without coxswain 2 men - Olympic Rowing |date=3 June 2017 |website=Olympic.org |accessdate=28 November 2017}}</ref> Following the London games he retired from rowing.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.thecourier.com.au/story/986016/olympic-rowing-medallist-marburg-announces-retirement/ | title=Olympic rowing medallist Marburg announces retirement | date=12 November 2012 }}</ref>

==Professional career==
Marburg began his legal career as a solicitor at [[King & Wood Mallesons]] Melbourne in 2014. Since 2020 he has been a senior associate at [[Clayton Utz]] Melbourne.
