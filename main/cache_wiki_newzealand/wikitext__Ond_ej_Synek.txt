'''Ondřej Synek''' ({{IPA|cs|ˈondr̝ɛj ˈsɪnɛk}}; born 13 October 1982 in [[Stará Boleslav]]) is a Czech former [[Rowing (sport)|rower]]. He is a five-time World Champion in Single Sculls, winning in [[2010 World Rowing Championships|2010]], [[2013 World Rowing Championships|2013]], [[2014 World Rowing Championships|2014]], [[2015 World Rowing Championships|2015]] and [[2017 World Rowing Championships|2017]]. He won Olympic silver medals at the [[2008 Summer Olympics]] in Beijing and [[2012 Summer Olympics]] in London.<ref>{{cite Sports-Reference |url=https://www.sports-reference.com/olympics/athletes/sy/ondrej-synek-1.html |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200417235709/https://www.sports-reference.com/olympics/athletes/sy/ondrej-synek-1.html |url-status=dead |archive-date=2020-04-17 |title=Ondrej Synek}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.london2012.com/rowing/event/men-single-sculls/phase=rom012100/index.html |title=London 2012 single sculls 1x men – Olympic Rowing |website=London2012.com |access-date=29 August 2016 |archive-date=6 December 2012 |archive-url=https://archive.today/20121206013257/http://www.london2012.com/rowing/event/men-single-sculls/phase=rom012100/index.html |url-status=dead }}</ref>  In Beijing he came in as the chief rival of [[Mahé Drysdale]], but despite Drysdale coming down with a painful stomach flu and falling in the final stages of the final to bronze, he was upset for the gold by defending Olympic Champion [[Olaf Tufte]], who Synek had regularly been beating the previous couple years. In London he was jointly favored for gold with Drysdale after trading gold and silver at the previous two World Championships, but fell short to Drysdale in the final.

In Rio after having won 3 straight World Championships since London, he came in as the clear favorite and had a stated desire to badly want the Olympic Gold. However, he had narrowly missed in both Beijing and London.<ref>{{cite web |author=Matilda Bywater |url=http://rowglobal.com/2016/05/12/third-time-lucky-ondrej-synek-goes-for-olympic-gold/ |title=Third Time Lucky? Ondrej Synek goes for Olympic Gold. |website=RowGlobal.com |access-date=29 August 2016}}</ref> His quest for Olympic gold failed for a third consecutive time in Rio as he took bronze in the single sculls behind both Drysdale and the surprising [[Damir Martin]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://isport.blesk.cz/clanek/dalsi-sporty-olympijske-hry-loh-rio-2016/277389/skifar-synek-ma-bronz-vyhral-drysdale-po-tesnem-finisi.html |title=Rio ONLINE: Ondřej Synek má bronz ve skifu &#124; iSport.cz |website=Isport.blesk.cz |access-date=29 August 2016}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.o2sport.cz/ostatni-sporty/dalsi-medaile-veslar-ondrej-synek-pridal-bronz-o-zlatu-nevidane-rozhodovaly-centimetry/ |title=Další medaile! Veslař Ondřej Synek přidal BRONZ! O zlatu nevídaně rozhodovaly centimetry |website=O2sport.cz |date=13 August 2016 |access-date=29 August 2016}}</ref> As a five-time World Champion in single sculls, and with a total of 13 World and Olympic single scull medals, he is by far the most successful single sculler in history to not win an Olympic Gold. He confirmed after his Rio defeat to want to take a likely final attempt at the elusive Olympic gold medal in [[Rowing at the 2020 Summer Olympics|Tokyo 2020]],<ref>{{cite news | title= Mahe Drysdale's rowing rival Ondrej Synek plans to contest 2020 Tokyo Olympics | url=http://www.stuff.co.nz/auckland/local-news/national-news/84005910/Mahe-Drysdales-rowing-rival-Ondrej-Synek-plans-to-contest-2020-Tokyo-Olympics | work=[[Auckland Now]] | first=Ian | last=Anderson | date= 7 September 2016 | access-date=12 November 2017}}</ref> but in June 2021 he announced on his Facebook page that he will not be going to Tokyo.<ref>{{cite web |title=Ondřej Synek |url=https://www.facebook.com/OSynek |website=www.facebook.com |access-date=14 June 2021 |language=en}}</ref> In September 2021 Synek announced his retirement.<ref>{{cite news | title= Veslař Synek ukončil kariéru: Chtělo se mi brečet, ale jsem velkej kluk | url=https://isport.blesk.cz/clanek/ostatni-vodni-sporty-veslovani/402543/veslar-synek-ukoncil-karieru-chtelo-se-mi-brecet-ale-jsem-velkej-kluk.html | work=CNC | first=Martin | last=Hašek | date= 23 September 2021 | access-date=23 September 2021}}</ref>
