'''Isadora''' "'''Izzy'''" '''Cerullo''' (born 24 March 1991) is a [[Brazilian Americans|Brazilian-American]] [[rugby sevens]] player.

==Early life and education==
Cerullo was raised in [[Raleigh, North Carolina]]. Her parents immigrated to the United States from [[Brazil]]  in the last years of the country's [[Brazilian military government|military dictatorship]] which lasted from 1964 to 1985.<ref>{{cite news |author=Stephanie Nolen |date=August 4, 2016 |url-access=subscription |url=https://www.theglobeandmail.com/sports/olympics/brazil-recruited-athletes-from-around-the-world-to-play-in-the-olympics/article31286372/ |title=Brazil recruited athletes from around the world to play in the Olympics |publisher=The Globe and Mail |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160811144555/http://www.theglobeandmail.com/sports/olympics/brazil-recruited-athletes-from-around-the-world-to-play-in-the-olympics/article31286372/ |archive-date=2016-08-11 |url-status=live}}</ref> She is one of four children and a triplet, with two brothers the same age and one older brother. She is a dual citizen of the United States and Brazil.<ref>{{cite web |agency=Associated Press |url=http://olympics.nbcsports.com/tag/isadora-cerullo/ |title=Isadora Cerullo – OlympicTalk |publisher=NBC Sports |date=2016-07-23 |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160822054418/http://olympics.nbcsports.com/tag/isadora-cerullo/ |archive-date=2016-08-22 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Meri-Jo Borzilleri |url=http://americansportsnet.com/road-to-rio-belmonts-brian-baker-hopes-next-week-starts-olympic-medal-epoch/ |title=ROAD TO RIO &#124; Belmont's Brian Baker hopes next week starts Olympic medal epoch  |publisher=American Sports Network |date=2016-07-26 |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20161011161738/http://americansportsnet.com/road-to-rio-belmonts-brian-baker-hopes-next-week-starts-olympic-medal-epoch/ |archive-date=2016-10-11 |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Tales Azzoni |agency= The Associated Press |url=http://www.providencejournal.com/sports/20160723/foreign-athletes-use-brazilian-connections-to-make-it-to-rio |title=Foreign athletes use Brazilian connections to make it to Rio |date=Jul 23, 2016 |publisher=providencejournal.com |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160818072916/http://www.providencejournal.com/sports/20160723/foreign-athletes-use-brazilian-connections-to-make-it-to-rio |archive-date=2016-08-18 |url-status=live}}</ref> Cerullo graduated in 2009 from [[William G. Enloe High School]], where she was a member of the varsity soccer and cross-country teams. She went on to attend [[Columbia University]] to study medicine and was a member of the rugby team and a writer for the [[Columbia Daily Spectator]], graduating in 2013.<ref>{{cite web |last=Lokey |first=Anna |url=http://columbiaspectator.com/spectrum/2016/08/09/former-columbia-rugby-proposed-rio-olympics |title=Former Columbia Rugby player is proposed to at the Rio Olympics  |publisher=Columbia Daily Spectator |date=Aug 9, 2016 |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160811062520/http://columbiaspectator.com/spectrum/2016/08/09/former-columbia-rugby-proposed-rio-olympics |archive-date=2016-08-11 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://columbiaspectator.com/contributors/isadora-cerullo |title=Isadora Cerullo  |publisher=Columbia Daily Spectator |access-date=2016-08-11 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160919100620/http://columbiaspectator.com/contributors/isadora-cerullo |archive-date=2016-09-19 |url-status=dead }}</ref> While a student at Columbia, she worked as an [[emergency medical technician]].

==Career==
Cerullo was recruited to play internationally while a member of Philadelphia Women's Rugby Football Club.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://sports.yahoo.com/news/olympics-2016-how-an-american-turned-down-columbias-med-school-to-play-rugby-for-brazil-180045766.html |first1=Jeff |last1=Eisenberg |date=Aug 7, 2016 |website=Yahoo Sports |title=Olympics 2016: How an American turned down Columbia's med school to play rugby for Brazil |access-date=2016-08-10 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160810073441/http://sports.yahoo.com/news/olympics-2016-how-an-american-turned-down-columbias-med-school-to-play-rugby-for-brazil-180045766.html |archive-date=2016-08-10 |url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.pwrfc.org/alumnae.html |title= Alumnae |website=Philadelphia Women's Rugby Football Club |access-date=2016-08-10 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160216142542/http://www.pwrfc.org/alumnae.html |archive-date=2016-02-16 |url-status=dead}}</ref> Cerullo moved to [[São Paulo]] to play rugby professionally. She won a bronze medal at the [[Rugby sevens at the 2015 Pan American Games – Women's tournament|2015 Pan American Games]] as a member of the [[Brazil women's national rugby sevens team]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://results.toronto2015.org/IRS/en/rugby-7/athlete-profile-n10158950-cerullo-isadora.htm |title=Isadora CERULLO |publisher=[[2015 Pan American Games]] |access-date=2016-07-17 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170510115820/http://results.toronto2015.org/IRS/en/rugby-7/athlete-profile-n10158950-cerullo-isadora.htm |archive-date=2017-05-10 |url-status=dead}}</ref> She was selected for the  [[Brazil women's national rugby sevens team|Brazil women's rugby sevens team]] to compete in the [[2016 Summer Olympics]] in [[Rio de Janeiro]].<ref>{{cite web |url=http://www.worldrugby.org/womens-sevens-series/news/174251 |title=Rio 2016 hosts Brazil name Olympic squads |website=World Rugby |language=en-GB |access-date=2016-07-19 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160817092547/http://www.worldrugby.org/womens-sevens-series/news/174251 |archive-date=2016-08-17 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Brazil's women's rugby team placed ninth at the 2016 Olympics.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Rio 2016 rugby-7 women Results |url=https://olympics.com/en/olympic-games/rio-2016/results/rugby/rugby-7-women |access-date=2023-05-30 |website=olympics.com}}</ref>
