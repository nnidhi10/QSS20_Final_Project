'''Sport in Pakistan''' is a significant part of [[Culture of Pakistan|Pakistani culture]]. [[Cricket]] is the most popular sport in [[Pakistan]].<ref name="CW">{{cite book |last=Abbott |first=David |title=Changing World – Pakistan |date=2015-01-01 |publisher=Encyclopædia Britannica |isbn=978-1-62513-365-6 |editor-last=McCann |editor-first=Jacqueline |pages=100–110 |chapter=Chapter 3: Social Changes |chapter-url=https://books.google.com/books?id=0FZzCQAAQBAJ&q=cricket&pg=PT39 |orig-year=1st pub. 2010}}</ref> [[Association football|Football]] has also gained popularity in recent years, and is the second most popular sport in the country.<ref name="VD">{{Cite web |last=Hussain |first=Abid |title=A 'very different' Pakistan? National football team aim to end dire record |url=https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2023/10/11/pakistan-national-football-team-2026-world-cup-qualifier-cambodia |access-date=2024-02-29 |website=Al Jazeera |language=en}}</ref><ref name="FP">{{Cite web |title='Football played more than cricket in Pakistan in last five years' |url=https://www.geosuper.tv/latest/32411-football-played-more-than-cricket-in-pakistan-in-last-five-years |access-date=2024-02-29 |website=www.geosuper.tv |language=en-US}}</ref> [[Field hockey]] is the [[national sport]], and was popular for several decades, with some of Pakistan's greatest sporting accomplishments having taken place in this sport, along with [[Squash (sport)|squash]].<ref>{{Cite web |title=Golden period Of Pakistan sports {{!}} Sports {{!}} thenews.com.pk |url=https://www.thenews.com.pk/tns/detail/559897-golden-period-pakistan-sports |access-date=2024-08-11 |website=www.thenews.com.pk |language=en}}</ref> [[Polo]] and [[Traditional games of Pakistan|traditional sports]] like [[kabaddi]], [[Tent pegging]] and other well-known games are also played.<ref name="CW" />

In cricket, the country has had an array of success over the years, and has the distinct achievement of having won each of the major [[International Cricket Council|ICC]] international cricket tournaments: [[Cricket World Cup|ICC Cricket World Cup]], [[Men's T20 World Cup|ICC World Twenty20]], and [[ICC Champions Trophy]];<ref>{{cite web |last=Narayanan |first=Deepu |date=19 June 2017 |title=CT17 final stats: Pakistan become fourth team to win all three ICC crowns |url=https://www.cricbuzz.com/cricket-news/95302/ct17-final-stats-pakistan-become-fourth-team-to-win-all-three-icc-crowns |access-date=2022-08-21 |website=[[Cricbuzz]]}}</ref> as well as the [[ICC Men's Test Team Rankings|ICC Test Championship]].<ref>{{cite web |date=21 September 2016 |title=David Richardson presents ICC Test Championship mace to Misbah-ul-Haq |url=https://www.icc-cricket.com/news/183562 |access-date=2022-08-21 |website=[[International Cricket Council]]}}</ref> The [[Pakistan Super League]] ranks among the top [[List of Twenty20 cricket competitions|T20 leagues]] globally.<ref>{{cite web |date=13 February 2019 |title=PSL's brand value stands at $230m or Rs 32.258 bn |url=https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/431453-n-a |website=The News International}}</ref>

[[Football in Pakistan]] is as old as the country itself. Shortly after the creation of Pakistan in 1947, the Pakistan Football Federation was created, and the nation's founder [[Muhammad Ali Jinnah]] became its first Patron-in-Chief.<ref>{{cite web |last=Ahsan |first=Ali |date=23 December 2010 |title=A history of football in Pakistan — Part I |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/593095/a-history-of-football-in-pakistan-part-i |access-date=22 August 2022 |website=[[Dawn (newspaper)|Dawn]]}}</ref> The highest football division in the country is the [[Pakistan Premier League]]. Pakistan is known as one of the best manufacturers of the official [[FIFA World Cup]] ball.<ref>{{cite web |last=Waraich |first=Omar |date=11 February 2014 |title=Where soccer gets made |url=http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/soccer/news/20140211/far-post-sialkot-pakistan-soccer-balls-factory/ |url-status=dead |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140212144348/http://sportsillustrated.cnn.com/soccer/news/20140211/far-post-sialkot-pakistan-soccer-balls-factory/ |archive-date=12 February 2014 |access-date=11 January 2021 |work=The Far Post |publisher=Sports Illustrated}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |date=2018-01-28 |title=Footballs made in Pakistan to be used in FIFA World Cup 2018 |url=http://tribune.com.pk/story/1620487/7-footballs-made-pakistan-used-fifa-world-cup-2018 |access-date=2022-08-21 |website=[[The Express Tribune]]}}</ref>

In field hockey, Pakistan is one of the most successful national teams with a record four [[Men's FIH Hockey World Cup|Hockey World Cup]] wins, eight [[Field hockey at the Asian Games|Asian Games]] gold medals, three gold medals at the [[Field hockey at the Summer Olympics|Olympic Games]], and the only Asian team to have won the prestigious [[Hockey Champions Trophy|Champions Trophy]] three times.<ref name="CW" /><ref>{{cite book |author=Bill Mallon |title=Historical Dictionary of the Olympic Movement |author2=Jeroen Heijmans |publisher=Scarecrow |year=2011 |isbn=978-0-8108-7249-3 |edition=4th revised |page=291}}</ref><ref>{{cite book |author=V.V.K.Subburaj |url=https://books.google.com/books?id=Z0mjae1kdYUC&pg=PA771 |title=Basic Facts of General Knowledge |date=30 August 2004 |publisher=Sura College of Competition |isbn=978-81-7254-234-4 |page=771}}</ref>

Pakistan's [[Jahangir Khan]] is widely regarded as the greatest squash player of all time, followed by his compatriot [[Jansher Khan]].<ref name="SP" /><ref name="GP" /><ref name="QS" /><ref name="SM" /> From 1981 to 1986, Jahangir was unbeaten and during that time won 555 consecutive matches – the longest winning streak by any athlete in top-level professional sport, as recorded by ''[[Guinness World Records]]''.<ref name="JK" /><ref name="NT" />

In [[Northern Pakistan]], the annual [[Shandur Polo Festival]] at [[Shandur Top]] is an international event attended by enthusiasts from all over the world.<ref name="PM" /> The Shandur polo ground at [[Shandur Pass]] is the world's highest, at approximately {{convert|3734|m}}.<ref name="tourism" />

The [[Pakistan Sports Board]] was established in 1962 as a corporate body to promote and develop uniform standards of competition in sports in Pakistan, and regulating and controlling sports in Pakistan on a national basis. The Pakistan Sports Board is supported by the Pakistan Sports Trust, which assists hard-up players and associations so they can continue participating in sports in the [[Olympic Games]], [[Asian Games]], [[World Games]], and [[Commonwealth Games]].

== History ==
{{See also|Sport in British India}}[[Muhammad Ali Jinnah]], the founder of Pakistan, had a great love for the sport and fully realized the vital role it played in the development of nations and the inculcation of discipline among the masses. In a meeting with the Organizing Committee of the First Pakistan Olympic Games held at [[Karachi]], Jinnah told the first President of the [[Pakistan Olympic Association]], Ahmed E.H.:

{{blockquote|Dedicate yourself to sports promotion, for when you and I are gone, leadership will go into the hands of Youth, and Youth is our wealth, a raw material, that must be hammered into shape, into burnished steel to strive and smite in defense – the defense of the integrity and solidarity of Pakistan – the defense of the ideology of Pakistan.}}

The first National Games were held at Polo Ground, [[Karachi]] from 23 to 25 April 1948. Sportsmen and officials from [[East Pakistan]] (now [[Bangladesh]]) and all the integrated Provincial units of [[West Pakistan]] took part in these games. The total number of athletes was 140. No competitors were, however, invited from any foreign country. Competitions were held in [[basketball]], [[boxing]], [[cycle sport|cycling]], [[track and field]], [[volleyball]], [[Olympic weightlifting|weightlifting]], and [[wrestling]].

=== Sports structure ===
{{See also|Departmental sports in Pakistan}}
Since independence, Pakistan's sports system has developed along multiple overlapping models rather than a single club-based pyramid. The most distinctive of these has been the departmental model, where state-owned enterprises, government ministries, and the armed forces employ athletes as staff and field teams in domestic competition. This approach, inherited from colonial-era [[works team]]s, became dominant from the 1960s to the 1990s across cricket, football, hockey, and athletics, providing stable employment for players through institutions such as [[Pakistan International Airlines]], [[Water and Power Development Authority|WAPDA]], and the armed services.<ref name=":0">{{Cite web |last=Husain |first=Saeed |date=2025-05-09 |title=The Lost Era Of Departmental Sports |url=https://aurora.dawn.com/news/1145444 |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Aurora Magazine |publisher=[[Dawn Media Group]] |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Hasan |first=Shazia |date=2023-03-05 |title=REVIVING FOOTBALL IN LYARI |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1740465 |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":2">{{Cite web |last=Chaudhry |first=Ijaz |date=2023-11-05 |title=HOCKEY: THE 'CUSTOMS' OF HOCKEY |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1786228 |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref>

Alongside departments, federations have periodically promoted regional and provincial teams, representing administrative units such as Punjab, Sindh or Karachi, particularly in the [[National Games of Pakistan|National Games]] and domestic cricket. Community clubs and academies have remained central at the grassroots, especially in football hotbeds such as [[Lyari]] in Karachi, but have historically lacked resources compared to departmental employers.<ref name=":1" /><ref name=":10">{{Cite web |last=Sohail |first=Shahrukh |date=2020-08-09 |title=FOOTBALL: A LEAGUE FOR PAKISTAN FOOTBALL |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1573329 |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=InpaperMagazine |first=From |date=2013-01-13 |title=In-depth: Pakistan football |url=https://www.dawn.com/2013/01/13/in-depth-pakistan-football/ |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":4">{{Cite web |last=Ahsan |first=Ali |date=2010-12-23 |title=A history of football in Pakistan — Part II |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/593096/a-history-of-football-in-pakistan-part-ii |access-date=2025-02-03 |website=DAWN.COM |language=en}}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |last=Ahsan |first=Ali |date=2010-12-23 |title=A history of football in Pakistan — Part III |url=https://www.dawn.com/2010/12/23/a-history-of-football-in-pakistan-part-iii/ |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref>

Since the 2010s, a fourth model has emerged with franchise-based leagues, most notably the [[Pakistan Super League]] in cricket or [[Super Kabaddi League]], which operate on commercial contracts and media rights.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ali |first=Mir Shabbar |date=2024-10-03 |title=Situationer: Why Pakistan cricket is still 'hobbling at the crease' |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1862755 |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=Dawn |language=en}}</ref> The coexistence of departments, regions, clubs, and franchises has produced a hybrid and sometimes unstable sporting landscape, with policy shifts periodically reshaping the balance among these models.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2025-07-07 |title=Another domestic overhaul: PCB cuts down QeA trophy, culls Champions Cup tournaments |url=https://www.espn.com/cricket/story/_/id/45689146/pcb-cuts-qea-trophy-culls-champions-cup-tournaments |access-date=2025-09-06 |website=ESPN.com |language=en}}</ref>

=== Cricket ===
{{Main|Cricket in Pakistan}}{{see also|Pakistan Cricket Board}}
[[File:Pakvaus.jpg|thumb|A cricket match between Pakistan and Australia at [[Lord's]].]]

[[Cricket]] is the most popular sport in Pakistan. Pakistan has won international cricket events, which include the [[1992 Cricket World Cup]], the [[2009 ICC World Twenty20]] and the [[2017 ICC Champions Trophy]] besides finishing as runner-up in the [[1999 Cricket World Cup]], [[2007 ICC World Twenty20]] and the [[2022 T20 World Cup]]. Pakistan also won the ACC Asia Cup in [[2000 Asia Cup|2000]] and [[2012 Asia Cup|2012]] and all three versions of the [[Austral-Asia Cup]].

Pakistan's cricket teams take part in domestic competitions such as the [[Quaid-e-Azam Trophy]], the [[Patron's Trophy]], ABN-AMRO [[Twenty20|Twenty-20]] Cup, and the ABN-AMRO Champions Trophy. [[Pakistan Cricket Board]] also organize a franchise based [[Twenty20|T20]] cricket league known as the [[Pakistan Super League]].

Pakistan has also been a major host nation, co-hosting the [[1987 Cricket World Cup|1987]] and [[1996 Cricket World Cup]]s and the [[2025 ICC Champions Trophy]], while hosting the [[2008 Asia Cup]] and co-hosting the [[2023 Asia Cup]]. Pakistan won the [[1998–99 Asian Test Championship]], reached No. 1 in ICC Test rankings in 2016 under [[Misbah-ul-Haq]], and holds a record six [[Hong Kong Sixes]] titles. The women’s team won gold medals at the [[Cricket at the 2010 Asian Games|2010]] and [[Cricket at the 2014 Asian Games|2014 Asian Games]], underlining Pakistan’s strength across formats. Pakistan also hosted the [[2025 Women's Cricket World Cup Qualifier|2025 ICC Women’s Cricket World Cup Qualifiers]], further highlighting its growing role in international women’s cricket.

On March 3, 2009, 12 militants with [[guns]], [[grenades]], and [[rocket launchers]] attacked a [[bus]] carrying the [[Sri Lanka national cricket team]] to a match at [[Lahore]]'s [[Gaddafi Stadium]] in Pakistan. Six Pakistani [[policemen]] escorting the team and two [[civilians]] were killed while seven Sri Lankan players and an assistant [[Coach (sport)|coach]] were injured. Since the [[terrorist attacks]], no foreign cricket teams toured Pakistan until 2015 when Zimbabwe toured for an away series. A month after the attack on the Sri Lankan team, the [[International Cricket Council]] relieved Pakistan of co-hosting duty for any [[2011 Cricket World Cup]] games. === Field hockey ===
{{Main|Field hockey in Pakistan}}
{{See also|Pakistan men's national field hockey team}}
[[File:Hockey Nederland tegen Pakistan 3-1, Amsterdam, de Wereldcup die Pakistan onlan…, Bestanddeelnr 925-0964.jpg|thumb|Pakistan with the [[1971 Men's Hockey World Cup]] trophy]]
The [[Pakistan Hockey Federation]] (PHF) is the national governing body of field hockey in Pakistan. The Pakistan Hockey Federation Women Wing (PHFWW) is the official organization of [[women's field hockey]] in Pakistan.

The [[Pakistan men's national field hockey team|Pakistan national field hockey team]] has won 3 gold medals at the [[Olympic Games]], and lifted the [[Hockey World Cup]] 4 times, being the country with most World Cups till the present. It has also won the most [[Asian Games|Asian]] gold medals and is the only Asian team to have won the prestigious [[Hockey Champions Trophy|Champions Trophy]] with 3 titles. It used to be consistently ranked among the top teams in the world. However, lately, there has been a decline in results, with the national team failing to qualify for both the 2016 and 2020 Olympics. The hockey team also failed to qualify for the 2023 World Cup.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2090501/pakistan |title=Four-time champions, Pakistan fail to qualify for Hockey World Cup 2023 |date=26 May 2022}}</ref> Also, because of the PHF's poor economy, there were two years without any international matches between 2019 and 2021. PHF secretary lamented over lack of funding could even ban Pakistan hockey team.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.thenews.com.pk/latest/1012859-phf-secretary-laments-lack-of-funding-could-ban-pakistan-hockey-team |title=PHF secretary laments lack of funding could ban Pakistan hockey team |date=23 November 2022 }}</ref> As of December 2022 the team is ranked 16th in the world.<ref>[https://fih.ch/rankings/outdoor/ FIH Men's and Women's World Ranking]</ref> Notable players include World-Record holder [[Waseem Ahmed (field hockey)|Waseem Ahmed]], [[Mohammed Saqlain]], and [[Sohail Abbas]].

Beyond its Olympic, World Cup, Champions Trophy and Asian Games success, Pakistan has achieved notable titles including three [[Men's Hockey Asia Cup]], multiple [[Sultan Azlan Shah Cup]] victories, and three [[Men's Asian Champions Trophy]] titles. The team has also won gold medals at the [[South Asian Games]], earned podium finishes at the [[Hockey at the Commonwealth Games|Commonwealth Games]] and [[Afro-Asian Games]], and finished runners-up at the [[2024–25 Men's FIH Hockey Nations Cup]]. Pakistan will make its debut in the [[2025–26 Men's FIH Pro League]], signaling a return to elite international competition.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.fih.hockey/events/fih-pro-league/news/pakistan-receives-formal-invitation-to-join-fih-hockey-pro-league-needs-to-confirm-participation-until-8-august|title=Pakistan receives formal invitation to join FIH Hockey Pro League; needs to confirm participation until 12 August|publisher=fih.hockey|date=23 July 2025}}</ref>
The [[National Hockey Championship (Pakistan)|National Hockey Championship]] is the main domestic field hockey competition of Pakistan since 1948.

=== Football ===
{{Main|Football in Pakistan}}{{see also|Pakistan Football Federation}}
[[File:Pakistan lineup during 1997 SAFF Gold Cup.jpg|thumb|[[Pakistan national football team]] at the [[1997 SAFF Gold Cup]]]]
Pakistan is known as the manufacturer of the official [[FIFA World Cup]] ball. The [[Pakistan Football Federation]] (PFF) is the governing body in Pakistan.

The origin of football in Pakistan can be traced back to the mid-nineteenth century when the game was introduced during the [[British Raj]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Wasim |first=Umaid |date=2019-12-18 |title=A football tournament in KP offers hope for the future |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1522836 |access-date=2024-02-03 |website=DAWN.COM |language=en}}</ref> Shortly after the creation of Pakistan in 1947, the [[Pakistan Football Federation]] was created, and [[Muhammad Ali Jinnah]] became its first Patron-in-Chief.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ahsan |first=Ali |date=2010-12-23 |title=A history of football in Pakistan — Part I |url=https://www.dawn.com/2010/12/23/a-history-of-football-in-pakistan-part-i/ |access-date=2024-08-11 |website=DAWN.COM |language=en}}</ref>

Pakistan’s football team has won two gold medals at the South Asian Games, in [[Football at the 1989 South Asian Games|1989]] and [[Football at the 1991 South Asian Games|1991]], and secured a bronze medal in [[Football at the 1987 South Asian Games|1987]]. In regional competition, Pakistan achieved third place at the [[1997 SAFF Gold Cup|1997 SAFF Championship]] (then known as the SAFF Gold Cup). [[Jinnah Sports Stadium|Jinnah Stadium]] in Islamabad has long served as the primary home ground for the Pakistan national football team, and the country hosted the [[2014 SAFF Women's Championship]] at the venue. Pakistan also hosted the [[1993 SAARC Gold Cup]] and the [[2005 SAFF Gold Cup]].

From 1948 to 2003, the [[National Football Championship (Pakistan)|National Football Championship]] was held in Pakistan. After 2003, it was replaced by the [[Pakistan Premier League]]. However, due to the lack of professional football clubs, football was mainly held on amateur basis. As the global popularity of football surged, the sport’s standing in Pakistan deteriorated. Some of the reasons for this decline was the administration’s lack of attention to football and the [[Works team|departmental system]] running in Pakistan, which was abolished in other countries after the 1960s.<ref name="RP">{{Cite web |date=2024-06-26 |title=Rising Popularity of Football in Pakistan Reflects Growing Interest in the Sport |url=https://dailytimes.com.pk/1204028/rising-popularity-of-football-in-pakistan-reflects-growing-interest-in-the-sport/ |access-date=2024-08-11 |website=Daily Times |language=en-US}}</ref> However Lahore based [[Wohaib FC]] emerged as a leading club of the country in the early 1990s and became the first Pakistani club to pass the qualifying round of the [[AFC Champions League Elite|Asian Club Championship]], doing so in the [[1992–93 Asian Club Championship|1992–93]] edition.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Ahsan |first=Ali |date=2010-12-23 |title=A history of football in Pakistan — Part III |url=https://www.dawn.com/2010/12/23/a-history-of-football-in-pakistan-part-iii/ |access-date=2023-12-02 |website=DAWN.COM |language=en}}</ref> The Pakistan Football Federation also faced several bans by [[FIFA]] during the years.<ref name="RP" /> Nonetheless, the sport gained popularity in the 21st century.<ref name="VD" /><ref name="FP" /><ref name="RP" /> The [[Pakistan National Football Challenge Cup]] is the national [[single-elimination tournament|"knockout" cup competition]] in Pakistani football, run by the Pakistan Football Federation. The [[National Women's Football Championship (Pakistan)|Pakistan National Women's Football Championship]] is the annual women's championship.

=== Badminton ===
The [[Pakistan national badminton team]] represents Pakistan in international [[badminton]] team competitions.<ref>{{cite web |date=15 May 2004 |title=Sudirman date is huge filip for Smillie |url=https://www.scotsman.com/sport/sudirman-date-is-huge-filip-for-smillie-1-1011986/amp |access-date=6 August 2018 |work=[[The Scotsman]]}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Populorum |first1=Mike |title=Archiv SudirmanCup |url=https://www.sbg.ac.at/populorum/badminton/archiv_sudirmancup.htm |access-date=8 May 2019 |website=sbg.ac.at}}</ref> It is controlled by the [[Pakistan Badminton Federation]], the governing body for badminton in [[Pakistan]]. The national team was established in 1953.

Pakistan has a notable early history in Asian badminton, having won bronze medals in the [[1962 Asian Badminton Championships – Men's team|1962 Asian Championships]] and the [[Badminton at the 1978 Asian Games – Men's team|1978 Asian Games]], marking some of the country’s most significant team achievements on the continental stage.

In [[1979 WBF World Championships|1979]], Pakistan competed in the World Championships organized by the [[World Badminton Federation]] (WBF), a rival organization to the [[International Badminton Federation]] (now the [[Badminton World Federation]], BWF). At this event, Pakistan secured a silver medal in men’s singles and a bronze medal in the men’s team competition. The WBF later ceased operations in 1981 and merged with the IBF.
Pakistan made limited appearances in major mixed-team global competitions, participating in the [[Sudirman Cup]] in 1993, 1995, and 1997, though the team was eliminated at the group stage in all three editions. After a long absence from the international spotlight, Pakistan returned to elite competition by participating in the [[Badminton at the 2018 Commonwealth Games – Mixed team|2018]] and the [[Badminton at the 2022 Commonwealth Games – Mixed team|2022 Commonwealth Games]] event through a bipartite invitation.

Pakistan made its Olympic badminton debut at the [[Badminton at the Summer Olympics|2020 Tokyo Summer Olympics]], when [[Mahoor Shahzad]] represented the nation in the women’s singles discipline after receiving a [[Tripartite Commission]] invitation. This marked Pakistan’s first-ever Olympic qualification in badminton, with Mahoor Shahzad becoming the first Pakistani badminton player to compete at the Olympic Games.<ref>{{Cite web |date=2021-07-24 |title=Pakistan's Mahoor Shahzad loses badminton opener in Tokyo |url=http://tribune.com.pk/story/2311873/pakistans-mahoor-shahzad-loses-badminton-opener-at-tokyo-olympics |access-date=2022-07-30 |website=The Express Tribune |language=en}}</ref>

=== Tennis ===
{{Main|Tennis in Pakistan}}
[[File:Aisam qureshi.jpg|thumb|[[Aisam-ul-Haq Qureshi]] at the US Open.]]

[[Tennis]] is a very popular sport and Pakistanis compete in various international events. The [[Pakistan Tennis Federation]] (PTF) organizes the game in the country. [[Khwaja Saeed Hai]] was the first Pakistani to reach a [[Grand Slam (tennis)|Grand Slam]] tournament, playing at [[The Championships, Wimbledon|Wimbledon]], the [[US Open (tennis)|US Open]], and the [[French Open]] where he made it to the third round. [[Haroon Rahim]] was a very successful player, he was the winner of several [[Association of Tennis Professionals|ATP]] Singles and Doubles titles. Lately, [[Aisam-ul-Haq Qureshi]] created history in Pakistani tennis, as he reached the finals of [[2010 US Open – Men's doubles]] and [[2010 US Open – Mixed doubles]].

The [[Pakistan Davis Cup team]] have previously reached the world group play-offs, with the help of Aisam-ul-Haq Qureshi, currently Pakistan's number one, and [[Aqeel Khan]], the number two. Tennis events are very rare in Pakistan and there is hardly an international tennis event, apart from ATP tournaments.

=== Skiing ===

==Motorsport==

==Mountaineering==

With the greatest concentration of the highest peaks of the world many of them very challenging climbs, Pakistan is a prime location for skilled mountain climbers. Five peaks are over 8,000 meters. The Alpine Club of Pakistan (ACP) founded in 1974, is the national mountaineering and climbing federation. [[Chiltan Adventurers Association Balochistan]] is the major affiliated unit of ACP founded in 1984. [[Nazir Sabir]], [[Ashraf Aman]], [[Hayatullah Khan Durrani]] [[File:Group of Chiltan Adventurers with Lino Lacedelli Italian Mountaineer 2004.jpg|thumb|right|Group of Chiltan Adventurers with [[Hayatullah Khan Durrani]] [[Lino Lacedelli]] and [[Gianni Alemanno]] at [[Concordia (Pakistan)|Concordia]] base camp 2004]]Lt.Col Abdul Jabbar Bhatti, Col Sher Khan, and [[Meherban Karim]] are Pakistan's most experienced mountaineers.

[[Pakistan Alpine Institute]] is the pioneer of "big wall climbing" in Pakistan.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://tribune.com.pk/story/620088/making-their-mark-capitals-climbers-scale-trango-braak-in-g-b/|title=Capital's climbers scale Trango Braak in G-B - The Express Tribune|date=21 October 2013|website=tribune.com.pk|access-date=4 April 2018}}</ref> Pakistan's First Big Wall Climbing Expedition has been completed by Imran Junaidi and Usman Tariq in September 2013.<ref>{{cite web | url=http://www.pakistanalpine.com/articles/expeditions/pakistans-first-big-wall-climbing-expedition/ | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20131023011104/http://www.pakistanalpine.com/articles/expeditions/pakistans-first-big-wall-climbing-expedition/ | archive-date=23 October 2013 | title=Pakistan's First Big Wall Climbing Expedition &#124; Pakistan Alpine Institute |date=10 May 2012}}</ref>

[[Samina Baig]] is the first [[Pakistani people|Pakistani]] woman and the third Pakistani to climb [[Mount Everest]]. She is also the youngest Muslim woman to climb Everest, having done so at the age of 21. Samina is also the first Pakistani woman and the first Muslim to climb the [[seven summits]].<ref>{{cite news |title=samina. baig |url=http://www.bbc.co.uk/urdu/pakistan/2013/05/130519_pak_mount_everest_rh.shtml |newspaper=BBC Urdu}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=http://tribune.com.pk/story/551508/samina-baig-first-pakistani-woman-to-scale-mount-everest/ |title=Samina Baig: First Pakistani woman to scale Mount Everest |work=The Express Tribune |date=19 May 2013 |access-date=20 May 2013}}</ref><ref>{{cite news |url=http://www.thehindu.com/news/international/south-asia/first-pakistani-woman-to-scale-everest/article4730455.ece |title=First Pakistani woman to scale Everest |work=[[The Hindu]] | date=19 May 2013 |access-date=20 May 2013}}</ref>

Up until recently, [[Swimming (sport)|swimming]] struggled as a sport in Pakistan. But there has been a surge in interest, particularly with [[Rubab Raza]]. She was the youngest Olympic competitor in Pakistan's history, aged 13 at the [[2004 Summer Olympics]]. [[Kiran Khan (swimmer)|Kiran Khan]] known as "Golden Girl" also came to attention when she won 7 gold medals, 3 silver medals, and 3 bronze medals at [[National Games of Pakistan|28 National Games]] in 2001. She represented Pakistan at the [[2008 Summer Olympics]]. Other notable swimmers include [[Iftikhar Ahmed Shah]], [[Muhammad Ramzan (swimmer)|Muhammad Ramzan]], [[Muhammad Bashir (swimmer)|Muhammad Bashir]], [[Ahmed Nazir]], [[Bisma Khan]], [[Syed Muhammad Haseeb Tariq]], [[Lianna Swan]], [[Jehanara Nabi]], [[Anum Bandey]], [[Mumtaz Ahmed]], [[Adil Baig]], [[Israr Hussain]], [[Kamal Masud]] and [[Haris Bandey]].<ref>{{cite web | url=https://hopkinssports.com/sports/mens-swimming-and-diving/roster/kamal-masud/13018 | title=Kamal Masud - Men's Swimming |website=hopkinssports.com}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.thenews.com.pk/print/910641-jehanara-sets-three-swimming-records-as-wapda-win |title=Jehanara sets three swimming records as WAPDA win |work=The News |date=22 November 2021 |access-date=18 December 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://nation.com.pk/20-Nov-2021/seven-national-records-made-in-national-swimming |title=Seven national records made in National Swimming |work=The Nation |date=20 November 2021 |access-date=18 December 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.geo.tv/latest/141816-Female-swimmers-win-bronze-medal-for-Pakistan-in-Islamic-solidarity-games |title=Female swimmers win bronze medal for Pakistan in Islamic solidarity games |work=Geo TV |date=15 May 2017 |access-date=18 December 2021}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |url=https://www.dawn.com/news/1659074/army-take-charge-at-women-open-national-swimming |title=Army take charge at Women Open National Swimming |work=Dawn |date=20 November 2021 |access-date=18 December 2021}}</ref>
